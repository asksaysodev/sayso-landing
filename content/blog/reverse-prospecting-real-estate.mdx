Reverse prospecting in real estate is an MLS feature that compares one of your active listings to the saved searches other agents have set up for their buyers, then shows you a list of those buyer's agents. Instead of searching for a buyer, you let the system point to agents whose clients are already looking for a home like yours, and you contact those agents about your listing. The buyers themselves stay hidden, because the results show agent contact information rather than the buyer's name.

This guide explains what the feature shows and hides, how listing agents use it, what to say in the email or call that follows, and how it fits with other kinds of prospecting. The exact buttons and menu names differ from one MLS to the next, so treat the steps here as a pattern and confirm the details in your own MLS's help pages.

## What Reverse Prospecting Means in the MLS

Most prospecting starts with a person or a list: you decide who to reach and then reach out. Reverse prospecting starts with a property. You select a listing, the MLS searches the saved buyer searches that agents have attached to their contacts, and it returns the agents whose searches match the main details of your listing.

Several MLS help pages describe it the same way. METRO MLS says reverse prospecting "allows a listing agent to market its listings to buyers' agents" and connects "the right property with the right buyer." The Canopy MLS help page calls it a way to find and contact agents who have buyers searching for listings like yours. A Berkshire REALTORS blog post describes it as a database function that matches potential buyers with your listings.

The prospect in this case is the buyer's agent, who is a potential partner on the transaction, not the buyer. That is the main difference from other [prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/), where the person you contact is usually a homeowner or a lead who might hire you directly.

## What You Can and Cannot See

The details vary by system, but the published help pages agree on the broad picture.

| You can usually see | You usually cannot see |
|---|---|
| The names and contact information of buyer's agents with a matching saved search | The buyer's name or personal details |
| A reference number for the contact | Which of the agent's clients is the match |
| In some systems, a history of whether the contact was emailed and how many listings were sent | Anything about the buyer beyond what the agent shares with you |

For a match to appear, the saved search has to meet a few conditions. The Flexmls help pages from ARMLS say the main fields of your listing must match the saved search, the search must have a contact attached to it, and that contact must have reverse prospecting enabled on its details screen. METRO MLS says both parties must opt in to the feature in Flexmls. RWorld's guide for Matrix users adds that the search must have been created or updated within the past year, and that the setting defaults to "Yes" unless the buyer's agent unchecks it.

Those conditions mean the list is a starting point, not a complete picture of the market. An agent who has not saved a search for a client, or who has switched the feature off, will not appear. Because these pages are written for specific systems and some are several years old (the ARMLS article dates from 2018 and the METRO MLS article from 2022), check your MLS's current help documentation before you rely on any detail.

## How Agents Use Reverse Prospecting Step by Step

The exact path depends on your system. In Matrix, the Canopy MLS and RWorld guides point to My Matrix or My Listings, then My Active Listings, then a Reverse Prospecting button. In Flexmls, the guides point to a Reverse Prospecting option under the Contacts menu, where you check the listings you want searched and start the search. The pattern is the same in both.

1. **Pick the listing.** Reverse prospecting works from active listings, so start with one you hold.
2. **Start the search.** Choose the reverse prospecting option in your MLS and select the listing or listings.
3. **Review the results.** Look at the agents returned and any history columns the system shows. In Matrix, for example, the Canopy help page describes columns for whether a contact was emailed before and how many listings were sent to that contact.
4. **Contact the agents.** Canopy's help page notes that you click an agent's name to send an individual email and that mass emailing is not available there. Other systems may differ, so follow your MLS's rules on how you may contact members.
5. **Record what happens.** Note who replied, who asked for details and who scheduled a showing, so you do not repeat the same message and can follow up.

A few moments are good times to use the feature: when a listing first goes live, after an open house or showing activity slows, and after a price or other main detail changes, since the list of matches can change when the property's main fields do. Which of those moments works best is a judgment call, since the help pages reviewed here do not measure it.

## What to Say to a Buyer's Agent

Agents are busy and receive many listing emails. A message that respects their time and fits what their client wants stands out from a mass blast. The best opening is one sentence about the property and one question about their client's criteria, because the system has told you only that a search matches, not what the buyer cares about.

<ScriptExample label="Email or message to a buyer's agent">
Subject: A home that may fit your buyer's search in [Area]

Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I just listed [Address], a [beds/baths] home in [Neighborhood] at [Price], and it came up when I checked the MLS for agents with matching saved searches. If it fits what your client has been looking at, I'd be glad to send details or set up a showing. Is this close to what they want, or are there details I should know before I follow up?
</ScriptExample>

The same idea works on a call, which is an option when you want an answer sooner.

<ScriptExample label="Call to a buyer's agent">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling because the MLS showed you have a client with a saved search that matches my new listing at [Address]. It's a [beds/baths] home in [Neighborhood] at [Price]. Do you have a minute for me to tell you about it, or would it be better if I sent the details by email?
</ScriptExample>

If you reach voicemail, keep it short and give them a reason to call back.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail for a buyer's agent">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I have a new listing at [Address] in [Neighborhood] that matches a search you have saved in the MLS, and I'd like to send you the details. You can reach me at [Phone], and I'll also send you an email with the information.
</ScriptExample>

### What they say next

- **"My client is looking at other homes right now."** Respond with: "That makes sense. Would it be helpful if I send the details anyway, so you have it if they widen their search? And if you tell me what they are missing in the homes they have seen, I can tell you honestly whether mine fits."
- **"What is the price flexibility?"** Respond with: "I can share what I know about the seller's situation and timeline once I understand what your client is looking for. What is their price range?" This keeps the conversation moving without sharing anything your seller has asked you to keep private.
- **"Can you send me the information?"** Respond with: "Absolutely. I'll send it today. What is the best email, and is there anything specific you would like included, such as the property disclosures or a showing window?"
- **"I already sent that to my client."** Respond with: "Thanks for checking. If they liked it, I'm glad to set up a private showing or answer any questions they have. If they passed, do you mind telling me what stood out so I can be more useful on the next one?"
- **"I'm not interested."** Respond with: "No problem at all. Would you like me to keep you in mind when I list something new in [Area]?" Then add them to your follow-up list so you can contact them when you have a better fit.

Check what your seller has agreed to share before you answer questions about their motivation or flexibility. Your listing agreement and your brokerage's rules guide what you can say.

The MLS tells you which agents to contact, but the conversation that follows still depends on how you respond when someone says "my client is not interested" or asks a question you did not expect. ## What Is a Prospect, and Where Does Reverse Prospecting Fit?

A prospect in real estate is a person who might become your client but has not hired you yet. Prospecting is the effort of finding and contacting prospects. The National Association of REALTORS describes it as [seeking out potential clients](https://www.nar.realtor/farming-prospecting) through methods such as calling, networking, online marketing and social media.

Reverse prospecting is a narrower tool inside that larger set. It helps you reach other agents rather than homeowners, and it only works when you hold a listing. For the homeowner-facing methods, such as farming, circle prospecting and expired listings, see the [overview of real estate prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/). ## Common Mistakes With Reverse Prospecting

- **Sending the same message to everyone.** The system shows that a search matches, not what the buyer wants. A message that asks about their criteria gets better answers than a brochure.
- **Trying to get the buyer's information.** These systems keep the buyer anonymous by design, and your MLS may have rules about how you may contact buyers or their agents.
- **Using it once.** A listing's matches can change as agents add searches, so a single check at launch may miss agents who set up a search later.
- **Skipping the follow-up.** One message is easy to overlook. A second message with a new angle, such as a price change or a showing window, gives them another reason to respond.
- **Sending too many messages.** Contacting the same agent about every listing, regardless of fit, trains them to ignore you.

## FAQ

**What does reverse prospecting mean in the MLS?**
It means searching other agents' saved buyer searches for matches to your listing, so you can contact the buyer's agents whose clients may be interested. The buyer's name stays hidden.

**What are the different types of prospecting?**
Common types include contacting your sphere and past clients, farming a neighborhood, circle prospecting, calling lists such as expired listings and FSBOs, online outreach and reverse prospecting. The [prospecting strategies guide](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) compares them.

**Do I need to be a listing agent to use reverse prospecting?**
The tool works from your own active listings, so you need at least one listing. Buyer's agents can also manage whether their contacts appear in other agents' reverse prospecting results, according to the Matrix guide from RWorld.

**Is reverse prospecting the same in every MLS?**
No. Names, menus, available columns and contact rules differ between systems such as Flexmls and Matrix, so read your own MLS's help pages before you start.
