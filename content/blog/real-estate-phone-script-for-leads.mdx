A good real estate phone script for leads matches how the lead found you. Internet, open house, referral, sign call and past client leads each raised their hand in a different way, so each needs its own opener, a qualifying bridge that fits what you already know, and an appointment ask with two specific times.

Below are all three parts for each of the five sources, written so they work whether the lead is buying, selling or both. These are calls to people who contacted you or were introduced to you. If you are calling expired listings, FSBOs or a neighborhood list, start with the [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) instead, and for the full set of qualifying questions to work through once a lead is talking, see our list of [questions to ask real estate leads](/blog/questions-to-ask-real-estate-leads/).

## How Should a Phone Script Change by Lead Source?

The source tells you what the lead already did and how much they already trust you. That decides what your first sentence leads with and how big a step you can ask for.

| Lead source | What the lead already did | Your opener leads with | The appointment you ask for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internet or portal lead | Filled out a form or saved a listing, often on several sites | The listing or area they looked at, while it's fresh | A 15-minute call or a showing |
| Open house visitor | Walked through a home and talked with you in person | A detail from that conversation | A showing or a sit-down consultation |
| Referral | Got your name from someone they trust | The referrer's name | A 15-minute meeting |
| Sign call | Called the number on your sign | A question about what caught their eye | A showing, or a home value visit for a neighbor |
| Past client | Bought or sold with you before | How the home and family are doing | A home value review or a search consultation |

The bridge in every script below does the same job: it uses one or two questions to find out what the lead wants and when, then connects that answer to the appointment. The [appointment setting script](/blog/appointment-setting-script/) guide covers the ask itself in more depth.

## Script 1: What Do You Say to an Internet Lead?

Internet and portal leads are browsing. They don't know you yet, and they are probably getting calls from other agents about the same form. Speed matters more here than with any other source, because the first agent who has a conversation with them has the advantage. Call within minutes of the lead coming in, and if you are with a client, send a text first: "Hi [Name], saw you're interested in [Area]. I'm with a client right now but I'd love to chat. Free in 30 minutes?"

<ScriptExample label="Internet Lead: Opener">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were looking at homes in [Area] on [Source] a few minutes ago, and I wanted to connect while it's fresh. Are you actively searching, or starting to explore what's out there?
</ScriptExample>

That last question sorts the call. "Actively searching" and "starting to explore" lead to different bridges.

<ScriptExample label="Internet Lead: Qualifying Bridge">
Active: "Good to hear. What's got you looking right now, and when are you hoping to be settled?"
Exploring: "No rush at all. What would need to happen for a move to make sense for you?"
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Internet Lead: Appointment Ask">
Active: "Based on what you've told me, I think I can help. Would it be worth 15 minutes to go over what's available, including a few homes that haven't hit the portals yet? I have Thursday at 2 or Friday at 10."
Exploring: "Would it help if I set you up on a custom search so you see new listings before they hit Zillow? I can filter it to exactly what you described, and we can talk again once you've seen a few."
</ScriptExample>

When the answer is "can you send me listings?", agree and ask for what you need to send the right ones: "Happy to. So I send you the right ones, can I ask a couple quick questions about what you're looking for?" Our guide to [handling "just send me listings"](/objections/just-send-listings/) has the full reply. A lead who submits an inquiry can generally be called for three months under the FTC's Do Not Call rules, but ask your broker how your office treats portal leads, and read our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/).

### Speed-to-Lead Checklist

1. **Set up instant lead notifications.** Route every new lead to your phone as a push notification or text, not only an email you check later.
2. **Call within minutes.** Dial as soon as the notification lands, using the opener above.
3. **Send a text if you can't call.** Use the template above so the lead hears from you first, then call as soon as you're free.
4. **Log the call in your CRM.** Write down what they told you, their timeline and the next step before you dial anyone else.
5. **Schedule the follow-up.** Put the next call or text on your calendar before you hang up, whether they booked an appointment or not.

## Script 2: How Do You Follow Up With an Open House Lead?

Open house visitors showed physical interest, which is a stronger signal than a web form, and they have already met you. Treat the follow-up as the second half of a conversation you started at the door, not a fresh introduction. Call the next day. For what to say during the open house itself, see our [open house scripts](/blog/open-house-scripts/).

<ScriptExample label="Open House Lead: Opener">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name]. You came through the open house on [Street] yesterday, and you mentioned [specific detail from your conversation]. Has anything changed since then, or are you still looking for [what they described]?
</ScriptExample>

The specific detail is what makes this work. "You mentioned the backyard was important for your kids" proves you listened, where "following up on the open house" sounds like every other agent. If you didn't capture a detail, use the property itself: "What did you think of the layout? Was that the kind of space you're looking for, or are you after something different?"

<ScriptExample label="Open House Lead: Qualifying Bridge">
"Are you looking to buy your next place first, or would you need to sell a home before you move?"
"And what timing are you working with?"
</ScriptExample>

The first question matters because many open house visitors own a home nearby. If they need to sell first, you have a seller conversation as well as a buyer one.

<ScriptExample label="Open House Lead: Appointment Ask">
Buying: "There are two homes near [Street] I think fit what you described better than this one. I can show you both Saturday at 11 or Sunday at 2. Which is easier?"
Selling first: "Then the first step is knowing what your home would sell for. I can come by Tuesday evening or Wednesday afternoon and walk you through it in 20 minutes."
</ScriptExample>

## Script 3: How Do You Call a Referral Lead?

Referral leads usually start warmer than any other source, because someone they trust already vouched for you. The worst move is sounding like a stranger reading from a list. Use the referrer's name in your first sentence, then ask an open question and let the lead talk.

<ScriptExample label="Referral Lead: Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. [Referrer's Name] mentioned you might be thinking about making a move and suggested I reach out. How's that going so far? Have you started looking, or are you still figuring out next steps?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Referral Lead: Qualifying Bridge">
"What has you thinking about a move, and is there a timing you're working toward?"
"Did [Referrer's Name] tell you much about how we worked together?"
</ScriptExample>

The second question lets the lead tell you what they already heard about you, which shows you what they expect from an agent.

<ScriptExample label="Referral Lead: Appointment Ask">
Early: "Would it help if I sent you an overview of what homes in [Area] are going for right now? No pressure, it gives you a baseline. I'll call Thursday to see what questions it raised."
Further along: "[Referrer's Name] told me you're looking at [Area] by [Timeline]. Want to sit down for 15 minutes so I can show you what's available? I have Wednesday at 5 or Saturday at 10."
</ScriptExample>

Thank the referrer the same day, whether or not the lead booked. If you want more of these calls, our [real estate referral scripts](/blog/real-estate-referral-scripts/) cover how to ask past clients and your sphere for introductions.

## Script 4: What Do You Say When Someone Calls About Your Sign?

A sign call is inbound: the caller saw your sign and dialed you, so the first ten seconds set the tone. Answer with your name and brokerage, then ask an open question so the caller does the talking. Sign callers are often buyers asking about that home, but some are neighbors wondering what their own home is worth, so find out which before you go further.

<ScriptExample label="Sign Call: Opener">
Thanks for calling, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Happy to help with [Street]. What caught your eye about it?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Sign Call: Qualifying Bridge">
Buyer: "Are you looking in that neighborhood in particular, or is it the style of home that caught your eye?"
Neighbor: "Are you curious what it means for your own home, or thinking about selling at some point?"
Not sure: "That's a fine place to start. What made you reach out today?"
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Sign Call: Appointment Ask">
Buyer: "The best way to know if it fits is to walk through it. I can show it to you tomorrow at 5 or Saturday at 10. Which works better?"
Neighbor: "A listing on your street is a good moment to find out. I can stop by Thursday afternoon or Saturday morning and show you what yours would likely sell for, about 20 minutes."
</ScriptExample>

Write down the caller's name, number and answers in your CRM before you hang up. A buyer who doesn't like this home still wants a home, so offer to send two or three others that fit before the showing.

## Script 5: How Do You Call a Past Client About Their Next Move?

Past clients already trust you, which makes this the easiest call on the list and the one agents put off most. Open with how they are doing in the home, not with business. Life changes like a new job, a growing family or kids leaving home are what start the next move, and a past client will often tell you about them if you ask.

<ScriptExample label="Past Client: Opener">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. It's been about [time] since you moved into [Street], and you came to mind this week. How's the house treating you?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Past Client: Qualifying Bridge">
"Is it still the right fit, or has anything changed with work or family that has you thinking about what's next?"
"Do you know anyone else who's been talking about a move?"
</ScriptExample>

The second question turns a check-in into a referral conversation, which is often where past client calls pay off.

<ScriptExample label="Past Client: Appointment Ask">
Thinking about selling: "Would it help to know what the house would sell for today? I can put it together and walk you through it Tuesday evening or Thursday afternoon, about 15 minutes."
Thinking about buying: "Let's sit down and look at what's out there before you decide anything. I have Wednesday at 6 or Saturday at 10."
Happy where they are: "Glad to hear it. I'll send you a quick update on what homes near you are selling for every few months so you always know where you stand."
</ScriptExample>

The last ask keeps you in touch with a past client who isn't moving, which is how you stay the agent they call when they are.

## What If the Lead Isn't Ready to Book?

Most first calls end without an appointment, whatever the source. When a lead says ["I'm not interested"](/objections/not-interested/), validate and redirect: "Totally fair. Can I ask, was there something specific that caught your eye when you reached out?" One question can reopen a conversation that felt closed. If they still aren't ready, set the next call or text before you hang up and use our [real estate follow-up scripts](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-scripts/) to keep it going. Teams with an [ISA](/glossary/isa-real-estate/) can hand the follow-up to the [ISA scripts](/blog/real-estate-isa-scripts/), which use the same openers by source.

## FAQ

**What is the best phone script for calling real estate leads?**
The best script matches the lead source. Internet leads need a fast, low-pressure opener. Referral leads need warmth and the referrer's name up front. Open house leads need a specific callback to your in-person conversation, sign calls need a question about what caught the caller's eye, and past clients need a check-in before any business.

**How fast should you call back a new real estate lead?**
As fast as you can, ideally within minutes. The first agent to have a meaningful conversation has the advantage, and the longer a lead waits the more likely they have spoken to someone else. If you can't call right away, send a short text to establish contact, then follow up as soon as you're free.

**What do you say when a lead says "just send me listings"?**
It usually means you haven't demonstrated enough value for the in-person meeting. Respond with: "Happy to. So I send you the right ones, can I ask a couple quick questions about what you're looking for?" Then work through your qualifying questions. Follow up in 3-5 days with specific listings and a softer meeting ask.
