These are phone scripts for leads already in your CRM: internet and portal leads, open house and sign call follow-ups, referral and sphere leads, and people who call you. Each one already raised their hand, so the call continues a conversation instead of starting one. Below you'll find openers by lead source, a four-question framework that moves the call to an appointment, a full example call with an internet lead, and a speed-to-lead checklist.

Every agent has leads sitting in their CRM right now that could become appointments this week. The gap isn't the leads. It's knowing what to say when someone picks up. These scripts follow the Open-Bridge-Close framework, adapted for leads who already know you exist. If you're calling cold lists like expireds, FSBOs or a neighborhood, start with our [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) instead.

## Why the First 5 Minutes Matter More Than the Perfect Script

Here's the idea that should reshape how you structure your day: a lead who just filled out a form is thinking about a move right now, and they are often filling out forms on other sites at the same time. The first agent to have a real conversation has the advantage, and every hour you wait gives another agent that chance.

This means the best real estate phone script for leads is the one you actually use in the first few minutes. A decent script delivered fast beats a perfect script delivered late.

The mistake most agents make: a new lead comes in, they think "I'll call after this showing," and by the time they dial, the prospect has already had a full conversation with someone else. The lead isn't cold. It's claimed.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Set up instant notifications for new leads. When one comes in, drop everything and dial as soon as you can, ideally within minutes. Your opening line matters far less than your speed. A simple "Hey [Name], you were just looking at homes in [Area], tell me what caught your eye" beats a rehearsed pitch delivered 45 minutes late.
</CalloutBox>

If you're on another call or in a showing, have a text template ready: "Hi [Name], saw you're interested in [Area]. I'm with a client right now but I'd love to chat. Free in 30 minutes?" That text establishes first contact while you finish what you're doing.

### Speed-to-Lead Checklist

1. **Set up instant lead notifications.** Route every new lead to your phone as a push notification or text, not just an email you check later.
2. **Call within minutes.** Dial as soon as the notification lands. Use the opener for the lead's source below.
3. **Send a text if you can't call.** Use the template above so the lead hears from you first, then call as soon as you're free.
4. **Log the call in your CRM.** Write down what they told you, their timeline and the next step before you dial anyone else.
5. **Schedule the follow-up.** Put the next call or text on your calendar before you hang up, whether they booked an appointment or not.

## Phone Scripts by Lead Source (With Openers and Bridges)

A Zillow lead who clicked on a listing needs a completely different approach than someone your past client referred to you. Match the script to the source.

### Internet and Portal Leads

These leads are browsing. They filled out a form, maybe saved a listing, and are probably doing the same on three other sites. They don't know you. Your job is to stand out from the other agents calling the same lead.

<ScriptExample label="Internet Lead: Speed-to-Lead Opener">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were just looking at homes in [Area] on [Source], and I wanted to connect while it's fresh. Are you actively searching, or just starting to explore what's out there?
</ScriptExample>

That closing question does the heavy lifting. "Actively searching" vs. "just exploring" tells you exactly how to handle the rest of the call.

**Active prospects** get the appointment bridge: "I have access to some properties that haven't hit the portals yet. Would it be worth 15 minutes to go over what's available? I have Thursday at 2 or Friday at 10."

**Explorers** get a softer next step: "No rush at all. Would it help if I set you up on a custom search so you see new listings before they hit Zillow? I can filter it to exactly what you described."

Either way, you've earned a next step. A lead who submits an inquiry can generally be called for three months under the FTC's Do Not Call rules, but ask your broker how your office treats portal leads, and read our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/). If you're not sure [how to start a real estate call](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/) with confidence on these, focus on speed and curiosity. Those two things carry you further than polish.

### Open House and Sign Call Follow-Ups

These prospects showed physical interest. That's a stronger signal than a web form. But most agents treat the follow-up like a cold call instead of continuing a conversation they already started. That's the mistake.

<ScriptExample label="Open House Follow-Up: Next Day">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name]. You came through the open house on [Street] yesterday, and you mentioned [specific detail from your conversation]. Has anything changed since then, or are you still looking for [what they described]?
</ScriptExample>

The specific detail is everything. "You mentioned the backyard was important for your kids" works far better than "just following up." It proves you listened. Most agents don't.

If you didn't capture a detail during the open house, use the property itself: "What did you think of the layout? Was that the kind of space you're looking for, or are you after something different?"

### Referral and Sphere Leads

Referral leads usually start warmer than any other source. Someone they trust already vouched for you. The worst move is sounding like a stranger reading from a list.

<ScriptExample label="Referral Lead: Warm Introduction">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. [Referrer's Name] mentioned you might be thinking about making a move and suggested I reach out. How's that going so far? Have you started looking, or are you still figuring out next steps?
</ScriptExample>

Use the referrer's name early. It's your credibility. Then ask an open-ended question and let the lead drive. Do not pitch.

If they're early: "Would it help if I sent you an overview of what homes in [Area] are going for right now? No pressure, just a baseline."

If they're further along: "[Referrer] told me you're looking at [Area] by [Timeline]. I've helped several families in that neighborhood this year. Want to sit down for 15 minutes so I can show you what's available?"

## How to Answer the Phone as an Agent or Agency

Not every call is one you place. When a buyer calls about a listing or a seller calls to ask what their home is worth, the first ten seconds set the tone. Answer with your name and brokerage, then ask an open question so the caller does the talking.

<ScriptExample label="Inbound Call: Answering the Phone">
Thank you for calling [Brokerage], this is [Your Name]. How can I help you today?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they ask about a specific listing:** say "Happy to help with that one. Are you looking in that neighborhood in particular, or is it the style of home that caught your eye?"
- **If they want to know what their home is worth:** say "I can help with that. What's prompting the question, are you thinking about selling or just curious where you stand?"
- **If they are not sure what they need:** say "That's a fine place to start. What made you reach out today?"

Each branch ends with a question about their plans, which is the same motivation question the framework below uses. Write down the caller's name, number and answer in your CRM before you hang up, and set the follow-up before the next call starts.

## The Qualification Bridge: Four Questions That Replace Awkward Small Talk

Most agents stall mid-call because they don't know how to transition from rapport to "let's meet." The conversation drifts, the lead gets uncomfortable, and the call ends without a next step. With warm leads, you have permission to go deeper, faster.

Here's a four-question framework that naturally bridges any lead call from "hello" to an appointment:

1. **Motivation.** "What's driving the move?" This tells you the why and the urgency level.
2. **Timeline.** "When are you hoping to be settled?" This tells you how fast to move.
3. **Research stage.** "Have you talked to other agents or seen any places you liked?" This reveals where you stand competitively.
4. **Commitment.** "Based on what you've told me, I think I can help. Would it make sense to sit down for 15 minutes this week?"

<CalloutBox type="tip">
The order matters. Motivation and timeline build context. Research stage reveals urgency. By the time you ask for the meeting, you've already demonstrated that you listened. The appointment feels like a logical next step, not a close. This is the same bridge principle behind every [appointment setting script](/blog/appointment-setting-script/) that actually converts.
</CalloutBox>

This framework works for every lead type: internet, referral, open house, even [ISA](/glossary/isa-real-estate/) handoffs. The words shift, but the structure stays the same.

When a lead responds to the commitment question with "just send me listings," that's not a rejection. It means you haven't built enough value for the meeting yet. Respond with: "Happy to. So I send you the right ones, can I ask a couple quick questions about what you're looking for?" Then circle back through the qualification questions. For a detailed breakdown on [handling "just send me listings,"](/objections/just-send-listings/) see our objection guide.

If the response is a flat ["I'm not interested"](/objections/not-interested/), validate and redirect: "Totally fair. Can I ask, was there something specific that caught your eye when you reached out, or were you just browsing?" One question can reopen a conversation that felt closed.

## Example Call: An Internet Lead From Start to Finish

Here's how the pieces fit on one call. The lead filled out a form on a portal about a three-bedroom in [Area] ten minutes ago.

**You:** Hey Maria, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were just looking at the three-bedroom on Oak Street on [Source], and I wanted to connect while it's fresh. Are you actively searching, or just starting to explore?

**Maria:** Kind of both. We're looking, but we're not in a rush.

**You:** That makes sense. What's got you thinking about a move right now?

**Maria:** Our lease is up and we're tired of renting. We want a yard for the kids.

**You:** A yard makes a big difference with kids. When are you hoping to be settled in a new place?

**Maria:** Our lease ends in May, so ideally before then.

**You:** Good, that gives us time to do it right. Have you talked with any other agents or seen any places you liked so far?

**Maria:** Not really. We’ve just been looking online. Can you just send me listings?

**You:** Happy to. So I send you the right ones, can I ask a couple quick questions? Is three bedrooms a must, and is there a monthly payment you want to stay under?

**Maria:** Three is a must. We'd like to stay under $2,800 a month.

**You:** Got it. Based on what you've told me, I think I can help. A 15-minute call this week would let me show you what's available in [Area] at that payment, plus a couple of homes that aren't on the portals yet. Does Thursday at 6 or Saturday at 10 work better?

**Maria:** Saturday at 10 works.

**You:** Perfect. I'll text you a calendar invite now, and I'll send three homes before then so we have something to talk about.

The opener names the source and asks one question. Motivation, timeline and research stage come next, in that order. The "just send me listings" objection gets agreement plus two questions instead of an argument, and the close offers two specific times. Log the answers in your CRM and send the listings before Saturday.

## FAQ

**What is the best phone script for calling real estate leads?**
The best script matches the lead source. Internet leads need a fast, low-pressure opener. Referral leads need warmth and the referrer's name up front. Open house leads need a specific callback to your in-person conversation. Use the Open-Bridge-Close framework and adapt the words to how the prospect found you.

**How fast should you call back a new real estate lead?**
As fast as you can, ideally within minutes. The first agent to have a meaningful conversation has the advantage, and the longer a lead waits the more likely they have spoken to someone else. If you can't call right away, send a short text to establish contact, then follow up as soon as you're free.

**How do you qualify a real estate lead over the phone?**
Ask four questions in order: motivation (why are they moving?), timeline (when do they need to be settled?), research stage (have they talked to other agents?), and commitment (would a 15-minute meeting make sense?). This framework moves the conversation from discovery to appointment without feeling forced.

**What do you say when a lead says "just send me listings"?**
It usually means you haven't demonstrated enough value for the in-person meeting. Respond with: "Happy to. So I send you the right ones, can I ask a couple quick questions about what you're looking for?" Then work through your qualification framework. Follow up in 3-5 days with specific listings and a softer meeting ask.
