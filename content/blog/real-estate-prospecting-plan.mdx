A real estate prospecting plan is a written weekly schedule that says whom you will contact, how you will contact them, when you will do it, and which numbers you will track. The simplest version has five parts: a goal worked back into weekly activity, two or three prospecting channels, protected time blocks on your calendar, a tracker that records every attempt and outcome, and a short weekly review. You can build all five in an afternoon and adjust them every Friday.

Below is a step-by-step plan with a sample week and a prospecting tracker you can copy. The numbers in the templates are blanks for a reason: the right figures depend on your goal, your market and your own conversion rates, so the [prospecting calculator](/tools/prospecting-calculator/) is where you find yours.

## Step 1: Start With Your Goal and Work Backward

Pick the number of clients you want to close this year, then divide it back through each step: signed clients, appointments, conversations and dials. If you know your own rate at each step, the math gives you the weekly activity you need. The calculator does this for you. You enter your yearly goal, the share of dials that become conversations, the share of conversations that become appointments, the share of appointments that become signed clients and the share of signed clients who close, plus how many weeks and days you prospect. It returns the dials, conversations and appointments needed each day and each week.

Use rates from your last 90 days if you have them, because older numbers may not reflect your current scripts and lead sources. If you are new and have no history, enter an estimate, label it as a guess and replace it with real numbers after your first four weeks. Rates quoted by other people describe their markets and their scripts, not yours.

## Step 2: Choose Two or Three Channels

A plan fails most often because it lists too many activities. Pick two or three from the [prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) and give each one a clear job.

| Neighborhood | One circle prospecting trigger or open house per week | [Circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/), [open house scripts](/blog/open-house-scripts/) |

## Step 3: Block the Time

The best plan on paper does nothing if the hours disappear into email and showings. Put your prospecting blocks on the calendar as appointments with yourself and schedule everything else around them.

How long should a block be? Published advice varies. Indeed's career guide suggests [spending 90 minutes every workday](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-prospect-in-real-estate) reaching out to potential clients, and Buildout's tips article suggests [60 to 90 minutes a day](https://www.buildout.com/blog-posts/real-estate-prospecting-tips). Treat these as starting points and adjust them to your weekly targets from step 1. A shorter block that you protect every day beats a longer block that happens twice a week.

## Step 5: Copy the Prospecting Tracker

A tracker is only useful if it takes less than a minute to fill in after each attempt. Paste the columns below into a spreadsheet or into your CRM's custom fields.

**Daily log: one row per attempt or conversation**

| Date | Channel | Name or source | Result | Conversation? | Outcome | Next step | Follow-up date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (date) | (list, sphere, neighborhood, listing, inbound) | (name or list name) | (no answer, voicemail, spoke) | (yes or no) | (appointment, follow up, not now, not interested) | (what you promised) | (date) |

**Weekly scoreboard: one row per week**

| Week of | Attempts | Conversations | Appointments set | Appointments held | Clients signed | Clients closed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (date) | (count) | (count) | (count) | (count) | (count) | (count) |

From the scoreboard, calculate these ratios each week:

- **Contact rate** = conversations ÷ attempts
- **Appointment rate** = appointments set ÷ conversations
- **Show rate** = appointments held ÷ appointments set
- **Sign rate** = clients signed ÷ appointments held
- **Close rate** = clients closed ÷ clients signed

The contact, appointment, sign and close rates are the ones the prospecting calculator asks for, so each month's results sharpen next month's targets. The show rate is an extra check on whether booked appointments turn into meetings. Signing and closing take weeks, so the last two columns will lag the others. Track the outcome as well as the activity, because a long list of attempts says little about whether the plan is working.

## Step 6: Do a 30-Minute Weekly Review

Every Friday, look at the scoreboard and ask one question: which step lost the most people this week? Then change one thing.

- **Few conversations from many attempts.** Look at the list, the time of day and whether you are leaving voicemails that invite a call back. The [voicemail scripts](/blog/real-estate-voicemail-scripts/) can help.
- **Plenty of conversations, few appointments.** The opening, the questions and the ask are the likely culprits. Read [how to start a real estate call](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/) and the [appointment setting script](/blog/appointment-setting-script/), then choose one change to try next week.
- **Appointments set but not held.** Confirm the appointment the day before, and send a short reminder with what to expect.
- **Appointments held, few signed.** Review how you prepare. The [listing appointment questions and checklist](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) is a good place to start.
- **Follow-ups not happening.** Put them in the calendar, not in your head. The guide on [how often to follow up](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/) gives a rhythm to start with.

When conversations are not turning into appointments, the live call is where the change has to happen. Some agents use [Sayso's Cue](/products/cue/) while they prospect so a suggested response is on screen when a prospect pushes back, and then they check on Friday whether the appointment rate moved. A tool is optional. Whatever you change, change one thing at a time so you can tell what worked.

## Common Prospecting Plan Mistakes

- **Too many channels.** Three channels done every week beat six done now and then.
- **No protected time.** If the block is not on the calendar, something else will take it.
- **Tracking effort but not results.** Count conversations and appointments as well as attempts.
- **Never reviewing.** A plan that never changes is a guess you stopped checking.
- **Skipping follow-up time.** Many of your best conversations will not end in an appointment the first time, so leave room to follow up.

## FAQ

**What should be in a real estate prospecting plan?**
A yearly goal broken into weekly activity, two or three prospecting channels, scheduled time blocks, a tracker for attempts and outcomes, and a weekly review. The templates above cover each of these.

**How do I create a real estate prospecting plan if I am new?**
Start with one calling channel and one relationship channel, use the calculator with estimated rates, and replace the estimates with your own numbers after four weeks. The [scripts for new agents](/blog/real-estate-scripts-for-new-agents/) help you start calling with words you trust.

**How long should I stick with a plan before changing it?**
Long enough to collect a meaningful number of attempts for each channel, which your calculator targets will tell you. Review every week, but make only one change at a time.

**What is the difference between a prospecting plan and a business plan?**
A business plan covers your goals, budget and income for the year. A prospecting plan is the weekly work that produces the leads and appointments the business plan needs.
