Cold calling real estate means phoning homeowners and leads who have not asked to hear from you, with the goal of starting a conversation that ends in a booked appointment. It works when each call has a specific reason behind it (a listing that expired, a home that just sold nearby, an owner selling without an agent), a short opener that earns a few more seconds, a question about the prospect's timeline, and a clear ask for the next step. Before you dial, check your list against the National Do Not Call Registry, because the federal rules cover real estate sales calls like any other.

This guide is the starting point for every real estate cold calling script on this site. It gives you an opener that fits most seller-side calls, the approach for each main lead source ([expired listings](/glossary/expired-listing), [FSBOs](/glossary/fsbo), [circle prospecting](/glossary/circle-prospecting) and buyer leads), answers to the objections you will hear most, and a link to the full script guide for each type of call.

The first conversation carries more weight than most agents give it. In the [2024 NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2024-11/2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2024_2.pdf), 81 percent of recent sellers contacted only one agent before choosing who to work with, and 66 percent used an agent who was referred to them or one they had worked with before. If you are not already that agent, the call is how you become the one they talk to.

## Cold Calling Real Estate: The Script to Start With

This opener works for most seller-side calls because it leads with a reason for calling and a question, not a pitch.

<ScriptExample label="Any Seller-Side Call: The Reason-and-Question Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling a few homeowners on [Street] because [a home nearby just sold / your listing came off the market / you mentioned selling at some point]. Quick question: if you were to make a move, would that be sometime this year or further out?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We're not selling": say "Understood. Is that a never, or a not right now?" A "not right now" is a conversation.
- **If they say** "Who is this again?": slow down, repeat your name and brokerage, and give the one-sentence reason for the call before asking anything else.
- **If they say** "Just send me something": say "Happy to. So I send the right thing, is it the value of your home or what's selling nearby that you'd want to see?"

## Before You Dial: Lists and the Rules

A cold call is only as good as the list behind it. Expired and withdrawn listings come from your MLS and from data services, which is where most agents start when [finding expired listings](/blog/how-to-find-expired-listings/). [Reverse prospecting](/blog/reverse-prospecting-real-estate/) in your MLS is another way to build a call list. Calling also works best as one of several [real estate prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/), next to door knocking, mail and your sphere, with your calling time protected in a [weekly prospecting plan](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-plan/).

Is cold calling legal for real estate agents? Yes, but it is regulated. The [FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule) says it is against the law to call a number on the National Do Not Call Registry unless you have an established business relationship with that person or they gave you written permission to call, and that if you must use the registry you need to update your lists against it at least every 31 days. Federal rules also bar sales calls to homes before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the local time of the person you are calling. Our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains how this applies to expired listings, FSBOs and past clients, and your broker can tell you how your office handles list scrubbing. This is general information, not legal advice.

Some agents hand the dialing to a virtual assistant or one of the [cold calling services for real estate](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-services/) instead of making the calls themselves.

## Why Most Cold Call Scripts for Real Estate Fall Flat

The calls that end with an appointment tend to be the ones that get past the opening line and into a real conversation. That comes down to three things: how you open, how you handle the first objection, and how often you ask for the appointment.

Most scripts fail because they are built around the agent, not the prospect. They open with "Hi, I'm calling because I'd love to help you sell your home." The prospect hears "salesperson" and the guard goes up immediately.

Effective real estate cold call scripts follow a three-phase structure:

### The Open-Bridge-Close Framework

**Phase 1: Open.** Earn permission to talk. Do not pitch. Ask a question that makes the prospect curious or gives them a reason to stay on the line.

**Phase 2: Bridge.** The discovery phase is where you should ask questions, listen, and build enough rapport to earn the right to suggest a next step. Most agents skip this entirely and jump to Phase 3, which is why so many of their calls end before the prospect has said anything about their plans.

**Phase 3: Close.** Ask for or suggest that the appointment is the next best step, using what you have gathered in phase two. If you can connect your appointment ask to the prospect's motivation and timeline, you win!

Every framework in this guide follows this structure. The words change based on the lead source, but the framework stays the same.

## Expired Listing Cold Call Scripts

[Expired listings](/glossary/expired-listing) are one of the strongest cold call sources because the homeowner has already shown they want to move, and they are often frustrated and skeptical of the next agent who calls. Open by acknowledging that the listing did not go the way they hoped, then ask whether they were getting showings with no offers or too little traffic, because that question shows you understand why homes do not sell. Our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) have the full openers, objection replies and follow-up sequence.

## FSBO Cold Call Scripts

[For Sale By Owner](/glossary/fsbo) homeowners are often open to talking with agents but do not want to pay a commission for work they believe they can handle themselves. Instead of arguing about commission, ask how the sale is going and what their timeline looks like, and treat the first call as a way to earn a second one. The [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/) cover the buyer-first opener, the follow-up plan and the objections FSBO sellers raise most.

## Circle Prospecting Scripts

[Circle prospecting](/glossary/circle-prospecting), calling homeowners near a recent sale or active listing, is the easiest cold call type for newer agents because the nearby sale gives you a reason to call. Mention the specific address, sale price and days on market, and when the homeowner says they are not thinking about moving, ask whether you can send a market update now and then, which turns a no into permission to stay in touch with [follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/). The [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) include the full just-sold script and how to handle the replies.

## Buyer Lead Scripts

Inbound buyer leads from Zillow, Realtor.com, your website or sign calls have already raised their hand, so the job is to reach them quickly, qualify them and offer a specific time to meet before they talk to other agents. Ask whether they have a move coming up, and when it is time to book, offer a day you are available instead of asking whether they would like to meet. The [phone scripts for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/) cover internet, open house and referral leads in full, and the same qualifying approach carries over to [buyer lead calls](/blog/buyer-lead-scripts/) and [calls to sellers](/blog/seller-scripts-real-estate/).

## How to Open Any Real Estate Cold Call

No matter which real estate cold call scripts you use, the first 10 seconds determine everything. In that window, the prospect decides whether you are worth talking to or just another interruption to their Tuesday morning. Most agents lose here because they sound scripted, nervous, or immediately sales-y.

### The Pattern Interrupt

Do not ask "Is now a good time?" That gives them a clean exit, and the socially acceptable answer is "no." Open with your reason for calling and a question about their plans instead, so the first thing they hear is a topic worth answering.

### Tone Beats Words

Here is something most script guides will not tell you: the exact words matter less than how you deliver them. An agent who sounds relaxed, confident, and conversational with a mediocre script will outperform an agent who sounds nervous and robotic with a perfect script every time.

The practical trick: smile while you dial. Literally. It changes your vocal tone in a way prospects can hear. And slow down your first sentence by about 30%. New agents rush through their opener because they are anxious, and fast speech signals "telemarketer" to the prospect's subconscious.

For a deep dive on opening techniques including the "reason for the call" framework and the "referral bridge," read [how to start a real estate call](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/). Cold calling real estate also takes practice out loud before you dial, so [practice your scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) and work through [role play scenarios](/blog/real-estate-role-play/) with a partner.

When nobody answers, a short [voicemail](/blog/real-estate-voicemail-scripts/) that gives your reason for calling can still earn a callback. A nearby listing or sale you handled is one of the easiest reasons to give, which is the idea behind [just listed and just sold calls](/blog/just-listed-just-sold-scripts/).

## Handling Objections Mid-Call

Most cold calls hit an objection early. The agents who book the most appointments are not the ones who avoid objections. They are the ones who expect them and have a response loaded before the prospect finishes the sentence.

The universal objection formula: **Validate → Question → Redirect.**

1. **Validate.** Acknowledge their position without agreeing or arguing. "I totally understand."
2. **Question.** Ask something that reopens the conversation. Make it about them, not you.
3. **Redirect.** Move toward the appointment or the next micro-commitment.

### The Three Most Common Objections

**"I'm not interested."** This is a reflex, not a considered decision. Most people who say this have not actually processed what you are offering. They are running an automatic "reject the salesperson" script of their own.

Response: "Understood. Is that a never, or a not right now?" A "not right now" gives you a reason to keep talking.

For the full script library on [responding to "not interested"](/objections/not-interested/), including the softer second-attempt approach, see our objection guide.

**"I already have an agent."** Do not compete. Compliment and plant a seed.

Response: "That's great, I'm glad you're working with someone. Just out of curiosity, how are they helping you [insert a topic]?"

See more scripts for [when a prospect already has an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/).

**"Call me later / I'm busy."** This is a soft no. If you just say "okay, when should I call back?" you will almost never reach them again.

Response: "No problem. Would later today or tomorrow morning be better for two minutes?"

Full response scripts for [the "call me later" objection](/objections/call-me-later/).

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Never argue with an objection. The moment you push back, the prospect's guard goes up permanently. Validate first, even if you disagree entirely. "I hear you" costs nothing and keeps the door open. The agents who try to "overcome" objections with logic lose. The agents who acknowledge and redirect win.
</CalloutBox>

## Closing for the Appointment on Every Call

This is where real estate cold call scripts either pay off or fall apart, and where most agents choke. They have a solid conversation, build rapport, handle objections smoothly, and then... never ask. They hang up hoping the prospect will somehow call them back.

Agents who let the appointment "happen naturally" rarely get one, because the prospect is waiting for a next step to be offered. The ask does not need to be aggressive. It needs to be specific.

### The Assumptive Close

"Based on what you've shared, I think it would make sense for us to sit down for 15 minutes, no pressure. I have Thursday at 2 or Friday at 10. Which works better for you?"

Two specific times. Not "when are you free?" Specificity reduces friction and eliminates decision fatigue. The prospect is choosing between two options, not deciding whether to meet at all.

### When They Hesitate

If you get a "maybe" or "let me think about it," do not retreat to "okay, call me whenever." Offer a lower-commitment step.

This converts a "maybe" into a concrete next step, which is always better than a dead end. Every next step you secure is a pipeline opportunity, and agents with full pipelines do not stress about individual calls.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Follow the Open-Bridge-Close framework.** Every call, regardless of lead source, should earn permission first, discover second, and close for the appointment third.
2. **The first 10 seconds decide the call.** Use a pattern interrupt, slow your pace, and sound like a human being, not a telemarketer reading a screen.
3. **Objections are engagement signals.** A prospect who pushes back is more engaged than one who politely says "sure, send me some info." Validate, question, redirect.
4. **Specificity wins.** Mention exact addresses, sale prices, and timeframes. Generic calls earn generic rejections.
5. **Ask for the appointment every single time.** Offer two specific times. The assumptive close is not pushy, it is professional.
6. **Consistency compounds.** A protected calling block every working day with solid scripts tends to beat an occasional burst of calls with a perfect script, because every call is practice.
7. **Log everything.** The appointment you book in 4 months starts with the CRM note you write today.

## FAQ

**What are the best real estate cold call scripts?**
The best scripts match the lead source. Expired listings, FSBOs, circle prospecting, and buyer leads each require different approaches. The scripts in this guide follow the Open-Bridge-Close framework and are designed to be adapted to your natural speaking voice. Start with the framework, then make it sound like you.

**Is cold calling legal for real estate agents?**
Yes, with limits. Numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry are off limits for sales calls unless you have an established business relationship or written permission, your lists must be checked against the registry at least every 31 days, and sales calls to homes are limited to 8 a.m. through 9 p.m. in the prospect's local time. Read our [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) and ask your broker how your office handles it.

**How many cold calls should a real estate agent make per day?**
There is no single right number. Pick a fixed calling block you can protect every working day, such as a morning hour, and measure how many conversations and appointments it produces. A smaller number of focused calls with a clear script usually beats a large number of rushed dials with no framework.

**What time is best for real estate cold calling?**
No study we found settles this, and the advice agents give conflicts. What holds across the advice is to stay inside the federal window of 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the prospect's local time (some states are stricter), then test a few blocks (early morning, midday, mid-afternoon, early evening, Saturday morning) and keep the ones where you reach the most people. Our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) guide shows how to test it.

**How do I get over the fear of cold calling?**
Fear decreases with repetition and preparation. Start with circle prospecting calls. Use a script until you have internalized the structure, then adapt the words to your voice. The anxiety usually eases as the calls add up. [New agents](/for/new-agents/) often benefit from real-time coaching tools that remove the pressure of remembering everything on your own.

**Is cold calling still effective in real estate in 2026?**
It can be, when it is done with a clear reason for each call, a script you have practiced, and consistent follow-up. The agents who say cold calling does not work are often making a handful of calls a week with no script and no follow-up plan, so it never gets a fair test.

**How do I get real estate leads without cold calling?**
Agents also build business through their sphere of influence, [open houses](/blog/open-house-scripts/), referrals, and follow-up with past clients. Choose the [prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) that fit your market and schedule, and keep the ones that produce appointments.

Coaching software falls into three groups: tools that coach you during the call, tools that review recordings afterward, and practice tools for role play.
