Cold calling real estate means calling homeowners and leads who have not asked to hear from you, with the goal of booking an appointment. The calls that work have a specific reason behind them, open with a question instead of a pitch, handle the first objection without arguing, and end with two specific times to meet. Before you dial, check your list against the National Do Not Call Registry, because federal rules cover real estate sales calls like any other.

This is a practitioner's playbook. The scripts come from the ones the agent development team at Anderson Real Estate Group writes for its own agents, and the mistakes are the ones we coach out of agents after listening to their calls. You will get an opener, a script for each lead source with the replies you will hear next, the live moments where calls are won or lost, a list of lines we hear on calls and what to say instead, and a way to structure a calling session so the dread does not decide whether you dial.

The first conversation carries more weight than most agents give it. In the [2024 NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2024-11/2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2024_2.pdf), 81 percent of recent sellers contacted only one agent before choosing who to work with, and 66 percent used an agent who was referred to them or one they had worked with before. If you are not already that agent, the call is how you become the one they talk to.

## Cold Calling Real Estate: The Opener Our Agents Start With

Most seller-side calls can open the same way: say who you are, give the one reason you are calling, and ask a question about their plans. The question matters more than anything else in the opener, because it hands the conversation to the prospect instead of starting a pitch.

<ScriptExample label="Any Seller-Side Call: The Reason-and-Question Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling a few homeowners on [Street] because [a home nearby just sold / your listing came off the market / your home value request came in]. Quick question: if you were to make a move, would that be sometime this year or further out?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We're not selling": say "Understood. Is that a never, or a not right now?" A "not right now" is a conversation.
- **If they say** "Who is this again?": slow down, repeat your name and brokerage, and give the one-sentence reason for the call before asking anything else.
- **If they say** "Just send me something": say "Happy to. So I send the right thing, is it the value of your home or what's selling nearby that you'd want to see?"

## Before You Dial: Lists and the Rules

A call is only as good as the reason behind it, and the reason comes from the list. Expired and withdrawn listings come from your MLS and from data services, and our guide to [finding expired listings](/blog/how-to-find-expired-listings/) walks through where agents look. [Reverse prospecting](/blog/reverse-prospecting-real-estate/) is another way to build a list from your MLS. For where calling fits next to door knocking, mail and your sphere, read our guide to [real estate prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) and then build a [weekly prospecting plan](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-plan/) around it.

Is cold calling legal for real estate agents? Yes, but it is regulated. The [FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule) says it is against the law to call a number on the National Do Not Call Registry unless you have an established business relationship with that person or they gave you written permission to call, and that if you must use the registry you need to update your lists against it at least every 31 days. Federal rules also bar sales calls to homes before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the local time of the person you are calling. Our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains how this applies to expired listings, FSBOs and past clients, and your broker can tell you how your office handles list scrubbing. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How to Structure a Calling Session

Agents who stop calling rarely stop because the scripts are bad. They stop because the hour feels awful. Here is how we set up a session so the agent dials whether or not they feel ready.

### The Dread Lives Before the Dial

When agents describe call anxiety, they describe the silence between dials, not the conversations. The worst moment is sitting with the list open, thumb over the next number, picturing every way it could go wrong. Once someone picks up, there is a job to do and most of the fear goes quiet.

So shrink the gap. Make your first dial within a minute of sitting down, before you have time to "get ready," because the opener is the same either way. Line up a batch of contacts before the block starts and move from one to the next without replaying the last call. Write the one note that matters and dial again; email and follow-ups wait until the block ends.

### Score Your Inputs, Not the Outcome

Judge a session by how many times you dialed and how many conversations you had, not by how many appointments you booked. You do not control whether the person who answers is motivated, already has an agent, or is having a terrible day. You do control your list, your opener, whether you ask a second question, and how many times you dial.

When appointments are the only scoreboard, most sessions feel like failure even when you did everything right, and that feeling makes tomorrow's block harder to start. When dials and conversations are the scoreboard, a session with a handful of two-way conversations is a win whether or not anyone booked.

### You Are Offering a Service, Not Interrupting a Life

Guilt is a big part of the dread. If you believe every call is an imposition, each dial costs you twice: once for the risk of rejection and once for the feeling that you are bothering someone. A move is one of the largest financial decisions a person makes, and some of the people on your list are already weighing it without anyone to talk it through with.

The person who is not thinking about moving will tell you in ten seconds and you both move on. The person who is thinking about it just got a useful conversation they may not have started on their own.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
The goal is not to stop disliking calls. It is to make the dread small enough that it stops deciding whether you dial. Start fast, call in batches, and grade yourself on effort you control.
</CalloutBox>

## Scripts by Lead Source

The lines below are adapted from the objection scripts our team uses. Each follows the same pattern: acknowledge what they said in a few words, ask one question about their situation, and offer a next step.

### Expired Listings

[Expired listing](/glossary/expired-listing/) owners already decided to sell once, watched it fail, and now their phone rings all morning. The agent who wins this call is the first one who asks about them instead of talking about themselves.

<ScriptExample label="Expired Listing: Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home on [Street] came off the market, and I know that's probably not the outcome you were hoping for. Are you still hoping to be moved by [Timeframe], or are you taking a break from it?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next, and what our scripts say back:

- **"I've had 30 agents call me today."** "I believe it, and I'm sorry about the phone ringing nonstop. Has anyone asked why you're moving? Where are you headed once the home sells?"
- **"We're going to relist with the same agent."** "I respect that. Have you already signed a new agreement with them?" If they have, step back and wish them well. If not, ask whether a second opinion would help before they decide.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
The most common expired-listing mistake is leading with credentials. Ask what buyers said about price and showings instead. "Showings but no offers" and "no showings at all" are different problems, and asking which one it was earns more credibility than any award.
</CalloutBox>

For the full library with follow-up sequences, see our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/).

### For Sale by Owner

[FSBO](/glossary/fsbo/) sellers are against paying for something they believe they can handle, so arguing about commission loses the call. Ask about their bottom line and their plan, and let the gaps show up in their own answers.

FSBO sellers are also a smaller group than many agents assume. Only 5 percent of sales were FSBO in the [2025 NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-11/2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2025.pdf), an all-time low. In the [2024 edition](https://nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2024-11/2024-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2024_2.pdf), 38 percent of FSBO sellers said the main reason was that they sold to a relative, friend or neighbor, 30 percent did not want to pay a commission or fee, and 63 percent did not actively market their home. That last number is your opening: most owners on a FSBO list have no marketing plan, and asking how buyers are finding the home tends to start a better conversation than any talk about commission.

<ScriptExample label="FSBO: Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner. I'm not calling to talk you out of it. How's it going so far?
</ScriptExample>

- **"We want to sell it ourselves to save the commission."** "Makes sense. How much are you hoping to save? What matters most to you, the commission or the bottom line? How are you handling buyers who come with an agent?"
- **"We'll call you if it doesn't sell."** "Fair enough. What date would you give yourself before calling an agent? Can I check in on [Date] to see how it's going?"

The date question is the whole call. Once the seller names their own deadline, your follow-up is something they agreed to. Log the date and set the task before you dial the next number.

### Circle Prospecting and Just Sold Calls

[Circle prospecting](/glossary/circle-prospecting/) is the easiest call for a newer agent because the reason is built in. The more specific the detail, the more the call sounds like local knowledge instead of a purchased list.

<ScriptExample label="Circle Prospecting: Just Sold">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. A home on your street at [Address] just sold for [Price] after [Days] on the market, and I'm letting a few neighbors know. Have you thought about what that might mean for your home's value?
</ScriptExample>

{/* AREG DATA: an anonymized AREG just-sold call where the specific address and sale price opened a conversation with a neighbor who was planning a move */}

Most people will tell you they are not moving. Here is what our team says back:

- **"We're not interested in selling."** "Understood. Is that a never, or a not right now? If you did sell, where would you go next? Would a yearly home value update be helpful?"
- **"We're waiting for rates to drop"** or **"I'll lose my low rate."** "Makes sense. What would make a move worth it to you: more space, a better location, less house? And what's the plan if rates stay where they are?"

A yearly value update is a win on this list, because the follow-ups are where it pays off. Explore more [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) and [follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/).

### Online Seller Leads Who Asked for a Home Value

Home value requests sit between cold and warm, and many people do not remember clicking. The call goes wrong when the agent acts as if the lead is ready to list, and goes right when the agent asks what is behind the curiosity.

<ScriptExample label="Online Seller Lead: Home Value Request">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Your home value request for [Address] came in through [Lead Source] on [Date]. Is that still your home? Were you curious about the value, or did something get you thinking about selling?
</ScriptExample>

- **"I was just curious what it's worth."** "Makes sense. Curiosity usually has a reason behind it. If the value came in at [Price], what would you do with that?"
- **"Just email me the report."** "Happy to. Online values can't see condition or upgrades. What have you updated since you bought it? I'll email it now, and then 15 minutes at the house to fine-tune it. Would today at [Time] or tomorrow at [Time] work?"
- **"The website says my home is worth more."** "An online estimate can't walk through your house, so it's a starting point. What upgrades have you done that it wouldn't know about?"

### Buyer Leads

Buyer leads raised their hand, so the job is finding out how ready they are and booking the next step before someone else does. Speed matters more than polish; the [buyer lead scripts](/blog/buyer-lead-scripts/) guide covers qualification in depth.

<ScriptExample label="Buyer Lead: First Call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were looking at [Property Address] on [Lead Source], and I wanted to reach you while it's fresh. What caught your eye about that one?
</ScriptExample>

- **"I'm just browsing."** "Perfect, that's the best time to start. How long have you been looking? If you found the right home tomorrow, would you be ready to move on it?"
- **"I only wanted info on this one home."** "Got it. Have you been inside it yet, or just seen the photos? Would you like to see it in person today at [Time] or tomorrow at [Time]?"
- **"I'll call the listing agent directly."** "You can. The listing agent represents the seller. Would you like your own agent looking out for your side?"
- **"I already have an agent."** "Great, are you under a signed buyer agreement with them?" If yes, respect it and step back. If not, ask whether they are happy with how it's going.

If the person turns out to be a seller, our [seller scripts for real estate agents](/blog/seller-scripts-real-estate/) cover the listing side, and the [phone scripts for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/) cover internet, open house and referral leads.

## The Live-Call Moments That Decide the Call

Calls are won or lost in a handful of moments that show up on almost every list. These are the ones we listen for when we review calls.

### The First Ten Seconds

The prospect decides in the first few sentences whether you are a person with a reason or a salesperson with a list. Nervous agents speed through the opener, and fast, flat speech sounds like a telemarketer. Slow the first sentence down and get to the question quickly.

Do not open with "Is now a good time?" It hands the prospect the easiest possible exit, and the polite answer is no. Lead with the reason and the question so the first thing they hear is a topic worth answering. For more openers, read [how to start a real estate call](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/).

### "How Did You Get My Number?"

This question is a test of whether you are hiding something. Answer it plainly and go right back to them: "Fair question. Your info came in through [Lead Source] on [Date] for [Address]. I'm [Your Name] with [Brokerage], we help homeowners in [City]. Are you still at [Address]?" Agents who dodge this question rarely get a second one.

### "Take Me Off Your List"

Our script for this is one line: "Understood, I'll remove you right now. You won't hear from me again." Then remove them before you dial the next number. Do not try one more question. It is the respectful response, and it keeps you on the right side of the do-not-call rules.

### The Blank Second After an Objection

A large share of call anxiety is fear of one moment: the prospect pushes back and your mind goes empty. The fix is to never improvise the parts you can prepare. Before the block starts, put an opener card in front of you with your opener and your answers to the two or three objections you hear most on that list.

<ScriptExample label="Opener Card: Keep This in Front of You">
Opener: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling a few homeowners on [Street] because [reason]. If you were to make a move, would that be this year or further out?"

If "I'm not interested": "Understood. Is that a never, or a not right now?"

If "Call me later": "No problem. Would later today or tomorrow morning be better for two minutes?"

If "We need to think about it": "Totally fine. What part do you need to think through: price, timing, or me?"
</ScriptExample>

The card is not there to read robotically. It is there so the next line exists when your mind goes quiet. ## Mistakes We Hear on Calls (and What to Say Instead)

These are the lines we flag most when listening back to agents' calls. Each one sounds reasonable in the moment, which is why they stick.

| What the agent said | Why it fails | What to say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Is now a good time?" | Hands them the easy no before you've given a reason | "I'm calling because [reason]. Quick question: would a move be this year or further out?" |
| "I'd love to help you sell your home." | A pitch before they've said anything; guard goes up | "Is selling a never, or a not right now?" |
| "Okay, when should I call you back?" | No time, no reason; the callback never happens | "Would later today or tomorrow morning be better for two minutes?" |
| "Sure, what's your email?" (then hanging up) | Gives away the only reason for a second conversation | "Happy to. What have you updated since you bought it? Then 15 minutes at the house to fine-tune it." |
| "Rates should come down next year." | Turns the agent into a rate forecaster nobody asked for | "What rate would get you moving? What's the plan if rates stay where they are?" |
| "When would you like to meet?" | An open question invites "I'll let you know" | "Would today at [Time] or tomorrow at [Time] work?" |
| Three questions in one breath | The prospect answers the easiest one and the rest disappear | Ask one question, then stop talking |

The failing line is about the agent's goal, and the replacement is a question about the prospect's situation. When you catch yourself explaining, arguing or pitching, turn the next sentence into a question.

## Handling Objections Mid-Call

Most calls hit an objection early. The agents who book the most appointments expect them and have the next line ready, using the same formula as every script above: acknowledge, ask one question, move toward a next step.

{/* AREG DATA: the five objections AREG agents hear most often on seller-side lists, ranked by frequency from call reviews, last 90 days */}

**"We need to think about it."** Our script: "Totally fine. What part do you need to think through: price, timing, or me? If that part was handled, would you be ready to move forward? When can we reconnect, [Day] at [Time]?" The "or me" is deliberate. It gives them permission to name the concern instead of disappearing. See more on [the "need to think about it" objection](/objections/need-to-think-about-it/).

**"I need to talk to someone else first."** "Of course. Who else is part of the decision? What do you think they'll ask first? Let's set a time when you can both be on." Get the second decision-maker on the next call instead of relaying everything secondhand. See [when a spouse needs to decide](/objections/my-spouse-needs-to-decide/).

**"Your commission is too high."** "I hear you. Commissions are negotiable and not set by law. What part feels too high, the number or what you get for it?" That second question splits a price objection from a value objection, and you handle them differently. More in [how much is your commission](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/).

**"I'm not interested"** and **"call me later"** are reflexes more than decisions. Use the lines from the opener card, and see our full pages on [responding to "not interested"](/objections/not-interested/), [the "call me later" objection](/objections/call-me-later/) and [when a prospect already has an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/).

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Never argue with an objection. Once you push back with logic, the prospect defends their position instead of describing it. Acknowledge first, then ask a question.
</CalloutBox>

## Asking for the Appointment

This is the moment agents skip. They have a good conversation and then say "Let me know if anything changes," which hands the next step to a prospect who will not take it. The ask does not need to be aggressive. It needs to be specific.

Every booking line in our scripts uses the same shape: "Would today at [Time] or tomorrow at [Time] work?" Two specific times turn the decision from "should I meet this person" into "which time is better." Tie it to what they told you: "You said you'd want to be moved by summer. Fifteen minutes at the house this week would give you a firm number to plan around. Would Thursday at 2 or Friday at 10 work?"

If you get a "maybe," offer a smaller step they can say yes to: a walkthrough to fine-tune the value, a check-in on a date they choose, or a call with the second decision-maker. For more booking lines, see our [appointment setting scripts](/blog/appointment-setting-script/).

## FAQ

**Is cold calling legal for real estate agents?**
Yes, with limits. Numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry are off limits for sales calls unless you have an established business relationship or written permission, your lists must be checked against the registry at least every 31 days, and sales calls to homes are limited to 8 a.m. through 9 p.m. in the prospect's local time. Read our [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) and ask your broker how your office handles it.

**How many calls should a real estate agent make per day?**
There is no single right number. Pick a calling block you can protect every working day and measure how many conversations and appointments it produces.

**What time is best for real estate cold calling?**
No study we found settles this, and the advice agents give conflicts. Stay inside the federal window of 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the prospect's local time (some states are stricter), then test a few blocks such as early morning, midday, late afternoon, early evening and Saturday morning, and keep the ones where you reach the most people. Our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) guide shows how to test it.

**Is cold calling worth it if I hate doing it?**
For most agents, yes, because it is one of the few prospecting methods where you decide how many conversations you start each day, and many agents avoid it for the same reasons you dislike it. The question is less whether it works and more how to get yourself to do it. Shorten the gap between dials, keep an opener card in front of you, and grade yourself on dials and conversations.

**How do I get over the fear of cold calling?**
You make the fear small enough to act through. Dial within a minute of sitting down, call in batches, and prepare answers to the objections you hear most so the blank second never comes. If one bad call rattles you, write the note that matters and dial the next contact right away; review it after the block. [New agents](/for/new-agents/) can start with circle prospecting, where the reason to call is built in.

**What is the best cold calling coaching software for real estate agents?**
Coaching software falls into three groups: tools that coach you during the call, tools that review recordings afterward, and practice tools for role play. Our guide to the [best real estate call coaching software](/blog/best-real-estate-call-coaching-software/) explains how each works and who it suits.
