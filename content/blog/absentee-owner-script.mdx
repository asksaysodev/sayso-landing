An absentee owner script names the property, confirms the person still owns it, and asks what their plans are for it: hold it, sell it, or buy more. Absentee owners own a home they do not live in, such as a rental, second home or inherited property, so lead with their plans and their numbers.

You can spot absentee owners in public records when the owner's mailing address is different from the property address. They decide on numbers and hassle more than emotion. Some want to sell, some want to buy more, and some are tired of managing from a distance, and the script below works for all three.

<ScriptExample label="Absentee owner first call">
Hi, is this [Name]? Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your property on [Street] in [City]. Do you still own that one?

Great. I work a lot in that area, and I'm reaching out to owners who don't live in their properties, because the plans tend to vary a lot. Are you planning to hold onto it for a while, or have you thought about selling at some point?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "It's a rental and we're keeping it": say "That makes sense. Are you looking to add another property, or is one enough to manage right now?"
- **If they say** "We've thought about selling": say "What has you thinking about it? Is it the tenant side, the market, or something else?"
- **If they say** "How did you get my information?": say "Ownership records are public, and I call owners in that area. If you'd rather not hear from me, I'm happy to take you off my list."

This call builds on the approach in our [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/). The difference is that an absentee owner has no emotional attachment to the neighborhood news you would use on a [circle prospecting script](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/), so lead with their plans and their numbers instead.

## What Is an Absentee Owner?

An absentee owner is a person or company that owns a property but does not live in it. The most common types are landlords with one or two rentals, owners of second or vacation homes, heirs who inherited a home, and owners who moved for work and kept the old house. Many live in another city or state, which matters for your call: calling hours are measured in the owner's time zone, not yours, as our guide to [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains.

## What Makes a Good Absentee Owner Cold Calling Script?

A good script for any real estate cold call does three things in the first 20 seconds: says who you are, gives a specific reason for calling this person, and asks a question that is easy to answer. For absentee owners, the property address is the specific reason and "Do you still own that one?" is the easy question. After that:

- Ask about their plans before you mention selling.
- Ask one question at a time and let the answer decide the next one.
- Offer numbers (what it would rent for, what it would sell for) instead of a pitch.
- Honor any request to stop calling on the spot.

## Which Absentee Owner Script Should You Use When?

| Situation | Script | Goal |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Any owner who lives elsewhere | First call | Learn whether they plan to hold, sell or buy |
| The property is a rental | Landlord script | Uncover tenant or management fatigue |
| The property looks vacant or was inherited | Vacant or inherited script | Understand the plan and the timeline |
| No answer | Voicemail | Get a callback with a specific reason |
| No answer after a call and voicemail | Text message | Start a conversation the owner can reply to |
| You only have a mailing address | Letter | Give the owner a reason to call you |
| You have a qualified buyer for that area | Buyer-in-hand call | Find out if the owner would consider an offer |

## What Do You Say to an Absentee Landlord?

Landlords respond to questions about returns and hassle, so ask about their strategy first.

<ScriptExample label="Absentee landlord script">
How long have you owned the place on [Street]? And what's your strategy with it right now: long-term hold, short-term rental, or something else?

How has it been managing it from a distance? Do you have a property manager, or are you handling tenants yourself?

A lot of owners in that area have built up more equity than they realize. Would it help if I sat down with you for 20 minutes and showed you what it would sell for against what it's earning you, so you can decide whether to keep it, sell it, or trade into something else?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "The rent covers the mortgage, so it's fine": say "That's a good spot to be in. Do you know what your equity is doing in the meantime? Sometimes that number changes the decision."
- **If they say** "The tenants have been a headache": say "I hear that a lot. What would it take for you to be done with it?"
- **If they say** "I'd rather buy another one": say "Then let's look at that. What would the next property need to have for the numbers to work?"

## What Do You Say When the Property Is Vacant or Inherited?

Some absentee owners have a home sitting empty after a move, a job change, or a family member's passing. Keep the tone gentle, and if the property came through an estate that is still in probate, switch to the [probate real estate script](/blog/probate-real-estate-script/).

<ScriptExample label="Vacant or inherited property script">
It looks like the home on [Street] may not have anyone living in it right now. Is that right?

What's the plan for it? Are you thinking of renting it, selling it, or keeping it in the family?

Is there a timeline you're working toward, or has it just been sitting while life gets busy?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We haven't decided": say "That's common. Would it help to know what it would rent for against what it would sell for? That usually makes the decision easier."
- **If they say** "It needs a lot of work": say "A lot of owners in your spot sell it as-is. I can come by and show you what that would look like next to fixing it up first."

## What Should You Say in an Absentee Owner Voicemail?

Include the address, since many absentee owners have more than one property. For more examples, see our [realtor voicemail scripts](/blog/real-estate-voicemail-scripts/).

<ScriptExample label="Absentee owner voicemail">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your property on [Street] in [City]. I have some recent numbers on what homes like it are renting and selling for, and I'd be happy to share them. Call or text me at [Number].
</ScriptExample>

## Absentee Owner Text Message Scripts

Texts work well for owners who live out of the area and screen unknown calls. Send them one at a time from your own phone, and check the [do not call rules](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) before you text a list, because the registry covers texts and automated texts need prior written consent.

<ScriptExample label="Absentee owner first text">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I tried calling about your property on [Street] in [City]. I have recent rent and sale numbers for that block if they'd be useful. Want me to send them over?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Absentee owner follow-up text">
Hi [Name], [Your Name] again. A home near yours on [Street] just [sold / rented], and I thought you'd want to know what it means for yours. Happy to share the details whenever you have a minute.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they reply** "Send the numbers": send them, then ask "Does that change anything about your plans for the property?"
- **If they reply** "Not selling": say "Understood. Would a once-a-year update on what it's worth be useful, or would you rather I not reach out?"
- **If they reply** "Stop": reply "Got it, I won't text again," and add them to your internal do-not-call list.

## Absentee Owner Letter Script

Public records give you the owner's mailing address even when you have no phone number. A short letter with one specific offer gets more replies than a general introduction.

<ScriptExample label="Absentee owner letter">
Dear [Name],

I'm [Your Name], an agent with [Brokerage] who works in [Neighborhood]. I'm writing about your property at [Street].

Owners who live out of the area often tell me it is hard to know what their property is worth today, or what it would rent for if the tenant moved out. I put together those numbers for homes like yours on request, at no cost and with no obligation.

If you'd like them, call or text me at [Number], or email [Email]. If you'd rather not hear from me, let me know and I'll take you off my list.

[Your Name]
</ScriptExample>

## The Buyer-in-Hand Call: Only When You Have a Real Buyer

If you are working with a qualified buyer who wants that specific area and that type of property, an absentee owner is worth a direct call. Only use this script when the buyer exists and has agreed to you reaching out on their behalf.

<ScriptExample label="Buyer-in-hand call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your property on [Street]. I'm working with a buyer who is looking for a [type of property] in that neighborhood, and nothing on the market fits right now.

Would you ever consider selling it if the price and timing were right for you?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "What would they pay?": say "That depends on the home's condition, which is why I'd want to see it. Would it be okay if I came by this week to take a look?"
- **If they say** "No, we're keeping it": say "Understood. Is it okay if I keep you in mind if prices in your area change a lot?"

## How Do You Handle Objections on Absentee Owner Calls?

- **"I'm not selling."** Ask whether they are looking to buy instead, then offer a yearly equity update. The [not interested objection](/objections/not-interested/) page has more exits that keep the door open.
- **"Maybe in a year or two."** Ask what would need to happen for that timeline to move, and set a follow-up. See the [not ready yet objection](/objections/not-ready-yet/) for wording.
- **"I'd just sell it to an investor."** Offer to compare a quick sale against listing it, so they see the difference in what they would walk away with.
- **"I'm worried about the taxes if I sell."** Do not give tax advice. Say "That's a good question for your tax advisor. I can put together the sale numbers so you have something concrete to bring them."

If an absentee owner mentions they are behind on payments, slow down and use the approach in our [pre-foreclosure script](/blog/pre-foreclosure-script/). If the property is part of a separation, the [divorce listing script](/blog/divorce-listing-script/) explains how to stay neutral between both owners.

## How Often Should You Follow Up With Absentee Owners?

Most absentee owners are not ready the first time you call, but their plans change when a tenant moves out, a repair comes up, or prices move. Set a follow-up based on what they told you: a few weeks out if they are deciding, a few months out if they mentioned a lease ending, and a yearly value update if they plan to hold. Each follow-up should bring something new, such as a nearby sale or a current rent estimate. ## FAQ

**What does absentee owner mean?**
An absentee owner is a person or company that owns a property but does not live in it. Rentals, vacation homes, inherited homes, and homes kept after a move all count. In public records, the owner's mailing address is different from the property address.

**How do you find absentee owners?**
Pull a list from public records or a data provider and filter for properties where the owner's mailing address doesn't match the property address.

**Are absentee owners good leads for real estate agents?**
They can be, because their decisions tend to be practical. Some are tired of managing a property from a distance, some want to sell while they have equity, and some want to buy more. Ask about their plans first rather than assuming they want to sell.

**What should you say in an absentee owner voicemail?**
Name the property address, give one specific reason to call back, such as recent rent and sale numbers, and leave your number. Keep it under 20 seconds.
