A good buyer lead script does three things on the first call: it opens with the reason you are calling, it asks why the buyer is thinking about a move right now, and it ends by booking a short buyer consultation for today or tomorrow instead of sending a list of homes. The first call is for learning the buyer's motivation and timeline well enough to earn the meeting where the real planning happens, and showing homes can wait until then.

This guide has the word-for-word scripts for four first-call situations: a new online buyer lead, a lead who asked to tour a specific home, a buyer who went quiet, and a buyer who says "just send me listings." Each one comes with what buyers usually say next and how to answer. The questions to ask at the meeting itself are in our [buyer consultation questions](/blog/buyer-consultation-questions/) guide, so this post stops where the consultation starts. Both sit inside our broader playbook on [how to talk to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/).

## Which Buyer Script to Use When

Match the script to how the buyer came to you, because a lead who just inquired needs a different first sentence than one you have not spoken to in months.

| Situation | Script | Goal of the call |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| A new online lead just inquired | New buyer lead, first call | Learn the move and book the consultation |
| The buyer asked to tour a specific home | Tour request call | Confirm the showing and learn why this home |
| The buyer inquired a while ago and went quiet | Re-engagement call | Find out if they are still moving |
| The buyer asks you to send listings | Redirect to the consultation | Keep control of the conversation |
| You reach voicemail | Voicemail and text | Get a reply |

## Script 1: The New Buyer Lead, First Call

Call as soon as you can after the inquiry, while the buyer still remembers filling out the form. Keep the call conversational. You are working toward a meeting, so the questions below are a short version of what you will cover later.

<ScriptExample label="New Buyer Lead: First Call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name], a real estate agent with [Brokerage]. You recently asked about homes in [Area] on [source], and I wanted to call you personally and thank you for reaching out. Is [Area] your primary location of interest, or are you open to other areas too?

What price range are you aiming for right now?

I'm curious, are you renting or do you own a home you'd need to sell before you buy?

The best way for me to help you find the right place is to spend 20 to 30 minutes talking through your plans and mapping out a game plan. That way we keep this simple. What works better for you, later today or tomorrow?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "I'm renting": say "Are you month to month or in a lease? If you found the right home, could you get out of your lease?" That tells you how soon they can move.
- **If they say** "I own a home and need to sell first": say "Is your house on the market yet? One of the first steps in knowing what you can buy is knowing what your home will sell for and what you'd net. Has anyone put together a market analysis for you?" This opens the door to helping with both sides of the move.
- **If they say** "I haven't talked to a lender": say "That's not a problem. I can have a lender I trust reach out to you if you'd like." The full lender conversation, including what to say to buyers who already have one or are paying cash, is in the [buyer consultation questions](/blog/buyer-consultation-questions/) guide.
- **If they say** "I'm already working with an agent": say "That's great. Did you sign anything with them?" If they did, say "Then you're in good hands, and I don't want to get in the middle of that. If anything changes, I'm happy to help." If they did not, ask what they are hoping an agent will do for them. More wording is on the [already have an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/) page.

Always ask why they are moving, even if the lead form already gave you a reason. The answer they give first is usually the surface one, so follow with "Tell me more about that." Our guide to the [LPMAMA framework](/blog/lpmama-real-estate/) shows how to cover location, price, motivation, agent, mortgage and appointment in the order that feels natural.

## Script 2: The Buyer Who Asked to Tour a Home

A buyer who requested a showing has told you which home they care about. Treat the call as two jobs: confirm the showing, and use the moment to find out why this home and why now.

<ScriptExample label="Tour Request: First Call">
Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I see you'd like to tour [Address] on [Day] at [Time]. Does that time still work for you?

Great, I'll confirm the showing and call you right back. While I have you, what did you like most about this home?

[Let them tell you what stood out. Ask "Why is that important to you?" once or twice.]

Who else is making the move with you? And I should have asked this already, but why are you looking at homes right now?

In a perfect world, when do you want to be moved in by?
</ScriptExample>

Before you hang up, tell the buyer exactly what happens next, so they know you are working for them:

<ScriptExample label="Tour Request: Closing the Call">
It was great talking with you, [Name]. Here is how I'm going to start working for you. First, I'll confirm [Time] on [Day]. If that doesn't work for the seller's side, what other days and times could I suggest? I'll call you back with confirmation within about 30 minutes.

Second, I'll find answers to the questions we talked about and bring them to our appointment. Third, since you're looking for [features], I'll pull together a few other homes I think could fit so we can talk about them in person. Is there anything else I can do to make this easier for you?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Can you just tell me if it's still available?": say "Happy to check on that. Since I'm doing that for you anyway, tell me what drew you to this one, so I can tell you if there are similar ones worth seeing."
- **If they say** "We're just curious, we aren't ready to buy": say "That's a good thing to know. What would have to be true for you to be ready?" Then book a short call instead of pushing for an in-person meeting.

### What to say about the written buyer agreement

Buyers are often surprised when an agent mentions an agreement before the first tour, so bring it up early and plainly. Since August 17, 2024, NAR's practice changes have required MLS participants who are working with a buyer to sign a written agreement with that buyer before touring a home, whether in person or on a live virtual tour. That requirement is for a written agreement, and it does not have to be a specific type of agency agreement, according to [NAR's explanation](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101). Your brokerage will have its own form, and your state may have additional rules, so ask your broker what to use.

<ScriptExample label="Mentioning the Buyer Agreement Before a Tour">
Before we head in, there's one quick thing I go over with every buyer. I use a short written agreement that lists what I'll do for you and how I'm paid. It's required before I show a home. Has anyone walked you through one before? Take a look, and if you have questions, ask me anything before you sign.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I toured with another agent and nobody mentioned this": say "That may be because it hadn't come up yet. I prefer to handle everything up front so there are no surprises later."
- **If they say** "I don't want to sign anything yet": say "I understand. Help me understand what feels unclear, and I'll walk you through it." Do not argue. Offer to go over the document in the consultation, and tell the buyer you can set the tour once the paperwork question is settled.

This is not legal advice. Follow your broker's guidance on which form to use and when.

## Script 3: The Buyer Who Went Quiet

Old inquiries are some of the easiest buyer calls to make, because the buyer already asked about homes and you are only checking in. The point of the first question is to find out whether they bought, kept looking, or paused.

<ScriptExample label="Old Buyer Inquiry: Check-In Call">
Hey [Name]? This is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I was going through our notes and saw you had asked about [number]-bedroom homes in [City]. Quick question, did you end up moving already, or are you still waiting?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We bought": say "Oh nice, congratulations. Just so I update our records and don't bother you again, what city did you end up buying in, and about how long ago was that?"
- **If they say** "We're still looking": say "Got it. Did you pause the search for a bit, or have you been casually looking this whole time? Moving is a big decision. What's pushing you to move now?" Then ask who is moving with them, which areas matter and why, and when they would ideally want to move.
- **If they say** "We're not sure we still want to": say "That makes sense. What would have to change for it to feel like the right time?" Set a follow-up date.

When the buyer is still looking and you have the answers, make the offer: "That lines up well with what you're describing. I sit down with people in your situation and walk through the three biggest things that matter in today's market. For some it confirms that moving now makes sense, and for others it helps them decide waiting is smarter. Would you be open to hearing those so you can decide either way?" Then book it using the close later in this guide.

## Script 4: When the Buyer Says "Just Send Me Listings"

Buyers often ask for homes early in the conversation, and the request is reasonable. Acknowledge it, then bring the conversation back to understanding the move. An automated search sends everything that matches a price and a bedroom count, and most of those alerts get ignored or buried. If all you do is send links, you are another search portal, and you stop guiding the conversation.

<ScriptExample label="Redirecting a Request for Listings">
I'd be happy to send homes. So I send the right ones, tell me about your goals. What's got you thinking about making a move right now?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I just want to see what's out there": say "That makes sense. What would have to be true about a home for you to want to see it in person?"
- **If they say** "Can't you just set up a search?": say "I can. I'd like it to be a good one, though, and I can't do that without knowing what matters to you. Give me 20 minutes and you'll only see homes worth your time. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?" The [just send listings](/objections/just-send-listings/) page has more ways to say it.
- **If they say** "We're just browsing": say "Totally fair. Most people start there. What caught your eye?" Then see the [just looking](/objections/just-looking/) page for the follow-up.

Some buyers should get a home search, and the rule is simple. Send homes to a buyer who has a signed buyer agreement, a defined motivation and timeline, and a scheduled next step. Until those conditions are true, keep working on understanding the move.

## Booking the Buyer Consultation

Every buyer script above is built to end here. Use two options, include the decision maker, and confirm the details before you hang up.

<ScriptExample label="Booking the Buyer Consultation">
Here's what I'd suggest: let's take 20 minutes to strategize. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?

Are you more of a morning, afternoon or evening person? And would that work for [spouse or other decision maker] too?

We can do a quick 20 to 25 minute sit down at my office or over coffee, and we'll be done before the coffee gets cold. Zoom works if you need it, but if we do that, I'd like our next one to be in person.

Let me confirm your email so I can send the invite. And if anything changes, just text me, even if we need to push it a few minutes.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Can we do it next week?": say "I can, but the sooner we sit down, the sooner you'll see homes that fit. I have [today] at [time] or tomorrow at [time]. Which is easier?"
- **If they say** "Do we have to meet? Can we do it by text?": say "We can text for quick things, but it goes much faster when we talk it through. It's a short call. What time works?"

## Voicemail and Text Scripts for Buyer Leads

Most first calls go to voicemail, so keep a short, curious version ready that does not pitch. Then send a text right away.

<ScriptExample label="Buyer Lead Voicemail">
Hey [Name], this is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. You were checking out homes in [Area] and I didn't want you to miss anything that fits what you're looking for. I'll send you a quick text as well. Talk soon.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Text After a Missed Call">
Hey [Name], it's [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I just tried you about the homes you were viewing in [Area]. Are you looking to move soon or just exploring options?
</ScriptExample>

## After the Call: How Often to Follow Up by Buyer Temperature

What the buyer told you on the first call decides how often you reach out. A simple three-level rhythm keeps your time on the buyers who are ready.

| Temperature | What you heard | How often to reach out |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Hot | Clear plan to buy within about 60 days: a lease ending, a move-in date, a lender conversation, active touring | At least once a week, and twice a week if the market is tight |
| Warm | A plan to buy in about 3 to 12 months, but not urgent: saving more, waiting for a bonus, a lease ending next spring | At least once a month |
| Cold | 12 months or more away, or no timeline: "maybe next year," "just browsing" | At least once a quarter |

These are working targets rather than rules, so adjust them to your market. Each contact should add something useful, such as a new listing that fits, a change in rates, or an update on their target neighborhood. Move a buyer up when they get pre-approved, start touring, or mention a firm date, and move them down when they go quiet for weeks. ## Mistakes That Kill Buyer Calls

The most common mistake is sending homes before the buyer's motivation and timeline are clear. It feels helpful, but it hands the conversation to a search portal and gives you nothing to follow up on.

Another is asking every question on the first call. The first call is for four or five questions and a booked meeting, and the rest belongs at the consultation. If you ask everything now, the meeting has nothing left to cover.

A third is letting the buyer set the meeting for next week. Meetings booked far out are far more likely to be rescheduled or cancelled, so offer today or tomorrow whenever you can.

The last is touring with a buyer you barely know and no agreement in place. Showing a few homes early to build rapport can be fine, but touring without structure stretches the process and rarely converts. A buyer consultation and a signed agreement turn a lead into a client.

## FAQ

**What is a good script for a buyer lead?**
Open with your name, your brokerage and the reason for your call, ask which area the buyer is interested in, ask why they are moving now, and book a 20 to 30 minute consultation for today or tomorrow. Adjust the wording to match how you talk.

**What do you say when a buyer asks you to send listings?**
Say you are happy to send homes, then ask what is behind the move so you can send the right ones. Move to a short meeting where you map out what they want, and send homes once the motivation, timeline and next step are clear.

**Do I need a signed agreement before showing a home to a buyer?**
Since August 17, 2024, NAR's practice changes require MLS participants working with buyers to enter a written agreement before touring a home, including a live virtual tour, according to [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101). Your brokerage has its own form and your state may add rules, so check with your broker.

**How soon should you call a new buyer lead?**
As soon as you reasonably can after the inquiry. The buyer is more likely to remember asking and to pick up, and there is a better chance they have not yet spoken to another agent.
