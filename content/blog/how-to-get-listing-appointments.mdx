To get more listing appointments, spend more of your calling time on the homeowners most likely to be thinking about a sale, ask for a short meeting while you have them on the line, and keep following up with the ones who say not yet. Sellers tend to hire people they already know: NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers reports that [66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with in the past](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers). That puts your past clients and sphere at the top of the list, followed by homeowners who have already given you a reason to call.

This guide covers where listing appointments come from, the call that turns a conversation into a booked meeting, what to say when a homeowner pushes back, how to follow up on a not-yet, and the simple math that shows which step to fix first. Once the meeting is booked, the [listing appointment checklist](/blog/listing-appointment-checklist/) covers how to prepare for it and the [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) guide covers what to ask.

## Where Listing Appointments Come From

Almost every listing appointment starts with a call or a conversation with someone who already had a reason to talk. The table below sorts the usual sources from warmest to coolest, with the guide to use for each one.

| Source | Who you are calling | Why they may take the call | Where to go next |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Past clients and sphere | People who already know you | They may be thinking about a move, and sellers often choose someone familiar | [What to say to old leads](/blog/what-to-say-to-old-leads/) |
| Home value requests | People who asked what their home is worth | They raised their hand | The script below |
| Leads who went quiet | Earlier sellers who said "not yet" | Their timeline may have changed | [Follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/) |
| Expired listings | Owners whose listing ended without a sale | They already tried to sell | [Expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) |
| For sale by owner | Owners selling without an agent | They want to sell | [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/) |
| Neighbors of a recent sale | Homeowners near a listing or sale | A nearby sale gives you a reason to call | [Circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) |

Start at the top, since people who already know you have the most reason to take the call. The same ask works on every row: a short meeting where you give a realistic number.

## The Call That Books the Listing Appointment

A listing appointment call has four moves. Give a reason for calling that is specific to the homeowner, find out why they are thinking about selling and when, explain what the meeting gives them, and offer two times. The home value request call below shows all four. It works for past contacts who asked about their value, and the same bones work on most seller calls.

<ScriptExample label="Home Value Request Call: From Conversation to Appointment">
Hi, is this [Name]? Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. It looks like you requested a home value on your property in [Month / Year], and I was going through our records to make sure we did not miss you. Quick question: did you end up doing anything with the property, or are you still there?

(If they still own it) Got it. Out of curiosity, what had you thinking about the value back then? (Pause and let them talk.)

(If they are thinking about selling) Just to see whether I can help, mind if I ask a few quick questions? What is making you consider a move? If you did sell, when would you ideally want to be out? Are you already talking with an agent, or just gathering information? Roughly how long have you owned the home, and do you have a sense of what you still owe on it?

Based on what you are describing, the online estimates are probably off. What I normally do is stop by for 20 minutes, look at the condition, upgrades and layout, and give you a realistic range. That way you are deciding based on facts instead of guesses. Would it make sense to do that in the next couple of days?

Would mornings or afternoons work better? And is anyone else involved in the decision who should be there?
</ScriptExample>

Notice that the meeting is described in terms of what the homeowner receives (a realistic number) and how little time it takes (20 minutes). The ask offers a choice between times, so the homeowner is deciding when to meet instead of whether to. If your schedule allows it, offer today or tomorrow. The further out the meeting, the more time there is for plans to change or for another agent to call first.

### What they say next

- **"I was just curious."** Say: "Totally fair. Most people who check their value are thinking about selling, thinking about refinancing, or wondering whether they have built equity. Which one sounds like you?" Then follow the branch they choose.
- **"I don't remember requesting that."** Say: "No worries, it was a while back. You still own [Address], right? If you woke up tomorrow and someone offered you a strong price for it, would you even consider moving?" A yes moves you into the questions above. A no gives you an opening for "Have you checked what homes around you are actually selling for lately?"
- **"We might sell in a few months."** Say: "That helps. What is driving that timeline? If you are a few months out, this is a good time to look at it, because decisions about condition and preparation now can change what you net later. Let's spend 20 minutes walking through it so you can plan."
- **"Can you just tell me a number now?"** Say: "I can give you a ballpark from the data, but condition and upgrades move the number, and I would rather give you one you can plan around. Twenty minutes in the home will do that."
- **"We're refinancing, not selling."** Say: "When you are refinancing, the number matters a lot. Have you gotten a specific value from anyone, or just online estimates? I can come by and give you a realistic range so you know where you stand before the bank locks anything in."

## When a Homeowner Says "I'm Not Interested"

Many listing calls hit this reply somewhere, and it often means "not today" or "not from a stranger." Treat it as a request for a reason to keep talking. Acknowledge it, ask one curious question tied to something you know about their home or situation, and offer the smallest next step. Our page on handling [the "not interested" objection](/objections/not-interested/) has more wording.

<ScriptExample label="Turning Not Interested Into a Short Meeting">
That's fair, and I will not take much of your time. I was calling because homes on [Street] have been selling and I wanted to see whether anything is on your mind about your own plans in the next year or so. Is moving something you have thought about at all, or is it completely off the table?
</ScriptExample>

If they answer "completely off the table," thank them, ask whether you may send a market update once or twice a year, and log the answer. If they answer "maybe someday," ask what would have to be true for it to happen, and treat the answer as the start of a timeline.

Keeping the right response in front of you matters most when a homeowner pushes back in the middle of your ask. ## Follow Up on the Not-Yet Sellers

Some homeowners who say not yet are planning to sell later. The follow-up call should reference what they told you, give one reason that is new, and ask for the same short meeting again. Use their own timeline to set how often you call: weekly for someone selling within about 60 days, monthly for someone three to twelve months out, and quarterly for everyone further away. The full cadence is in our guide on [how often to follow up with real estate leads](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/).

<ScriptExample label="Follow-Up Call to a Seller Who Was Not Ready">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I am calling you back like I said I would. When we last spoke, you were planning to be in [Place] by [Date]. Are those still the plans?

I was thinking about you because something changed: [one real change, such as a sale on their street or a shift in inventory nearby]. I am curious whether you still want to be moved by [Date], or whether you were planning to start the process closer to then.

To get you one step closer, all we need is a quick 15-minute strategy session so I can help you map this out on the timeline that works for you. Sound fair? What works better, [Day] or [Day]?
</ScriptExample>

The reason for the call should always be real. A recent sale nearby, a change in homes for sale on their street, or an answer to a question they asked last time all qualify. "Just checking in" does not.

## Know Your Numbers: How Many Listing Appointments Does It Take?

The number of appointments it takes to get a listing depends on your own signing rate, so the number worth knowing is yours. Divide the listings you signed by the appointments you held over the last 90 days. As an illustration of the arithmetic, if you sign 4 of every 10 appointments, you need 2.5 appointments per listing, so signing 3 listings a month takes about 8 appointments, or 2 a week. Those figures are placeholders, not benchmarks. Numbers quoted on other sites vary widely and rarely explain how they were measured, so this guide does not borrow one.

Track five counts each week. The step with the weakest result tells you what to fix.

| Step | What to count | What a weak number usually points to |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Dials to conversations | Calls where you spoke with the homeowner | The list you are working or the times you call |
| Conversations to appointments set | Meetings booked after a real conversation | What you say on the call, especially the ask |
| Appointments set to appointments held | Meetings that actually happen | Confirmation. See [how to book appointments](/blog/how-to-book-appointments-real-estate/) for a confirmation sequence |
| Appointments held to listings signed | Listing agreements signed | Preparation and the presentation. Start with the [listing appointment checklist](/blog/listing-appointment-checklist/) |
| Listings signed | Your output for the week | The result of the four steps above |

Enter your own rates in the [prospecting calculator](/tools/prospecting-calculator/) to see how many dials, conversations and appointments your goal requires. The conversation-to-appointment step is the one you control most directly, because it depends on the words you use in the first few minutes. A few percentage points there reduce the number of calls you need to make for the same goal.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Offer the meeting as a way for the homeowner to get something, such as a realistic price range or a plan, rather than as a favor to you. "20 minutes to see what your home would sell for" gives the homeowner a clear reason to say yes, which "a listing presentation" does not.
</CalloutBox>

## FAQ

**How can realtors get more listings?**
The most reliable sources are past clients and sphere, homeowners who have asked about their home's value, expired and FSBO owners, and neighbors of recent sales. Call each group with a specific reason and end every conversation with a request for a short meeting. The [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/) is where those conversations turn into signed listings.

**How do I win a listing appointment once I have it?**
Preparation and good questions do most of the work. Bring comparable sales and a net sheet, ask about the seller's goals and timeline before you talk about price, and set a specific next step before you leave. The [listing appointment checklist](/blog/listing-appointment-checklist/) covers what to do before, during and after.

**How many listing appointments does it take to get a listing?**
Divide the listings you signed by the appointments you held over the last 90 days to get your own rate. The number changes with your lead sources, your preparation and your market, so track yours instead of using a number from someone else's business.

**Should I give a home value on the call?**
Give a ballpark only if the homeowner needs one to agree to the meeting, and explain that condition and upgrades can move the number. A 20-minute visit lets you give a range you are willing to stand behind.
