To prepare for a listing appointment, confirm the meeting and ask your key questions on a short call, research the property and pull comparable sales, build a net sheet and a pre-listing package, pack your materials, and decide what next step you will ask for before you arrive. At the appointment you learn the seller's goals, walk the home, present your pricing and marketing plan, and ask for a decision. Afterward you send a recap the same day or the next, record what you learned, and call when you said you would.

This guide puts those steps in order, with a printable checklist at the end, and answers the questions agents search most: what to bring, what happens at a listing appointment, how long it takes, and what changes when the appointment is a call instead of a visit. If you are still working on getting the meeting booked, read [how to get more listing appointments](/blog/how-to-get-listing-appointments/) and our [guide to booking appointments](/blog/how-to-book-appointments-real-estate/) first. The questions to ask on the confirmation call and at the table are in our [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) guide, so this one focuses on the preparation around them.

## What Happens at a Listing Appointment

A [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/) is the meeting where a seller decides whether to hire you. People also search for a pre-listing appointment, which generally means the same meeting, held before the seller signs a listing agreement. The material you send ahead of it is often called a pre-listing package or packet.

Tom Ferry's guide to the meeting describes five stages, which is a sensible order to plan around: a welcome and agenda, discovery and a tour of the home, your presentation, questions from the seller, and agreement and next steps. It also puts the whole meeting at [45 to 90 minutes](https://www.tomferry.com/blog/what-happens-at-a-listing-appointment/), depending on the property and how much the seller wants to discuss. Tell the seller how long to expect when you confirm, so they set aside the time and everyone who needs to be there can attend.

| Stage | What happens | What you prepared for it |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Welcome and agenda | You set the plan for the meeting and confirm the goals | A one-line agenda and your confirmation call notes |
| Discovery and tour | You learn the seller's situation and walk the home | Your questions, property research, a way to take notes |
| Presentation | You show comparable sales, a pricing range and your marketing plan | Comparable sales, net sheet, marketing plan |
| Seller questions | You answer pricing, timing and fee questions | Your answers to the questions sellers always ask |
| Next steps | You ask for a decision or agree on a specific follow-up | Listing agreement, disclosures, a follow-up date |

## Before the Appointment: How to Prepare for a Listing Appointment

Preparation works best in a sequence. Spread over two days, none of it is long, and the order matters because the answers from one step shape the next.

### Two Days Out: Confirm and Ask the Key Questions

Call the seller to confirm the day, time and address, and use the same call to learn what you cannot get from records: why they are moving, how soon, what they hope to list for, whether anyone else will weigh in, and whether they are talking to other agents. Ask who is on the title of the home as well, because, depending on your state, everyone on the title may need to sign the listing agreement.

The logistics at the end of this call are easy to skip and they protect the appointment. Mention the package you will send, ask whether everyone involved in the decision will be there, and say how long you expect to need.

<ScriptExample label="Confirmation Call: Logistics and Pre-Listing Package">
I will be sending over a package of information for you to review before I arrive. Will you take a few minutes to look through it?

Will all decision makers be present when I arrive?

Do you have any questions for me before I come out?

I expect our meeting to take between [X] and [Y] minutes. Does that work for you?

Perfect. I look forward to seeing you on [Day] at [Time].
</ScriptExample>

### Research the Property

Pull the public record and tax history, any prior MLS listings and how long they stayed on the market, and the HOA rules if there is an association. If the home was listed before, read the history for price changes and days on market, because the seller will expect you to have a view on why it did not sell. Our guide to [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) shows how to read that history.

### Build the Pricing Picture

Gather recent comparable sales, the active listings that will compete with this home, pending sales, and expired or withdrawn listings nearby. Note days on market and price changes, because those show how buyers are responding at each price point. Then settle on how you will present the number. Some agents give a range with reasons and narrow it after seeing the home. Others hold the number until after they have done more research. Either approach works when you decide it in advance and explain it to the seller.

Build a net sheet from your price range and what you learned about their mortgage balance. A net sheet shows the seller what they would walk away with after costs, which turns an abstract price into a number they can plan around.

### Assemble the Pre-Listing Package

Tom Ferry's guide describes a [pre-listing packet](https://www.tomferry.com/blog/listing-appointment-checklist/) as the trust-building material you send before the meeting, as distinct from the fuller presentation you deliver at it, and suggests six parts: your short bio and local experience, an overview of your selling process, recent neighborhood activity, questions for the seller to consider, examples of your marketing and past results, and an agenda. Send it the day before so the seller arrives at the meeting already knowing how you work.

### Practice Out Loud

Rehearse your opening, your pricing explanation and your answers to the fee question, ideally with a colleague or your broker playing the seller. Our guide on [how to practice real estate scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) shows how to make that practice useful.

### What to Bring to a Listing Appointment

| Item | Why it matters |
| ----- | ----- |
| Comparable sales, printed and on a device | The seller can see how you reached your range |
| Net sheet | Shows what they keep at your recommended price |
| Listing agreement and required disclosures, printed and digital | You can sign at the table if the seller is ready |
| Marketing plan and examples of your work | Makes your plan concrete |
| A copy of the pre-listing package | Everyone in the room has the same pages |
| Your question list and a notepad | You ask everything you planned and record the answers |
| A charged tablet or laptop and a charger | Your presentation and records stay available |
| Pens, clipboard and business cards | Small items that are awkward to be missing |
| Your phone for photos, with the seller's permission | Gives you the condition and details to build the CMA afterward |

## During the Appointment

Arrive a few minutes early so you are not rushed, and open by setting the agenda. Telling the seller what to expect puts you in the lead without being forceful.

<ScriptExample label="Setting the Agenda">
Thanks for having me. Here is how I would like to use our time. First I would like to hear what you are hoping to accomplish, then walk through the home with you. After that I will show you what has been selling nearby, where I think your home fits, and how I would market it. We will finish with your questions and agree on next steps. Does that work?
</ScriptExample>

Then work through the stages in the table above. A few habits help throughout:

- Ask your goals question before you walk the home, so your comments about the property connect to what the seller cares about.
- Take notes as the seller talks, because the details they mention (a deadline, a worry about showings, a disagreement with a spouse) shape your plan.
- Confirm who else is involved in the decision before you present, so you are not presenting to half the decision makers.
- Show your range and your reasoning together, then ask what the seller thinks before you move on.
- Before you leave, ask for the decision or agree on a specific follow-up date and time.

## After the Appointment

The follow-up is part of the appointment. Send a short recap the same day or the next, with what you covered, the net sheet and any information you promised. Record what the seller said about timing, price and concerns in your CRM, along with the date you promised to call. Then call when you said you would.

<ScriptExample label="Recap Email After the Appointment">
Hi [Name], thank you for having me out today. Here is a summary of what we covered: your goal of [goal] by [date], the pricing range of [range] and why, and the plan for marketing the home. I attached the net sheet and the comparable sales we discussed. You asked about [question], and the answer is [answer]. I will call you on [Day] at [Time] to answer anything that has come up. If you would like to move ahead sooner, I can send the listing agreement tonight.
</ScriptExample>

If the seller did not sign, find out what is holding them back and answer it, and keep the follow-up tied to something specific, such as a net sheet update or a recent sale. The scripts for that conversation are in the [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) guide.

## If It Is Your First Listing Appointment

A first appointment goes better with a plan you have already spoken out loud. Rehearse the opening and the pricing conversation with someone before you go, bring a printed agenda so the structure does not depend on memory, and ask your broker or team leader to review your comparable sales and pricing range ahead of time. Prepare your answer to "What is your experience?" honestly and focus on what you will do for this seller.

## When the Listing Appointment Is Held Remotely

Some sellers, especially those who live out of town, want to meet by phone or video first. The structure is the same, but the preparation changes:

1. Send the pre-listing package and comparable sales ahead of time, and share your screen during the call so you can walk through them.
2. Ask the seller to send photos or a short walkthrough video of the home beforehand, since you cannot see the condition yourself.
3. Give a range instead of a single number, and say plainly that a visit will firm it up.
4. Confirm every decision maker is on the call, and send the listing agreement electronically afterward if they are ready.

When the meeting is remote, you cannot slide a printed market report across the table, so market numbers need to be in front of you. Some agents keep local data on screen during seller calls with a tool like [Sayso's Pulse](/products/pulse/), which shows live local market data during the call.

## The Listing Appointment Checklist

Copy this list into your notes or print it for each appointment.

<ScriptSheet title="Listing appointment checklist">

**Two days out**
- Confirm day, time, address and who will attend
- Ask motivation, timeline, price hopes and whether they are talking to other agents
- Confirm who is on the title
- Mention the pre-listing package and the length of the meeting

**Research**
- Public records and tax history
- Prior MLS listings, days on market, price changes
- HOA rules, if any
- Comparable sales, active competition, pending sales, expired and withdrawn listings

**Prepare**
- Decide how you will present the price (range or single number)
- Build the net sheet
- Assemble and send the pre-listing package the day before
- Rehearse the opening and the pricing conversation out loud

**Pack**
- Comparable sales, net sheet, marketing plan, package copies
- Listing agreement and disclosures, printed and digital
- Question list, notepad, pens, clipboard, business cards
- Charged device and charger

**At the appointment**
- Set the agenda and confirm the goals
- Walk the home and take notes
- Confirm all decision makers are involved
- Present range and reasoning, then ask the seller's view
- Answer questions on price, timing and fees
- Ask for the decision or set a specific follow-up time

**After**
- Send the recap and net sheet the same day or next day
- Record timing, price and concerns in your CRM
- Call on the date you promised
- Write down what worked and what to change

</ScriptSheet>

## FAQ

**What should I bring to a listing appointment?**
Bring comparable sales, a net sheet, your marketing plan, the listing agreement and disclosures, a copy of your pre-listing package, your question list, and a charged device. The table above explains why each item matters.

**How long does a listing appointment take?**
Tom Ferry's published guidance says 45 to 90 minutes, depending on the property and the seller's questions. Tell the seller what to expect on the confirmation call so they plan for it.

**What does pre-listing mean?**
It refers to the work done before a seller signs a listing agreement, which includes the pre-listing appointment itself and the package you send ahead of it. The package is meant to build trust before you meet.

**What do I do if the seller wants to interview more than one agent?**
Ask what matters most to them in the agent they choose, prepare to show how you meet those points, and set a follow-up date before you leave. The [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) guide has a script for this situation.
