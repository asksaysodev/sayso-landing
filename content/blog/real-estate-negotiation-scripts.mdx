A good real estate negotiation script does three things: it asks what matters to the other person besides the number, it ties your position to facts both sides can see, and it offers a trade in place of a concession. When a seller, buyer or other agent pushes on price, commission or terms, the first line to reach for is a question: "Besides the number, what matters most to you here?"

This guide gives you word-for-word scripts for the conversations where negotiation happens: the compensation conversation with a buyer, a seller who asks you to lower your fee, a seller who wants to list high, a low offer that just came in, a buyer who wants to offer well under asking, and an inspection request. Each script comes with the replies you are likely to hear next. They are written for both sides of the transaction and work in any market.

## The Three Moves Behind Every Negotiation Script

Most negotiation conversations go wrong in the first ten seconds, when the agent defends a position before understanding why the other person holds theirs. The scripts below follow the same three moves.

1. **Find out what matters.** The number someone says out loud often stands in for something else: certainty, speed, fairness, or a goal they have not told you yet. Ask before you answer.
2. **Anchor to facts.** Recent sales, the written agreement, the inspection report and the timeline are things both sides can look at together. Facts take the conversation off the question of whose opinion is right.
3. **Trade, do not concede.** When you move on something, ask for something in return, or move on a different lever such as timing or terms. This keeps the other side from learning that pushing works.

These are the same ideas behind our [objection handling framework](/blog/real-estate-objection-handling/), which uses acknowledge, redirect and advance on every pushback.

## Commission and Compensation Conversations

Keep these conversations about your own arrangement and the value behind it. This guide does not quote typical commission rates, since what you charge and what is agreed in your market is a matter for you, your brokerage and your state's rules.

### The Compensation Conversation With a Buyer

Under the NAR settlement practice changes that took effect August 17, 2024, agents who are MLS participants may not work with buyers before touring homes without a written agreement, and offers of compensation can no longer appear on the MLS. That puts a compensation conversation at the front of many buyer relationships, often at the front door of the first showing. Check your local MLS and brokerage rules for the exact requirements where you work.

<ScriptExample label="The compensation conversation at the first showing">
Before we head in, there's one thing I need to go over so everything is clear. I need a signed agreement before we tour. It lists the address we're touring, today's date, and how my brokerage would be paid if you end up buying. Has anyone walked you through one of these before?

Take a look. If you have questions about any of it, ask me now and we'll go through it together.
</ScriptExample>

If the agreement you are using is limited to a single showing and does not commit the buyer long term, you can add: "This one only covers this home today. It doesn't lock you into working with me." Only say that if it is true of the document you are handing over.

- **If they say** "How much is that?": say "It's written right here so nothing is a surprise." Point to the line. "Compensation isn't set by law, so if something in it doesn't feel right, tell me and we'll talk it through." Say that only if you are willing to have the conversation.
- **If they say** "I'd rather not sign anything": say "Totally fair. The practice rules for agents who use the MLS require a written agreement before we tour, so I'm not able to skip this step. Take as long as you need to read it."
- **If they say** "Another agent said they would do it for less": say "That's good to know. What did they say they'd do for you in return? I'd rather compare what each of us does than only the number. Here's what I would do for you: [list the three or four things that matter most to this buyer]."

### The Fee Question From a Seller

When a seller asks you to lower your fee, answer with a question before you answer with a number. The reply you get shows you what the seller is really comparing.

<ScriptExample label="Seller asks, 'Can you do it for less?'">
That's a fair question, and I'd rather talk about it openly. Can I ask what you're comparing it to?

(Listen.)

That helps. What do you want most from the agent you hire? Let's make a short list, and then we can look at how the fee fits the plan.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "A friend paid less": say "Good to know. Did they get the same things you're asking me for, like pricing guidance, marketing and negotiating the offers? Let's put the two side by side."
- **If they say** "I just want to keep more of the proceeds": say "That makes sense. Let's look at your net sheet together. What you keep depends on the sale price and the costs, and my fee is one piece of that."
- **If they say** "Everyone else is willing to negotiate": say "I'm willing to talk about it too. If we change something about the fee, I'd also want to look at what the plan includes, so we're both getting what we need."

If your brokerage allows you to adjust your fee, decide ahead of time what you would want in return, such as a shorter agreement, a firm list-price plan or an agreed marketing scope. ## Price Conversations

### Set the Price Frame Before You Present

The easiest price negotiation to win is the one you start before the listing appointment. On the confirmation call, ask the seller what they hope to list for, then put the goal on the table so you are working toward the same outcome.

<ScriptExample label="Pre-appointment price frame">
When we meet, how much are you hoping to list the home for, realistically?

(Listen.)

I study homes and pricing every day, and my goal is to recommend the price that gets your home the most exposure and ultimately sells. That makes sense, right?

With that in mind, what's the lowest price you would consider?

(Listen.) And do you have a sense of what you still owe on the property?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I won't take a penny less than [number]": say "Thanks for being straight with me. What's behind that number? I'll look at the comps with it in mind and show you how the market sees it when we meet."
- **If they say** "Another agent said it's worth more": say "I'd like to see how they got there. When we sit down, let's walk through the recent sales together so you can judge for yourself."

The questions on this call come from the [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) and give you the numbers you need for the conversation. If the seller still wants to list above the comps once you sit down, our guide to the [price too high objection](/blog/price-too-high-objection/) walks through how to hold the line, and the [price too high](/objections/price-too-high/) page has the quick version.

### The Low Offer Call (Seller Side)

When an offer comes in lower than the seller hoped, call before you send anything in writing. The first reaction is usually emotional, and your job is to move the conversation from the headline number to the full picture.

<ScriptExample label="Low offer call to the seller">
Hi [Name], we received an offer on [Address]. I want to walk you through it before anyone reacts. It's at [price], with a closing date of [date] and these conditions: [list them]. Let me tell you what I see in it, and then we'll decide on a response together.

An offer has three parts: the price, the terms and the timing. If you could improve one, which would matter most to you?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "That's insulting, reject it": say "I understand why it feels that way. A reply keeps the buyer talking, and a flat rejection can end the conversation. Let's decide what you would need to see to say yes, and counter with that."
- **If they say** "Just accept it, I'm tired of this": say "I hear you. Before you decide, let me show you how it compares to the recent sales nearby, so whatever you choose, you're choosing with the full picture."
- **If they say** "Counter at full asking": say "That's an option. Let's talk about what it could mean, since the buyer may come back, or may walk. Which terms would you trade for a higher price?"

### The Under-Asking Conversation (Buyer Side)

A buyer who wants to offer well below the list price is often reacting to fear of overpaying. Take that seriously, and show them what the comps say.

<ScriptExample label="Buyer wants to offer well under asking">
I can write whatever you decide, and I want you to see the whole picture first. Would it help if I pulled the comps and showed you what similar homes sold for and how long they took? Then we can pick a number you feel good about.

Besides the price, what matters most to you about this offer? The closing date, the inspection, when you can move in?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I don't want to overpay": say "That's reasonable. The goal is a price that holds up against the comps. Let's look at how this one compares."
- **If they say** "The seller can just say no": say "They can. If they do, how would you feel about walking away versus coming up? Let's decide that before we write it, so you're not deciding under pressure."
- **If they say** "Just write it, I'll handle it": say "I will. I want you to know the risks and the options first, so you can make the call knowing them. [Walk through the comps.]"

Buyers are also better prepared for any of these conversations when financing is settled early. A simple opener is, "Have you had the opportunity to speak with a lender yet, or is that something I can help you with?" If they have not, explain that getting financing sorted before they find the right home keeps them from losing time or the house. ## Terms: Where Many Negotiations Are Decided

Price gets the attention, but terms often decide whether both sides feel the arrangement is fair. Terms are also the easiest way to trade, because something that costs one side little can be worth a lot to the other. What you can offer depends on your contract forms, your market and your broker, so check before promising anything.

| Lever | Why a buyer may care | Why a seller may care |
|---|---|---|
| Closing date | Lines up with a lease or a current home sale | Fits a move or a purchase of their own |
| Possession or rent-back | Time to move in once they close | Extra time to move out |
| Inspection response | Repairs, a credit or a price adjustment | A defined list instead of an open-ended request |
| Contingency timelines | More time to line up financing or a sale | Less time before the buyer can walk away |
| Included items | Appliances, window coverings and fixtures | Items they plan to take with them |

### The Inspection Request

The inspection report is where a calm conversation can turn tense. Sort the findings before anyone responds.

<ScriptExample label="Inspection request call to the seller">
Hi [Name], the buyer's inspection came back and they've sent a request. Before we react, let me walk you through it. I've sorted the items into three groups: safety or structural, minor repairs, and cosmetic. Which of those matter most to you?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I'm not fixing anything": say "Understood. Would you consider a credit or a price adjustment on the larger items if the buyer drops the smaller ones? That keeps the sale moving without you managing repairs."
- **If they say** "That's more than I expected": say "I know it feels like a lot. Let's look at each item and decide which ones have a real cost. Then we'll respond to the buyer with a clear proposal."

<ScriptExample label="Inspection findings call to the buyer">
Hi [Name], the inspection is back. I'd like to go through it with you so we decide what to ask for. Which items worry you most? We'll start with those and decide whether a repair, a credit or a price adjustment fits best.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I want everything fixed": say "I understand. Sellers tend to respond better to a focused request, so let's pick the items that matter most and ask for those. Which three would you not want to live without?"

## Mistakes That Weaken a Negotiation

- **Answering the number before asking what is behind it.** The first number is often a position, and the reason behind it is where you find room to trade.
- **Conceding without asking for anything.** If you move on price, ask for a firmer timeline, a cleaner contract or a shorter response window.
- **Arguing opinion against opinion.** Use the comps, the agreement or the report, and look at them with the other person.
- **Reacting on the first call.** Deliver offers and inspection requests with a plan for the response, and wait for the emotion to settle.
- **Making promises about the contract you cannot back up.** Terms depend on your forms, your market and your broker.
- **Quoting what is "typical" for commission.** Talk about your own arrangement and what it includes, and let the client compare.
