The best way to ask for a real estate referral is to make it the second half of a check-in call. Start by asking how life is going in the home you helped them buy or sell, listen for a minute, and then ask one specific question: "Who do you know who has mentioned a move?" A call that opens with the ask sounds like a favor request, while a call that earns the ask sounds like a conversation between two people who already like each other.

This guide gives you word-for-word scripts for past clients and for people in your sphere who were never clients, along with what to say when they answer "I'll keep you in mind," a text and voicemail for each, and a contact rhythm so the ask never shows up out of nowhere. Every script works whether the person you are asking might refer a buyer or a seller.

## What a Referral Call Is Actually For

The goal of a referral call is to put a specific person in the other party's mind, and a name in your notes. Asking "do you know anyone looking to buy or sell?" asks someone to scan their whole life for an answer, and most people say no because nothing comes to mind in the moment. A better ask gives them a smaller thing to search for, such as someone who recently mentioned a new job in another city, someone with a baby on the way, or a neighbor who talks about downsizing.

Your [sphere of influence](/glossary/sphere-of-influence/) is the group of people who already know and trust you: past clients, friends, family, neighbors and professional contacts. These calls belong in the same weekly block as the rest of your [follow up](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-scripts/), and they work best when the person has heard from you without an ask a few times first.

## Script 1: The Past Client Check-In With a Referral Ask

Use this for anyone you helped buy or sell. A natural time is six to twelve months after closing, then again each year around the anniversary of the move.

<ScriptExample label="Past client check-in that ends with a referral ask">
Hey [Name], it's [Your Name]. I'm not calling about anything in particular. It's been about [a year] since you moved into [Street], and I wanted to see how the house is treating you.

(Listen. Ask one follow-up about something they mention.)

Anything you'd change now that you've lived there a while?

(Listen again.)

I'm glad to hear it. Can I ask you something? I'm always glad to meet the people my clients know. Who do you know, at work, in the neighborhood or in your family, who has mentioned a move in the next year or so, even something vague?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Not really, nobody comes to mind": say "That's completely fine, people rarely have a name ready on the spot. If you hear someone talk about a new job, a baby on the way or kids heading off to school, would you mind passing along my number?"
- **If they say** "My brother mentioned wanting to move": say "That's great to hear. What has he said so far?" After they answer: "Would you rather introduce us by text, or should I reach out and mention your name?"
- **If they say** "Sure, I'll keep you in mind": say "Thank you, I appreciate that. It doesn't have to be a big question. If someone asks what their house is worth or whether it's a good time to move, send them my way."
- **If they say** "We've actually been thinking about moving ourselves": treat it as a new lead. Ask what is driving the move and when they would want to be settled, and use the questions in our guide to [talking to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/).
- **If they say** something negative about how the transaction went: do not ask for the referral on this call. Say "Thank you for telling me. What happened?", listen, and offer to make it right. A referral ask after an unresolved complaint costs you the relationship.

Only use the line "I'm always glad to meet the people my clients know" if it is true for you. If most of your business already comes from referrals, say that instead: "A lot of my business comes from people my clients pass my name to." A statement that is true for you will always sound better than a borrowed one.

## Script 2: The Thank-You Call After Closing

Clients often feel the most gratitude in the weeks right after closing, which makes it an easy moment to plant the idea without making it the point of the call.

<ScriptExample label="Post-closing thank-you call">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I wanted to check that the move-in is going okay and thank you again for trusting me with it. I really enjoyed working with you.

(Listen.)

One thing I wanted to mention. As you tell people about the move, if anyone asks who helped you, I'd be glad if you passed my name along. I'll send you a quick text with my contact info so it's easy to forward. Would that be okay?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Of course, happy to": say "Thank you. And if anything about the house comes up in the first few months, call me, even if it has nothing to do with real estate."
- **If they say** "We'll probably tell people": say "That means a lot. I'll send that text now so it's in your phone."

Send the text while you are still on the call, or right after: "Thanks again, [Name]. Here's my contact info in case anyone asks who you worked with: [Name, Number, Email]."

## Script 3: The Sphere of Influence Call (Friends, Neighbors and Contacts Who Were Never Clients)

People who have never hired you need a reason to think of you. Start with an interest in their life and keep the real estate part short.

<ScriptExample label="Sphere of influence call">
Hey [Name], it's [Your Name]. Quick one, and I'll be fast. I'm making a point this year to stay in touch with people I like and haven't talked to in a while, and you were on my list. What's new with you?

(Listen for a life event: a job, a baby, a move, a parent downsizing.)

That's great. Real estate wise, I'm taking on [a few more clients] this year. If you hear anyone talking about buying or selling, even in passing, I'd be happy to talk with them for fifteen minutes with no strings attached.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I don't know anyone right now": say "No problem, I'm not fishing. What about you? Any plans for your own place in the next couple of years?" This often turns the contact into a lead.
- **If they say** "I'd feel weird recommending a friend": say "I get that. You don't have to recommend anything. If they mention it, you can just say 'I know someone you can talk to,' and I'll take it from there."
- **If they say** "Are you doing this full time?" or asks about your business: answer plainly and briefly, then return to them. "Yes, I work with [buyers and sellers] in [Area]. How's your family doing?"

## Script 4: Give First, Then Mention Real Estate

Some contacts, especially small business owners, respond better when you offer to help them first. This version suits people who own a business, because a mention from you is something they can use.

<ScriptExample label="Give-first call for a sphere contact who owns a business">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I was thinking about you because a couple of the people I work with have been asking me for a good [their trade]. How is business going this season?

(Listen. Note what kind of customer they want.)

I'd be glad to send people your way when it fits. And if anyone you work with ever mentions a move, I'd appreciate you doing the same.
</ScriptExample>

Say the opening line only if it is true. If nobody has asked you, simply ask how business is going and offer to send people their way when it fits. Recommend them only if you would genuinely stand behind them, because a recommendation that goes badly weakens the relationship you were trying to build.

## Ask With a Life Event, Not a General Question

A specific prompt gives people something to remember. Pick the one or two that fit the person you are calling.

| Prompt | What to say | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| New job or relocation | "Does anyone you know have a new job that might mean a move?" | Buyers and sellers |
| New baby or growing family | "Anyone you know who is outgrowing their space?" | Mostly buyers |
| Kids leaving for school | "Anyone you know whose kids are leaving, and who is thinking about a smaller place?" | Mostly sellers |
| Parent downsizing or inheriting a home | "Do you know anyone who is helping a parent with a house?" | Sellers, sometimes both |
| Renters tired of renting | "Do you know anyone who is renting and curious what buying would look like?" | Buyers |
| Landlords | "Anyone you know who owns a rental and has thought about selling it?" | Sellers |

Avoid prompts that touch on a divorce or loss of a family member unless the person raises it first. Listen for it instead.

## When You Get a Name: Turning It Into an Introduction

A name only helps if the person on the other end expects to hear from you. Ask the referrer which way they prefer.

<ScriptExample label="Text to the person who gave you the name">
Thank you for thinking of [Friend]. Would it be easier if you introduced us in a group text, or would you like me to reach out directly and mention your name? Either is fine with me.
</ScriptExample>

When the referrer is happy for you to reach out, the first message to the new contact should be short and carry the referrer's name in the first sentence.

<ScriptExample label="First text to the referred person">
Hi [Friend], this is [Your Name]. [Referrer] mentioned you've been thinking about [a move] and thought we should talk. No pressure at all. I'm happy to answer questions. Would a quick call this week work?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Sure, call me tonight": say "Great, I'll call at [Time]. Is there anything I should know before we talk?"
- **If they say** "I'm just exploring, I don't want to be pitched": say "That's fine. I won't pitch you. I'm happy to answer questions, and if it turns into more than that, we'll go from there."
- **If they don't reply**: wait two or three days, then send one more message that adds something useful, such as a recent sale nearby or an answer to a question the referrer mentioned. After that, mention to the referrer that you reached out, and leave the next step to them.

Once they pick up, the call changes into a referral lead call. Our guide to [real estate phone scripts for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/) has the warm introduction opener and the four questions that follow it.

### Close the Loop With the Person Who Referred Them

Thank the referrer whether or not it turns into business. Keep it short, and only share what the new person has agreed you can share.

<ScriptExample label="Thank-you text to the referrer">
Hi [Name], I spoke with [Friend] today. Thank you for introducing us. I'll keep you posted on how it goes, and thank you for thinking of me.
</ScriptExample>

## Text and Voicemail Versions

Not every check-in will reach a live person, so keep a version for each channel. Both ask nothing, because the ask belongs in a live conversation.

<ScriptExample label="Check-in voicemail">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. Nothing urgent. I was thinking about you and wanted to see how things are going at [Street]. Call or text me back at [Number] when you get a minute.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Check-in text">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. It's been about a year since you moved into [Street]. How is the house treating you?
</ScriptExample>

If the person replies to the text, move to a call: "Glad to hear it. Do you have five minutes tonight or tomorrow? I'd love to hear more."

## A Contact Rhythm So the Ask Never Comes Out of Nowhere

Referral requests tend to land better when the person has heard from you several times without being asked for anything. Our [sphere of influence entry](/glossary/sphere-of-influence/) suggests grouping contacts into A, B and C lists, contacting your top list roughly monthly, and reaching everyone else at least four times a year. A rhythm built on that structure looks like this.

| Tier | Who | Touches per year | When to ask for a referral |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Past clients from the last two years and your closest contacts | Monthly, with at least four of them as calls | On one or two of the calls each year |
| B | Older past clients and friendly acquaintances | Quarterly | On one call each year |
| C | Wider sphere and professional contacts | Quarterly, mostly messages | Rarely; focus on being useful |

Make most of the touches useful instead of requesting anything: a market update for their neighborhood, a congratulations on something they posted, an anniversary note, or a helpful local resource. The ask then follows one of the calls above and feels like a natural next sentence. The same logic applies to buyers and sellers who did not hire you right away, which our guide to [how often to follow up](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/) covers.

## Mistakes That Make Referral Calls Awkward

- **Opening with the ask.** If the first thing they hear is a favor, the rest of the call becomes about the favor.
- **Asking a question that is too big.** "Do you know anyone who wants to buy or sell?" has no easy answer. A life-event prompt does.
- **Offering gifts or payments without checking first.** See the note below.
- **Forgetting the follow-up.** If someone says "I'll keep you in mind," put a reminder in your CRM and check in after a few weeks. People mean it, and they forget.
- **Sharing the referred person's details.** Tell the referrer you reached out. Leave the rest to the new contact, who may not want their plans shared.
- **Asking everyone for a referral in the same month.** Spread the asks across the year so it stays a conversation.

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Check with your managing broker before offering any payment or gift connected to a referral. Federal rules under RESPA Section 8 (12 CFR 1024.14) say that "no person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback or other thing of value" for referring settlement-service business, with an exception for cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between real estate agents and brokers. State rules and brokerage policies add to that. This is general information, not legal advice.
</CalloutBox>

## Keep the Details You Hear on These Calls

The details are what make the next ask specific. A daughter moving back in the fall, a job that is going remote, or a parent who is thinking about downsizing are all reasons to call again with a better question than "do you know anyone?" ## FAQ

**Do realtors pay for referrals?**
The rules depend on federal law, your state and your brokerage. Federal rules under RESPA Section 8 generally prohibit giving or accepting a fee or other thing of value for referring settlement-service business, with an exception for cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between real estate agents and brokers. Ask your managing broker before you offer or accept anything.

**How often should you ask past clients for referrals?**
Stay in touch several times a year, and ask directly on one or two of those calls. Asking on every call turns the relationship into a request line.

**Is it okay to ask for a referral by text?**
A text works for a gentle reminder, such as the post-closing message with your contact info. The specific life-event question works better on a call because the person can answer in the moment and you can ask a follow-up.
