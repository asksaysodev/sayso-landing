A divorce listing script has one job: show both spouses that you are neutral. Tell each of them that you work for both owners, that they will get the same information at the same time, and that your role is to get the home sold well, not to take a side.

Most divorce listings come from referrals by family law attorneys, mediators, or past clients. The script below is for the first call with whichever spouse reached out, and it sets up the call to the other spouse before you talk about price.

<ScriptExample label="First call after a referral">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. [Attorney or referral name] mentioned you might need some help with the home on [Street]. I know this is a hard time, and I'll keep things simple.

Before anything else, I want you to know how I work in these situations. I represent both owners equally. Anything I share with you, I share with [Spouse's name] at the same time, and I won't take sides on decisions between the two of you.

Can you tell me where things stand with the house? Has a sale been agreed to, or is it still being worked out?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We've agreed to sell": say "That helps. Is there a timeline from the agreement or the court that we need to work within?"
- **If they say** "My spouse doesn't want to sell": say "I understand. I can't help one side convince the other, but I can give you both the same clear numbers so the decision is based on facts. Would that be useful?"
- **If they say** "Talk to my attorney": say "Absolutely. I'm happy to coordinate with your attorney on timing and anything they need from me."

For the broader framework behind openers and appointment asks, see our [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/).

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Divorce situations can involve court orders and agreements that control whether and how a home is sold. Follow your state's rules and your brokerage's policy, keep both owners informed, and leave legal questions to their attorneys. You are there to help with the property, not to give legal advice or take a position in the divorce.
</CalloutBox>

## Which Divorce Listing Script to Use When

| Situation | Script | Goal |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| One spouse calls after a referral | First call | Establish neutrality and learn where things stand |
| Calling the other spouse | Second spouse call | Earn the same trust from the other owner |
| Building referral relationships | Attorney introduction | Become a trusted resource for family law offices |
| Checking back in | Follow-up call | Keep both owners moving together |

## The Second Spouse Call

Call the other spouse soon after the first conversation, ideally the same day. If they hear about you secondhand, they may assume you already took a side.

<ScriptExample label="Second spouse call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. [Spouse's name] reached out about the home on [Street], and I wanted to call you directly rather than have you hear about me through someone else.

I work for both owners equally. Anything I share with one of you, I share with both at the same time. Would it help if I walked you through how I'd approach the home, and then we find a time that works for both of you to meet, together or separately?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I didn't agree to this": say "That's fair, and nothing happens without both of you. I'd just like you to have the same information your spouse has."
- **If they say** "I don't want to be in the same room": say "That's completely fine. I can meet with each of you separately and send you both the same written summary afterward."

## The Attorney Introduction Script

Use this when you call a family law or mediation office to introduce yourself as a neutral resource.

<ScriptExample label="Family law attorney introduction">
Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I help divorcing couples sell their home, and I'm reaching out to a few family law offices in [City]. My approach is simple: I represent both owners equally, share every update with both parties at once, and coordinate with counsel on timing. Would it be helpful if I sent you a one-page summary of how I handle these listings, in case a client needs an agent?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We already have agents we refer": say "That makes sense. If one of them is ever unavailable or there's a conflict, I'd be glad to be a backup."

## The Follow-Up Call

When the owners need time, check in with both of them the same way.

<ScriptExample label="Divorce listing follow-up call">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I wanted to check in on the home on [Street]. I'm calling [Spouse's name] today as well. Is there anything that has changed on your end, or anything you'd like me to look into for both of you?
</ScriptExample>

## Objections on Divorce Listing Calls

- **"We need to decide together first."** Agree, and offer to meet with both of them so they get the same facts. The [my spouse needs to decide](/objections/my-spouse-needs-to-decide/) page has wording you can adapt.
- **"The price is too high" or "too low."** Expect each spouse to want a different number. Show both of them the same comparable sales, and use the [price too high objection](/objections/price-too-high/) approach to keep the discussion on the data.
- **"We'll wait until things settle down."** Ask whether there is a deadline from their agreement, and follow up when they tell you to.

Once both owners agree to meet, prepare as you would for any [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/), with a plan to send a written summary to both of them afterward. Other seller conversations that call for this level of care include the [probate real estate script](/blog/probate-real-estate-script/) and the [pre-foreclosure script](/blog/pre-foreclosure-script/). If one spouse has already moved out and kept the home as a rental, the [absentee owner script](/blog/absentee-owner-script/) may fit.

## FAQ

**How do you get divorce listings as a real estate agent?**
Most come from referrals by family law attorneys, mediators, financial professionals, and past clients. Introduce yourself to local family law offices, explain how you stay neutral, and follow up with a short written summary of your process.

**Should a listing agent represent both spouses in a divorce?**
Both spouses are usually owners, so the listing agent works for both of them. That means sharing the same information with both at the same time and not taking a position on decisions between them.

**What should you avoid saying to divorcing sellers?**
Avoid anything that sounds like you are siding with one spouse, keeping secrets from the other, or giving legal advice. Don't comment on the divorce itself, and keep every update the same for both owners.

**What if only one spouse wants to sell?**
Don't try to persuade the other spouse on anyone's behalf. Offer both of them the same numbers and let their attorneys or the court handle the decision. Your role starts once both owners, or a court order, allow the sale.
