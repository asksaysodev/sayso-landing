A typical conversation between a real estate agent and a customer follows five beats. The agent says who they are and why they are calling, asks why the move matters right now, learns the timeline and the money in a light way, and ends by proposing a specific next step. The agent talks less than the customer for most of the call, and every question builds on something the customer just said.

Below are two annotated examples of that pattern, one with a buyer and one with a seller. Each call is broken into short stages, with the words the agent says, what the customer might say, and a note on why each turn is there. These are written examples, not recordings of real calls, and the names, places and numbers are made up so you can swap in your own.

## What a Good Agent and Client Conversation Looks Like

Both examples follow the same five beats. Keep this table nearby as you read, then use it to review your own calls.

| Beat | What the agent does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | Names who they are, the reason for the call and asks if it is a good time | The customer knows what the call is about within ten seconds |
| 2. Motivation | Asks why the move matters now | The reason tells you the timeline and what to say later |
| 3. Details | Learns the location, price or home details, and whether they are working with another agent | You can recommend a next step that fits |
| 4. Money | Asks about payment, financing or what they need to walk away with, in plain language | The customer sees the plan as realistic, and you avoid surprises |
| 5. Next step | Proposes a specific meeting with two time options | A time on the calendar beats "call me when you're ready" |

The questions in beats 2 to 4 are the LPMAMA flow (location, price, motivation, agent, mortgage, appointment), which our guide to [LPMAMA in real estate](/blog/lpmama-real-estate/) explains in detail. The order below changes slightly in each call because the customer's answers decide what to ask next.

## Example 1: A Buyer Call From an Online Inquiry

Maya asked about three-bedroom homes near [Area] on the brokerage's website. The agent is calling a short time later.

### Stage 1: The Opening

<ScriptExample label="Buyer call, stage 1: open and ask permission">
Agent: Hi, is this Maya?

Maya: Yes, who's this?

Agent: Hi Maya, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You asked about three-bedroom homes near [Area] on our site, and I wanted to see if I could help. Is now an okay time for a few minutes?

Maya: Sure, I have a few minutes.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:** The agent gives a name, a company and the reason for the call in two sentences, so Maya never wonders who is calling or what they want. Asking whether it is a good time gives her control, which lowers her guard. The question that follows can be a real one because she agreed to the conversation.

### Stage 2: Motivation and Timeline

<ScriptExample label="Buyer call, stage 2: why now and when">
Agent: Great. What got you looking at three-bedroom homes right now?

Maya: Our lease ends in June, and we're expecting a baby in the spring, so the apartment won't work.

Agent: Congratulations. So June is the date that matters. If the right home came up in April, would you be able to move on it, or is June the earliest?

Maya: April would be fine if it's the right place.

Agent: Got it. And what is it about [Area] that you like?

Maya: My sister lives there, and we've heard good things about the schools.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- "What got you looking right now" asks for the reason, not the budget. Her answer gives the deadline (June), the life event (a baby) and a reason for the location (her sister and the schools).
- "Congratulations. So June is the date that matters" repeats her words back, which shows the agent listened and confirms the date.
- "If the right home came up in April" tests how ready she is without asking "are you serious?" The answer shows a real window, between April and June.
- The location question asks why the area matters instead of simply which area. The answer ("my sister," "the schools") is something to bring up again later.

### Stage 3: Price and Other Agents

<ScriptExample label="Buyer call, stage 3: payment and who else is involved">
Agent: Do you have a price range in mind? A monthly payment you'd feel comfortable with works too.

Maya: Around $2,800 a month, I guess. I haven't talked to a lender.

Agent: That's a helpful starting point. Is anyone else helping you look, or have you been searching on your own?

Maya: Just on our own. We visited an open house with an agent, but we didn't sign anything.

Agent: Thanks for telling me. Plenty of buyers haven't talked to a lender at this stage, and that's normal. It helps to sort it out before you find the home, so you're ready to move when the right one appears. I can connect you with a lender I trust if you'd like.

Maya: Maybe, let's see.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- Offering a monthly payment as the answer makes the money question easier than asking for a purchase price. Many buyers think in payments.
- "Is anyone else helping you look" and her answer ("we didn't sign anything") tell the agent whether another agent has a claim on the relationship.
- The agent treats the missing lender conversation as common instead of a problem, then offers help once. "Maybe, let's see" is enough, and the agent does not push.

### Stage 4: The Next Step

<ScriptExample label="Buyer call, stage 4: propose a specific meeting">
Agent: Based on what you've told me, the best next step is a 20 minute sit-down, in person or on video. I'll show you what's available in your range near [Area] and map out the timeline backward from June. I have Tuesday at 6 or Wednesday at noon. Which works better?

Maya: Do we have to meet? Can you just send me some listings?

Agent: Happy to send listings. So I send the right ones, it helps to spend a few minutes on what you actually want. It's about twenty minutes. Would Tuesday at 6 work, and is your partner able to join?

Maya: Okay, Tuesday works. He should be able to come.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- The meeting is tied to things Maya said: the range, the area and the June deadline. It sounds like the next step in her plan, not a sales meeting.
- Two specific times are easier to answer than "when are you free?"
- When she asks for listings, the agent agrees to send them and gives a reason to meet first. A listing alert alone does not give the agent a way to keep the conversation going.
- "Is your partner able to join" brings the other decision maker in early.

### What a Buyer Might Say Next

- **If they say** "We're just looking": say "That makes sense, most people start there. What would have to happen for looking to turn into moving?"
- **If they say** "I'm not ready to meet yet": say "Totally fair. What would you want to know before you'd feel ready? I can start by sending you a few things and checking in next [week]."
- **If they say** "I'm already working with someone": say "Good to know. Have you signed anything with them, or have you mostly been talking?" If they have not signed, ask what they hope to be different. If they have, wish them luck and ask if you can stay in touch.
- **If they say** "Can we just talk on the phone?": say "Sure, we can cover the basics now. The meeting is for the timeline and the homes themselves, which are easier on a screen or at a table. Would a video call work?"

## Example 2: A Seller Call From a Home Value Request

Dan requested a home value for his house some time ago. The agent is following up.

### Stage 1: The Opening

<ScriptExample label="Seller call, stage 1: reconnect without pressure">
Agent: Hi, is this Dan?

Dan: Yes.

Agent: Hey Dan, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You requested a home value on [Street] a while back, and I was going through my notes and wanted to make sure nobody followed up with you. Did you end up doing anything with the house, or are you still there?

Dan: Still here. I was just curious what it was worth.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:** The agent starts with the customer's own action, the value request, and gives an easy reason for calling ("make sure nobody followed up"). The question has two simple answers, "I sold" or "I'm still here," so Dan is not put on the spot. "Just curious" is the most common answer, and the agent has a plan for it.

### Stage 2: Why They Asked

<ScriptExample label="Seller call, stage 2: find the reason behind 'just curious'">
Agent: Totally fair. Most people who check their home's value are thinking about selling, thinking about refinancing, or curious how much equity they've built. Which one were you?

Dan: Honestly, probably selling in a year or so. Our kids are heading to college.

Agent: Congratulations to them. So the kids leaving is what's behind it. What are you picturing for after that?

Dan: A smaller place, maybe closer to my wife's work.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- Giving three options makes it easy for Dan to pick one, and being honest about why people check values lowers his guard.
- The answer ("selling in a year or so," "kids heading to college") surfaces the life event and a timeline. This is the motivation that sellers often hold back until asked directly.
- "What are you picturing for after that" asks about the future, not the house, which moves the conversation from curiosity to a plan.

### Stage 3: Timeline, Agent and Home Details

<ScriptExample label="Seller call, stage 3: timeline, other agents and the home">
Agent: If you did sell, when would you ideally want to be out?

Dan: Probably next summer, after the school year.

Agent: That gives us time to plan. Are you talking with an agent, or just gathering information?

Dan: Just gathering information. An online estimate gave me a number, and it seemed high.

Agent: Online estimates often miss condition and upgrades. Have you changed anything in the house since you bought it?

Dan: We redid the kitchen about five years ago, and the roof is newer.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- A target move-out date ("next summer") gives the agent a calendar to plan against.
- "Just gathering information" tells the agent that no one else is working with Dan yet, and the agent notes it without pushing.
- The agent does not argue with Dan's online estimate. The agent explains why estimates miss things and asks about upgrades, which lets Dan raise the kitchen and roof himself.

### Stage 4: Money and the Appointment

<ScriptExample label="Seller call, stage 4: what they need and the next step">
Agent: Just so I understand the financial side, do you have a sense of what you'd need to walk away with for the move to work?

Dan: I haven't worked it out, but we want to put something toward the next place.

Agent: That makes sense. What I usually do is stop by for twenty minutes, look at the kitchen, the roof and the layout, and give you a realistic range. That way you're deciding from facts, not guesses. Would Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning be easier?

Dan: Can't you just tell me over the phone?

Agent: I can give you the general picture, but the number depends on the condition and the upgrades, and anything I said without seeing it would be a guess. Twenty minutes in person gets you a number you can plan with. Would Tuesday afternoon work, and would your wife like to be there too?

Dan: Tuesday afternoon works. She'll want to be there.
</ScriptExample>

**Why it works:**

- Asking what Dan needs to walk away with covers the money question without asking whether the house is paid off, which can feel invasive.
- The meeting is connected to the things Dan mentioned (kitchen, roof) and to a clear benefit (a number to plan with).
- When Dan asks for a quote over the phone, the agent gives a reason to come by that is honest: the number depends on seeing the house.
- Inviting Dan's wife to the meeting brings the second decision maker to the table before the appointment, and not after.

### What a Seller Might Say Next

- **If they say** "I'm not selling for a year, so why meet now?": say "That's exactly why this is a good time. A year out, small decisions on repairs and timing can make a big difference. The meeting gives you a number and a plan, and you decide when to start."
- **If they say** "Another agent already told me what it's worth": say "That's useful. Did they walk through the house, or was it an estimate? I'd be glad to give you a second set of numbers."
- **If they say** "Send me a market report": say "Happy to. A report shows the neighborhood, and the home's condition decides where yours lands. A quick visit gets you a number that fits your house."

## The Same Turn, Done Weakly and Done Well

Most conversation problems happen on one turn. Compare the weak and stronger versions of the same moment, then check your own calls for them.

| Moment | Weak version | Stronger version |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | "Hi, I'm calling about your inquiry. Are you ready to buy?" | "I'm calling about the three-bedroom homes you asked about. Is now an okay time for a few minutes?" |
| Asking about budget | "What's your price range?" | "Do you have a monthly payment in mind that feels comfortable?" |
| Customer says "just curious" | "Okay, let me know if you ever want to list." | "Most people who check their value are thinking about selling, refinancing or curious about equity. Which one were you?" |
| Customer asks for listings | Sends links and waits | "Happy to send them. To send the right ones, what's got you thinking about a move?" |
| Asked for a value on the phone | Gives a number | "The number depends on the condition and upgrades. Twenty minutes in person gets you a figure you can plan with." |
| Ending the call | "Call me if you have questions." | "I have Tuesday at 6 or Wednesday at noon. Which works better?" |

## How to Talk to Clients as a Real Estate Agent: Five Habits Behind Both Calls

These habits show up in both examples. They are a better guide to talking with clients than any single line.

1. **Give a reason for the call in the first sentence.** People answer more openly when they know what the call is about.
2. **Ask why before you ask what.** Motivation questions come before price and property questions, since the reason decides the timeline.
3. **Repeat back one detail.** "June is the date that matters" tells the customer someone is listening.
4. **Use their words for the next question.** Maya's "the apartment won't work" leads to the move date. Dan's "kids heading to college" leads to the timeline.
5. **End with two time options.** A specific time is easier to agree to than an open invitation. Our guide to [closing for the appointment](/blog/how-to-close-for-appointment/) covers what to do if the answer is no.
