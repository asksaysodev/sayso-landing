A withdrawn listing script should start by finding out whether the seller still wants to sell, not by pitching a new listing. A withdrawn seller chose to take the home off the market, and in some cases is still under agreement with their listing agent, so your first job is to learn what changed and whether they are free to talk to a new agent. If they are, offer a short meeting to walk through their options.

This guide covers how a withdrawn listing differs from an expired one, a one-minute check before you dial, scripts for the main reasons sellers pull a home, a voicemail, a follow-up call, and the replies you will hear most. It is part of our complete [real estate cold call scripts](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) guide and follows the same structure as our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) post, because the two calls are related, but the seller's situation is not the same.

## Withdrawn, Expired and Cancelled: What Each Status Means for Your Call

The three statuses look alike on a search screen and mean different things on the phone. A May 2026 explainer from KDS Home Buyers describes an expired listing as one whose contract reached its end date without a sale, which happens on its own, and a withdrawn listing as one the seller deliberately removed before the contract period ended. Statuses are defined by each MLS, so read your own MLS rules. As one example, a 2018 post from the Greater Albuquerque Association of REALTORS® (GAAR) describes withdrawn status as "unavailable for showing" with the listing agreement "still in effect," and says cancelled status means the seller terminated the agreement in writing.

| Status | What happened | What it usually means for your call |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Expired | The listing contract reached its end date without a sale | The agreement has ended, so you can ask about their plans |
| Withdrawn | The seller took the home off the market on purpose | Find out why, and whether the agreement is still in place |
| Cancelled | The seller ended the agreement in writing | The agreement has been terminated, so they are likely free to talk to a new agent |

The GAAR post also says days on market stop counting while a listing is withdrawn, so a total days on market figure may understate how long the home was actually available. Look at the full status history rather than a single number.

## Check Before You Dial: The 60-Second Framework

Spend a minute on the listing before you call. The goal is to know what you can safely say, and to find one useful question to ask.

**Status and dates.** Is the listing withdrawn, cancelled or expired, and when did the status change? Check the listing agreement's end date if your MLS shows it. A seller whose agreement already ended is a different call than one whose agreement lasts another two months.

**Price history.** Did the price drop several times before the home came off? Several price changes suggest a pricing conversation. No changes at all suggest something else, such as a change of plans.

**Presentation.** Pull up the original photos and description. Dark rooms or a thin description give you something specific to mention if the seller wants to relist.

**Likely reason.** Reasons sellers pull a home vary. The KDS Home Buyers explainer lists a relocation, a health issue or a divorce, a decision to rent instead, a wish to change the price and relaunch without the days on market piling up, unhappiness with the agent, and seasonal timing. You do not know which one applies, so ask.

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Before you call, confirm the seller is free to talk to you. Article 16 of the REALTOR® Code of Ethics requires members to respect the exclusive representation agreements other REALTORS® have with their clients, and a NAR case interpretation (Case #16-5) concludes that once a listing expires, soliciting it does not violate Article 16. A withdrawn listing may still be under agreement, so ask your broker what to do if the status is unclear. Check [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) and your brokerage's policy as well. This is not legal advice.
</CalloutBox>

## The Withdrawn Listing Script to Start With

If you only use one script, use this one. It works on any first call because it asks about the seller's plan before it offers anything, and it gives you an answer to the most important question, whether the seller is still willing to sell.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing: First Call">
Hi [Name]? This is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your home on [Street]. I noticed it came off the market recently, so I wanted to ask: are you still planning to sell, or did something change?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "We decided to take it off the market": say "Is that because the move isn't necessary anymore, or because the process didn't feel worth it?" Then ask, "If the right strategy were in place, would you still consider selling?"
- **If they say** "Yes, we still want to sell": say "Got it. When you first listed, where were you planning to go? Is that move still important?" Then ask, "If you had to do it again, what would you change?"
- **If they say** "We're still working with our agent": say "Thanks for telling me. I wouldn't want to step in the middle of that. Would it be okay if I checked back after [date]?" Then note it in your CRM and leave them alone until then.
- **If they say** "We'll try again in the spring": say "That makes sense. What would you want to have done by then so you feel ready?" The [we'll wait for spring](/objections/well-wait-for-spring/) page has more.
- **If they say** "How did you get my number?": say "I keep track of homes in the neighborhood that recently came off the market, and yours was one. If you'd rather I not call again, I'll take you off my list right now." Then do it.

When the seller says yes to "would you still consider selling," move to the meeting: "Let's spend 20 minutes walking through it so you know exactly what your options look like. Are mornings or afternoons better? And is there anyone else who should be part of that conversation?"

## Withdrawn Listing Scripts by Reason

The first script works for everyone. Once you hear the reason, switch to the script that fits.

### The Seller Who Needs a Break

Some sellers pull a home because life got in the way: a family issue, a job change, a repair they need to finish first. They are not looking for a pitch today, and the right outcome of the call is permission to stay in touch.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing: The Seller Who Needs a Break">
Totally understand, and thanks for telling me. Moving is a big decision, and it's fine to hit pause. When do you think you might want to look at it again, a few months from now, or sometime next year?

If it's helpful, I can send you a quick note then on what homes like yours in [Neighborhood] are selling for, so you have a current number when you're ready. Is email or text better?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Please don't": say "No problem at all. If anything changes, you have my number." Then take them off your list and log it.
- **If they say** "Maybe in a few months": set a follow-up task for that month and put the reason in the notes. Our guide on [how often to follow up](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/) shows how to space the touches.

### The Seller Who Wants a Different Plan

Some sellers pulled the home because the first attempt was frustrating, whether that came from the price, the marketing or the service. Ask them to describe it before you say anything about yourself.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing: The Seller Who Wants a Different Plan">
On a scale of 1 to 7, how frustrated are you with how it played out?

When you first listed, where were you planning on going? Is that move still important?

If you had to do it again, what would you change? And have you ended your agreement with them, or is it still in place?

Would you be open to seeing what a different plan would look like before you decide what to do next? I'd like 20 minutes to walk through what I'd adjust so you can decide with clarity. Would this afternoon work, or would tomorrow be easier?
</ScriptExample>

To book it, use the standard sequence. Ask whether they would want a partner or spouse there, tell them you will be in and out in 20 minutes, confirm their email for the calendar invite, and close with "If anything changes, just text me, even if we need to push it a few minutes."

- **If they say** "We're going to relist with the same agent": say "Totally fair. What do you expect to be different with the same agent?" Then ask, "Would it hurt to get a second opinion before signing another agreement?"
- **If they say** "Our agreement is still in place": say "Thanks for being straight with me. I'll let you finish that with them. Mind if I check back after it ends?" Stop there for now.
- **If they say** "We were frustrated with the price we got offered": say "That's useful to know. Would you be open to looking at what homes like yours have sold for recently, so you can see where the market is?"

### The Seller Who Is Renting It Out Instead

Renting is one of the common reasons a home is withdrawn. These owners are not selling for now, but they may sell later, and they may need help with the rental itself. Treat them as a long-term relationship.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing: The Seller Who Decided to Rent">
That's a big decision. Is renting the plan for the long term, or a way to wait for a better time to sell?
</ScriptExample>

If they plan to rent long term, our [absentee owner script](/blog/absentee-owner-script/) has a landlord script that fits their next stage.

## Withdrawn Listing Voicemail and Follow-Up Call

Most calls to withdrawn sellers reach voicemail the first time. Keep the message calm and specific to their home, and do not mention the listing status, since another person in the household may hear it first.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing Voicemail">
Hi [Name], this is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your home on [Street]. I have some information about the neighborhood that I thought could be helpful whichever way you decide to go. You can reach me at [Number] whenever it's convenient.
</ScriptExample>

If the seller asked for time, check in after a few weeks with something new to share, such as a recent sale nearby.

<ScriptExample label="Withdrawn Listing Follow-Up Call">
Hi [Name], it's [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. We spoke a few weeks ago about your home on [Street]. I wanted to check in and see how you're feeling about the timing now. Have your plans changed at all?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We're still not sure": say "That's completely fair. Would it help if I sent you what's sold near you since we last spoke, so you can see how the market has moved?"
- **If they say** "We're ready to talk": say "Great. Let's spend 20 minutes walking through it so you know exactly what your options look like. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?"

## What to Say When Withdrawn Sellers Push Back

Withdrawn sellers usually push back because they just went through an exhausting process or made a decision they feel good about. Respect the decision first.

**"I'm not interested."** Accept it and leave the door open: "Understood. If anything changes, you have my number." Our page on [the not interested objection](/objections/not-interested/) has more.

**"I already have an agent."** Ask about the agreement, then respect it. See [already have an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/) for the full response.

**"We're going to sell it ourselves."** That is a legitimate choice. You can say, "Sounds good. If you'd like, I can share what homes like yours have sold for." The [want to sell ourselves](/objections/want-to-sell-ourselves/) page covers it in more depth.

**"We were disappointed with the whole process."** Validate it before you say anything else: "That sounds frustrating. What was the hardest part?" Our page on the [bad experience with agents](/objections/bad-experience-with-agents/) objection has more.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Never argue with a seller who has just made a decision to stop. Validate it, ask one question, and set a follow-up. The seller who says "not now" today is often the one who calls the agent who respected that.
</CalloutBox>

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a withdrawn listing and an expired listing?**
An expired listing reached the end of its contract without a sale, while a withdrawn listing is one the seller took off the market on purpose before the contract ended. Because a withdrawn listing may still be under agreement, find out where the seller stands before you pitch.

**What is the difference between withdrawing a listing and terminating it?**
In the GAAR post, a withdrawn status means the property is unavailable for showing while the listing agreement is still in effect, and a cancelled status means the seller terminated the agreement in writing. Rules differ by MLS, so check your own.

**Can you call a withdrawn listing?**
It depends on whether the seller is still under an exclusive agreement and on the Do Not Call rules that apply to your list. Respect the other agent's agreement, and ask your broker if you are unsure.

**How do you find withdrawn listings?**
Search your MLS by status for withdrawn, cancelled and expired over the last month or two, and review each listing's history before you call. Our guide on [how to find expired listings](/blog/how-to-find-expired-listings/) walks through the search.
