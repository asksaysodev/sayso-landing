[Circle prospecting](/glossary/circle-prospecting/) means calling the homeowners in a small area around a recent sale, a new listing or an open house to start a conversation about the neighborhood. Agents also call it circle calling or circle dialing. A circle prospecting script gives you a reason to call that has nothing to do with selling, because you are sharing neighborhood news instead of pitching. That is why it is the easiest entry point for agents who freeze on other types of outbound calls. The problem is that most agents use the same generic opener regardless of what triggered the call, and generic scripts get generic responses.

This guide gives you circle prospecting scripts matched to three specific trigger events, a worked example call, a voicemail framework that earns callbacks (since most dials end in voicemail), a short guide to building and checking your call list, and the conversation flow that turns "we're not moving" into a future appointment. For a plain-language definition and tips, see our [circle prospecting glossary entry](/glossary/circle-prospecting/). These scripts follow the Open-Bridge-Close framework from our complete [real estate cold call scripts](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) guide, adapted for the unique dynamics of neighborhood prospecting.

### Which Circle Prospecting Script to Use When

| What just happened | Script | Goal of the call |
|---|---|---|
| A nearby home closed | Just-Sold: The Neighborhood Update | Permission to send a value breakdown |
| You or another agent listed nearby | Just-Listed: The Open House Invitation | Get them to the open house |
| The neighborhood hit a price or sales milestone | Market Milestone: The Neighborhood Trend | Permission to send a market snapshot |
| Nobody picked up | Circle Prospecting Voicemail | A callback, or a warmer second call |

## Match the Script to What Just Happened on the Street

Most circle prospecting advice boils down to "call the neighbors and tell them a home sold." That works, but it is only one play. The strongest circle prospecting calls are anchored to a specific trigger event: something that happened on the prospect's street that makes your call feel like news, not a sales pitch.

Three trigger events produce the best conversations:

**Just Sold.** A home in the neighborhood closed. You have a specific address, price, and days on market. This is the most common trigger and the one every agent uses, but most waste it with vague delivery.

**Just Listed.** You or another agent just listed a home nearby. The angle shifts from "your neighbor's home sold" to "activity is picking up on your street." Just-listed calls work especially well when paired with an open house invitation.

**Market Milestone.** This is the trigger most agents miss entirely. When a neighborhood hits a pricing threshold, like the median crossing $500K, appreciation exceeding 10% year over year, or three homes selling in a single quarter, you have data-driven news that feels relevant to every homeowner on the street. These calls convert at a higher rate because you are not talking about one house. You are talking about their neighborhood's trajectory.

The script changes for each trigger. The opener changes. The bridge question changes. The close changes. Treat them as three distinct calls, not one script with a few words swapped out.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
The biggest mistake on circle prospecting calls: leading with "a home sold near you" without specifics. Mention the exact address, the sale price, and how quickly it closed. Specificity signals that you actually work the neighborhood. Vagueness signals that you bought a list and are reading from a screen.
</CalloutBox>

## Circle Prospecting Scripts for Every Trigger Event

### The Just-Sold Script

This is the foundation. A home closed, and you are calling homeowners within a few blocks. Lead with the data point, not your introduction.

<ScriptExample label="Just-Sold: The Neighborhood Update">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. A home just sold at [Address] for [Price], and it closed in just [X] days. I'm reaching out to a few homeowners nearby because that sale shifts the comparable values on your street. Would you like me to send you a quick breakdown of what your home is likely worth based on that sale?
</ScriptExample>

The comp breakdown offer is the bridge. It is genuinely useful to the homeowner whether they are thinking about a move or not, and it gives you a reason to follow up in three to five days with the actual report. That follow-up call is where the real conversation starts.

Here is what most scripts leave out: what to say after they respond. If they say yes, confirm their email, tell them you will have it over within 48 hours, and set your CRM reminder. If they say no, ask: "No problem at all. Out of curiosity, do you know roughly how long you plan to stay in the neighborhood?" That one question identifies timeline without any pressure. A homeowner who says "probably another two years" just told you when to call back with real intent.

### A Circle Prospecting Example, Start to Finish

Here is how a short just-sold call can unfold when the homeowner answers. The lines are an illustration, so put them in your own words.

<ScriptExample label="Circle Prospecting Example: From Opener to Follow-Up Task">
You: Hi Dana, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. A home just sold at 412 Oak Lane for $485,000, and it closed in 11 days. I'm calling a few neighbors because that sale changes the comparable values on your street. Would a quick breakdown of what your home is likely worth be useful?

Dana: Sure, I guess. We're not selling though.

You: Totally understand, most of your neighbors aren't either. I'll send it over by tomorrow. Out of curiosity, do you know roughly how long you plan to stay in the house?

Dana: Probably another two years, until our youngest finishes school.

You: That's helpful. I'll send the breakdown, and I'll check in when something sells nearby so you always know where your home stands. What's the best email for it?
</ScriptExample>

Three things happened on that call. The agent gave the homeowner something useful before asking for anything, learned a timeline without pressure, and ended with permission to stay in touch. Log "two years, youngest finishing school" in your CRM and set the next touch for the next nearby sale.

### The Just-Listed Script

Just-listed calls have a built-in advantage: you can invite them to an open house. An in-person visit to a neighbor's listing is the highest-converting touchpoint in circle prospecting because the homeowner gets to see your staging, marketing, and professionalism firsthand.

<ScriptExample label="Just-Listed: The Open House Invitation">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I just listed a home at [Address] down the street from you, and we're hosting an open house this [Day] from [Time] to [Time]. I'm inviting a few neighbors because you'll get to see the inside, and honestly, neighbors often know someone who'd love to move onto their street. Would you like to stop by?
</ScriptExample>

The "neighbors often know someone" line is deliberate. It gives them a low-pressure reason to attend that has nothing to do with their own plans. Once they walk through the door, you are in a face-to-face conversation where trust builds in minutes instead of months. This is the same principle behind the open house approach in our [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/) guide: get in the room and demonstrate value in person. If you are calling about a home you sold rather than listed, our [just listed and just sold scripts](/blog/just-listed-just-sold-scripts/) cover both versions in one place.

### The Market Milestone Script

This is the script nobody else is using. Most agents only call when they have a listing or a sale. But neighborhood data creates equally strong reasons to dial.

<ScriptExample label="Market Milestone: The Neighborhood Trend">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I track [Neighborhood] pretty closely, and something interesting happened this quarter: [X] homes sold, and the average sale price hit [Price]. That's up [X]% from last year. I'm reaching out to a few homeowners in the area because that shift might affect your home's value more than you'd expect. Would a quick market snapshot for your street be useful?
</ScriptExample>

This works because it positions you as someone who monitors the neighborhood proactively, not someone who only calls when they need a listing. Pull this data from your MLS. It takes five minutes to run a quarterly stats report, and it gives you a reason to call every homeowner on the street, not just the ones near a single sale.

## The Voicemail Script Most Agents Skip

Here is the reality of circle prospecting: you will reach voicemail on most of your dials. Most agents either hang up or leave a rambling message that gets deleted before it finishes. Both are wasted opportunities.

A good circle prospecting voicemail does one thing: creates enough curiosity to earn a callback or make the homeowner pick up the next time your number appears.

<ScriptExample label="Circle Prospecting Voicemail">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling because a home on your street just sold for a price that caught my attention, and I thought you'd want to know what it means for your property's value. My number is [Number]. Give me a call back, or I'll try you again in a few days.
</ScriptExample>

Three rules for circle prospecting voicemails:

**Keep it short.** Aim for about 20 seconds, because a longer message is easy to skip. Your voicemail is a headline, not an article.

**Lead with the hook, not your name.** "A home on your street sold for a surprising price" is more compelling than "Hi, I'm a real estate agent calling to introduce myself." Your name matters less than the reason they should care.

**Tell them you will call again.** This sets the expectation for your follow-up and makes them more likely to pick up next time. When they see your number a second time, it registers as "that person who called about the sale on my street" instead of "unknown caller." For structuring the full follow-up sequence after your voicemail, see our guide on [follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/).

Most agents treat voicemail as a failure. Agents who consistently build their pipeline treat voicemail as step one of a multi-touch campaign: the voicemail primes the homeowner and the second call starts the conversation. For voicemails across other situations, see our [real estate voicemail scripts](/blog/real-estate-voicemail-scripts/).

<CalloutBox type="tip">
If you are making a block of circle prospecting dials, most of them will go to voicemail. Batch them. Keep a notepad with the specific address and price for each neighborhood so you can personalize each message in a few seconds, because a message that names a real street reads as news instead of a form letter.
</CalloutBox>

## Build Your Circle Prospecting List and Check It First

A circle is the homeowners closest to the trigger property. Published guides disagree on the size. [Market Leader's circle prospecting guide](https://www.marketleader.com/blog/circle-prospecting-in-real-estate-a-simple-guide-to-generating-leads-around-your-listings/) suggests a quarter to a half mile around the property, with fewer homes in dense areas and more in suburban ones, and other guides leave the radius open. Start with the closest homes and widen only if the sale or listing is still relevant to the next street over. If you are deciding how much of your week circle prospecting deserves, our guide to [real estate prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) compares it with the other lead sources.

Once you have the addresses, you still need phone numbers, and those calls follow the same rules as any other sales call. Numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry are off limits for sales calls unless an exception applies, lists must be checked against the registry at least every 31 days, and calls to homes are limited to 8 a.m. through 9 p.m. in the homeowner's local time, according to the [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/qa-telemarketers-sellers-about-dnc-provisions-tsr) and [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200). Our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains how this works in practice, and your broker can tell you how your office scrubs lists. Homes without a callable number are a good fit for a mailer or a door knock instead.

If you are new to calling, our [real estate scripts for new agents](/blog/real-estate-scripts-for-new-agents/) shows which scripts to learn first, and circle prospecting is a gentle place to start.

<ScriptSheet title="Circle prospecting call sheet">

**Just sold:** "A home just sold at [Address] for [Price], and it closed in just [X] days. That sale shifts the comparable values on your street. Would you like a quick breakdown of what your home is likely worth?"

**Just listed:** "I just listed a home at [Address] down the street, and we're hosting an open house this [Day] from [Time] to [Time]. Would you like to stop by?"

**Market milestone:** "[X] homes sold in [Neighborhood] this quarter, and the average sale price hit [Price]. Would a quick market snapshot for your street be useful?"

**Voicemail:** "A home on your street just sold for a price that caught my attention, and I thought you'd want to know what it means for your property's value. My number is [Number], or I'll try you again in a few days."

**If they say "We're not moving":** "No problem at all. Out of curiosity, do you know roughly how long you plan to stay in the neighborhood?"

</ScriptSheet>

## FAQ

**How often should I circle prospect the same neighborhood?**
Every time a trigger event happens: a sale, a new listing, or a quarterly market shift. For your core farm area, that typically means reaching out every four to six weeks. The homeowner who hears from you three times over four months will call you when they are ready. The agent who called once eight months ago is forgotten.

**How many homes should I call per trigger event?**
Start with 25-50 homes within a half-mile radius of the trigger property. In denser neighborhoods, tighten the radius. In rural or suburban areas, expand it. The key is that every homeowner you call should realistically be affected by the event. If the sale happened three miles away, it is not relevant to them and you lose credibility.

**Is circle prospecting worth it for new agents?**
Yes, and it is arguably the best starting point. Unlike [expired listing calls](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) where homeowners are already getting hammered by experienced agents, circle prospecting calls are lower competition. You have a built-in reason to call, the scripts are straightforward, and you are building a database of homeowners who will remember you when they are ready. Pair it with [strong call openers](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/) and you will build confidence fast.

**What is circle dialing?**
Circle dialing is another name for circle prospecting. Agents also say circle calling. All three mean calling the homeowners around a recent sale, listing or open house, and the scripts on this page work for each.

**What do I say when they say "we're not thinking about moving"?**
Do not push. This is the most common response, and it is not a rejection. Say: "Totally understand, most folks aren't. I just like keeping homeowners in the loop when something happens on their street. Would it be okay if I sent you a market update now and then?" You have just converted a no into permission to follow up. For more response frameworks, see our guide to [handling "not interested"](/objections/not-interested/).
