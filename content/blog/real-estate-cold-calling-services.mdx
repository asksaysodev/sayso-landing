A real estate cold calling service, or a virtual assistant (VA) you hire directly, dials lists on your behalf and usually hands you the people who are interested or the appointments they booked. Making the calls yourself costs your time instead of a fee, and it keeps every conversation, objection and relationship in your own hands. Which one costs less depends on how the service charges, how many calls become real appointments, and what an hour of your time is worth.

This guide compares the two paths by cost structure and trade-offs. It names no company and quotes no prices, because published rates vary widely and change often. Instead it gives you the pricing models you will see, the costs that never show up on an invoice, the compliance question to ask about, and a worksheet you can fill in with your own numbers.

## What a Real Estate Cold Calling Service Actually Does

A calling service puts a trained caller, often working in a managed team, on a list you supply or approve. The caller dials, leaves voicemails, has a first conversation and either passes along interested prospects or books an appointment on your calendar. Some services also update your CRM, follow up on online leads and handle appointment reminders. A VA you hire directly does a similar job, with fewer layers and more of the management on you.

Some service pages describe the work mostly in terms of dials per day, speed of contact and how fast a caller can start, and say little about how the caller handles a prospect who pushes back, who owns the script, or who is responsible for do not call compliance. Those are the questions that decide whether outsourced calls feel like your business or like someone else's.

## Your Options Compared

| Option | Who does the calling | Who manages it | How you pay | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Call yourself | You | You | Your time | Agents who want to hear every objection and build the skill |
| VA you hire directly | A freelance or contract assistant | You | Hourly or per project | Agents who can write scripts and review calls |
| Managed calling service | A trained team | The provider | Hourly, monthly or per result | Agents who want volume without managing people |
| In-house ISA | A salaried or commissioned [inside sales agent](/glossary/isa-real-estate/) | You or your team leader | Salary or commission | Teams with steady lead flow and a manager |

An in-house ISA is the closest option to doing it yourself, since you choose the person, the scripts and the schedule. The [ISA scripts guide](/blog/real-estate-isa-scripts/) covers what that role says on calls.

## How Real Estate Cold Calling Services Charge

Service pages describe a few different pricing models. The model matters more than the headline number, because each one shifts risk to a different party. Some pages publish only a starting rate and others quote prices individually, so confirm the full cost in writing before you commit.

| Pricing model | How it works | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly | You pay for caller time, sometimes billed weekly | Hours spent are not the same as results. Ask what a typical hour produces, and how much of it is real dialing |
| Monthly retainer | A set fee for a managed program | You pay the same in a month with poor lists or a seasonal slowdown |
| Per appointment or per qualified lead | You pay when a result is delivered | The definition of "qualified" is everything. Agree in writing what counts, and what happens when the prospect does not show up |
| Per lead | You pay for contacts that meet a basic test | Contacts are not appointments. Check how many conversations it takes to get one |
| Software and DIY tools | You buy the list, dialer or coaching and make the calls | Your labor is the main cost, so the price tag understates the total |

## Costs That Do Not Appear on the Invoice

The fee is only part of what outsourcing costs. Add these before you compare it with making the calls yourself.

- **The list.** Someone has to provide it, and it has to be current. Expired, FSBO and other lists go stale quickly. If the service does not supply the list, that is a cost on your side.
- **Dialer, CRM and other tools.** Callers need to log what happens. Check whether their access to your CRM comes with an added seat cost.
- **Script writing and training.** A caller can only follow the script you give them. Writing, testing and revising it takes your time. The [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) is a good starting point for the scripts.
- **Management and call review.** You still need to listen to calls, give feedback and handle the handoff of every prospect. Agents and investors who describe outsourcing their calls in forums often say the same thing: a caller who reads a script without adapting it to the prospect can produce appointments that go nowhere, which is why they review calls themselves. Treat those comments as stories, not data.
- **Handoff and follow-up.** An appointment that no one prepared you for is harder to turn into a client. Someone also has to follow up with people who said "not now."
- **The cost of a poor first impression.** A caller represents your name. Prospects who hear an uneasy or generic pitch may not take your next call.

## The Compliance Question to Ask Any Provider

Calling consumers is regulated, and hiring someone else to dial does not make the rules go away. The FTC's Q&A for telemarketers and sellers says it is against the law for a seller [to call, or cause a telemarketer to call](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/qa-telemarketers-sellers-about-dnc-provisions-tsr), a number in an area code unless the seller has first subscribed to and accessed the part of the National Do Not Call Registry for that area code. The same page says that anyone required to use the registry must synchronize their lists with an updated version at least every 31 days.

This is general information, not legal advice, so check with your broker and your state's rules. The practical takeaway is to ask any provider, in writing, how they scrub lists, which registry versions they use, how they record opt-out requests and who is responsible if a number is called in error. The guide to [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains what applies to common lead types.

## A Cost Worksheet You Can Fill In

Use your own numbers. The goal is a cost per appointment held, because an appointment that does not happen is a cost with no result.

| Line | Outsourced | Calling yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly fees (service, VA or ISA pay) | (your number) | $0 |
| List and data | (your number) | (your number) |
| Dialer, CRM and tool costs | (your number) | (your number) |
| Hours of your own time each month (calling, management, call review) | (your hours) | (your hours) |
| Value of one of your hours | (your number) | (your number) |
| **Total monthly cost** = fees + list + tools + (hours × hourly value) | (your number) | (your number) |
| Appointments held each month | (your number) | (your number) |
| **Cost per appointment held** = total monthly cost ÷ appointments held | (your number) | (your number) |

To estimate the value of one of your hours, divide the income you want to earn in a year by the hours you actually work, and use that figure for both columns. To fill in the "appointments held" line for the option you have not tried yet, use the [prospecting calculator](/tools/prospecting-calculator/) with your own conversion rates, or ask a provider for the results they have delivered to agents in your market and verify them.

## Outsourced Calls vs. Calling Yourself: The Trade-Offs

| Factor | Outsourced calling | Calling yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Frees your hours for appointments | Calls take a block of your day |
| Cost structure | Predictable fee, or a fee tied to results | Your time, plus list and tools |
| Control of the message | You supply the script and the caller delivers it | You adapt the words as the prospect talks |
| Learning | You hear what happens only if you review calls | You hear every objection directly |
| Speed to start | A provider may start within days of signing up | You start today, but your skills grow with practice |
| Risk | You depend on the provider's quality and compliance | You depend on your own consistency |
| Scalability | Easier to add volume | Limited by your hours |

## When to Outsource and When to Make the Calls Yourself

Outsourcing tends to fit best when you already know your scripts work, your lead conversion is solid and your calendar is full of appointments you cannot get to. It also fits teams that already have a manager who reviews calls and a CRM that records the handoff.

Calling yourself tends to fit best when you are new to the work, when you are still finding the opening line and the answers that work in your market, or when each prospect is valuable enough that you want the first conversation to be yours. If you hate the calls, the better question is whether the discomfort is about the work or the preparation. The guides on [why people hate cold calling](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) and [overcoming call reluctance](/blog/how-to-overcome-call-reluctance/) cover the second case.

A middle path is also common. Some agents hand off list building and first-touch voicemails and keep the live conversations, or they hire an ISA and train that person on their own scripts. Others keep the calls and add live prompts to make them easier. ## Questions to Ask Before You Hire a Service or VA

- Who will call, and how are they trained on real estate conversations?
- Do I own the script, and can I change it whenever I like?
- What counts as a qualified lead or appointment, and what happens if the person does not show up?
- How do you scrub lists against the National Do Not Call Registry, and who is responsible for errors?
- Can I listen to recordings or samples of calls, and how often?
- What reports will I get, and what do they show beyond dials?
- How do I end the arrangement, and are there minimum terms?
- Who owns the contact data and the call recordings?

## FAQ

**How much does a real estate cold calling service cost?**
It depends on the pricing model: hourly, a monthly retainer, a fee per appointment or per lead. Because published rates differ and change, use the worksheet above and ask for the full cost in writing, including anything that is billed separately.

**Does cold calling still work for real estate agents?**
It works as one channel, and results depend on the list, the opening, the follow-up and the number of conversations you hold. Track your own results rather than relying on anyone's averages. The [prospecting plan](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-plan/) shows how.

**Can a virtual assistant make cold calls for real estate agents?**
Yes, agents do hire VAs for dialing and first contact. It helps to give the VA a clear script, a defined list and a qualification checklist, and to review calls regularly.

**What is the alternative to hiring a cold calling service?**
Make the calls yourself with a daily calling block, a targeted list and a script you practice. The [real estate prospecting strategies](/blog/real-estate-prospecting-strategies/) post compares other prospecting methods that do not involve calling strangers.
