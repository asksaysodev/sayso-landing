A good FSBO script is a short, honest conversation in four moves: say why you are calling, ask about the seller's plan, offer something useful that does not depend on them hiring you, and ask for one specific next step such as a 15-minute meeting. It does not ask for the listing on the first call. The complete call script below shows how that sounds from hello to the ask, with the replies you will hear along the way.

After the script you will find three more scripts based on how long the home has been listed, a 60-second pre-call research habit, what to say to the objections that come up on almost every call, and voicemail, text and email versions. The scripts follow the Open-Bridge-Close framework from our complete [real estate cold call scripts](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) guide, adapted for the [FSBO](/glossary/fsbo/) conversation.

## The Complete FSBO Call Script

Every agent calling FSBOs opens with some version of "I work with buyers in your area," and the seller has heard it several times that week. This call starts somewhere else: it asks about the seller's plan, uses what they say to offer something concrete, and ends with a time on the calendar. If you are new to the term, the [FSBO glossary entry](/glossary/fsbo/) covers what it means for sellers, buyers and agents.

<ScriptExample label="Complete FSBO Call, Start to Finish">
**1. Open (about 10 seconds)**

Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner, and I'm not calling to talk you out of that. Do you have a minute?

**2. Ask about their plan (30 to 60 seconds, and then listen)**

If everything goes the way you want, where are you moving to next, and when would you like to be there? ... How has it been going so far, any showings or offers? ... How did you land on your price?

**3. Offer something useful that does not depend on hiring you**

That helps, thank you. Whether or not we ever work together, I can send you the last three homes near you that sold, with the price and how long each took, so you can check your price against real numbers. What's the best number or email for that?

**4. Ask for one specific next step**

If those numbers look different from what you expected, would it make sense to sit down for 15 minutes and go through them together? I have Thursday at 4 or Friday at 10, which is easier?

**5. Confirm and end**

Great, I'll send the sales to you today and put Thursday at 4 on my calendar. If anything changes before then, my number is [Number].
</ScriptExample>

Each step has one job. The opener lowers the seller's guard, the questions give you their real motivation and timeline, the offer gives them a reason to keep talking, and the two-time ask makes the decision about when to meet instead of whether to meet. If the seller will not give you a meeting, settle for the weaker version of step 4, permission to send the sales and call back in five days, and log it.

### What They Say Next: Branches for the Call

| If the seller says | What it usually means | Say this |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not working with agents." | A protective reflex, usually about commission | "That makes sense, and I'm not asking you to. Would it help if I sent you what the last three homes on your street sold for so you can check your price?" |
| "Do you have a buyer?" | They want a sale, not a pitch | "I'm working with a few people looking in [Neighborhood]. Before I send anyone your way, what price and timing would make a sale work for you?" |
| "How much is your commission?" | A price check before they invest more time | Use the net-proceeds reframe in the objections section below, or the full guide to [the commission question](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/). |
| "We're doing fine on our own." | Confidence that has not been tested yet | "Good to hear. Are the offers coming from pre-approved buyers, or mostly lookers?" |
| "Send me something." | A polite brush-off, or real interest | "Happy to. What's the best email, and what would be most useful: recent sales near you or what buyers are asking for in [Neighborhood]?" |
| "I'm busy right now." | Bad timing, not rejection | "No problem. Would later today or tomorrow around [Time] be better for a five-minute call?" |

### Which FSBO Script to Use When

| Seller stage | Script | Goal of the call |
|---|---|---|
| Listed 1 to 2 weeks | The Neighborhood Expert | Permission to send a market snapshot and call back |
| Listed 2 to 4 weeks | The Open House Offer | Get inside the home |
| Listed 60+ days | The Market Update | A 15-minute meeting to review the numbers |

## Do 60 Seconds of Homework Before You Dial

Agents who get a second conversation with a FSBO seller rarely call blind. They spend one minute on the listing before dialing. This is the same principle behind the diagnostic approach in our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) guide: preparation changes your tone from "salesperson" to "someone who actually looked."

Here is what to check:

**Asking price vs. comparable sales.** Pull two or three recent comps in the same neighborhood. If the FSBO is priced 10-15% above comps, you already know the conversation. That gap is your opening.

**Listing photos and description.** Spend 10 seconds scanning their Zillow, Realtor.com, or yard sign listing. Dark photos, no staging, a two-sentence description. These are specific things you can offer to improve without sounding like you are pitching.

**How long it has been listed.** A FSBO in its first week is in a completely different emotional state than one that has been sitting for two months with no offers. Your script needs to match that state.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
When you can reference something specific about the listing on the call, you immediately separate yourself from every agent who is reading a generic script. "I noticed your home is priced about 8% above the recent sale on Maple Street" beats "I work with buyers in your area" every single time.
</CalloutBox>

## FSBO Scripts Matched to Seller Stage

A seller who listed yesterday and a seller who listed two months ago need different conversations. Fresh FSBOs are confident. Established FSBOs are starting to question their strategy. Stale FSBOs are frustrated and quietly open to help. Your FSBO script should meet them where they are.

### Fresh FSBOs (First 1-2 Weeks): The Neighborhood Expert

This seller just put the sign in the yard. They are excited, optimistic, and not interested in hearing why they need an agent. Do not pitch. Offer value and plant a seed for call two.

<ScriptExample label="Fresh FSBO: The Neighborhood Expert">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home on [Street] just went up for sale. I'm not calling to ask for the listing. I sell a lot in [Neighborhood] and I keep a list of every active buyer I'm working with. If any of them are a fit, would you be open to me bringing them by? Either way, I can send you a quick market snapshot for your street so you know exactly what comparable homes are going for. Would that be helpful?
</ScriptExample>

The goal on this call is not the [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/). It is earning permission to follow up. If they say yes to the market snapshot, you now have a reason to call back in five days with data, and that second call is where the real conversation starts.

### Established FSBOs (2-4 Weeks): The Open House Offer

By week two or three, the initial excitement has faded. They have had a few showings, maybe some lowball offers, maybe nothing at all. They are starting to wonder if this is harder than they thought but not ready to admit it.

<ScriptExample label="Established FSBO: The Open House Offer">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I noticed your home on [Street] has been on the market for a few weeks. How has traffic been? I ask because I've been working with several buyers in [Neighborhood], and I'd love to host an open house at your property this weekend at no cost to you. It gets more eyes on your home, and if one of my buyers ends up being the right fit, we can talk about how that would work. Would you be open to that?
</ScriptExample>

The open house offer works because it costs the seller nothing and solves their biggest problem: getting more people through the door. It also puts you inside the home, where you can demonstrate your expertise in person. Many agents find the open house conversation goes further than a first call alone.

**Follow-up tip:** After the open house, call within 24 hours with specific feedback. "Three of the eight groups mentioned the kitchen felt dark. A simple staging tweak could fix that. Want me to walk you through what I'd recommend?" You are now consulting, not selling.

### Stale FSBOs (60+ Days): The Market Update

After two months, most FSBO sellers fall into one of two camps: quietly frustrated or stubbornly committed. Either way, the dozens of agents who called in week one have moved on. The competition is gone. That is your advantage.

<ScriptExample label="Stale FSBO: The Market Update Re-Engagement">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Your home on [Street] has been on the market for a while now, and I wanted to share something. Two homes on your street have sold since you listed, one at [Price] and one at [Price]. The market has shifted a bit since you started, and your home's position looks different now than it did eight weeks ago. Would it be worth a 15-minute conversation to look at the numbers together?
</ScriptExample>

This works for the same reason the warm expired script works: you are not saying "you failed, let me fix it." You are saying the market has changed, and a fresh look at the data is worth 15 minutes. It gives the seller a face-saving reason to re-engage.

Stale FSBOs convert on call two or three, not call one. Log the conversation in your CRM and follow up in seven days with another data point. The agent who stays in touch wins when they are finally ready. ### "I Don't Want to Pay Commission"

This is the number one FSBO objection, and it is logical. They listed FSBO specifically to save 5-6%. Do not argue about your value. Reframe the math.

*"I hear you, that's actually the main reason most people go FSBO. Here's the question though: if working with me nets you $30,000 more after commission than selling on your own, would that be worth a conversation? I can show you the exact numbers for your street."*

The key is making it about their net, not your fee. For more scripts on this specific objection, see our guide to [handling "how much is your commission"](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/).

### "I'm Doing Fine on My Own"

If they genuinely are doing fine, respect it. But ask one question that plants a seed.

*"That's great to hear. Quick question: are you getting offers from pre-approved buyers, or mostly lookers? Because that's usually where the process stalls for sellers handling it themselves, separating the serious buyers from the curious ones."*

This highlights a specific pain point, qualifying buyers, without insulting their effort. If they admit traffic has been mostly "lookers," you are in a real conversation.

### "I've Already Had a Dozen Agents Call Me"

This is fatigue, not rejection. Acknowledge it and differentiate immediately.

*"I bet you have, and I'm sure most of them opened with 'I have buyers in your area.' I actually called because I looked at your listing and noticed [specific detail from your pre-call research]. Can I share what I'd do differently with the marketing if you're open to it?"*

<CalloutBox type="tip">
The pre-call research above is what makes this objection response work. If you cannot reference something specific about their listing, you sound like caller number thirteen. If you can, you sound like the only agent who did their homework.
</CalloutBox>

When a seller shuts down completely, do not push. A polite exit that leaves the door open is better than burning the lead. For scripts on [handling "not interested"](/objections/not-interested/), see our objection response library.

## Voicemail, Text and Email Versions

Plan the first touch as a voicemail plus a text rather than counting on a live answer. Check the [Do Not Call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) before you dial or text a list of owners. Keep every version short and built around the same offer as the call: recent sales near their home, no strings attached.

<ScriptExample label="FSBO Voicemail (Under 20 Seconds)">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your home on [Street]. I'm not leaving a sales pitch here, I just pulled the last few sales near you and thought they'd be useful for checking your price. My number is [Number], and I'll text you the details too.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="FSBO Text">
Hi [Name], [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner. I put together the last three sales nearby, with price and days on market, in case it's useful. Want me to send them over?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="FSBO Email After the Call">
Subject: The three sales near [Street]

Hi [Name], thanks for talking with me today. Here are the three homes near yours that sold recently, with the price and how long each took: [details]. One thing that stood out to me is [specific observation]. Does Thursday at 4 still work to go through them together?
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptSheet title="FSBO call sheet">

**First call, any stage:** "I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner, and I'm not calling to talk you out of that. If everything goes the way you want, where are you moving to next and when?" Then: "Whether or not we work together, can I send you the last three sales near you?" Then: "Thursday at 4 or Friday at 10 to go through them?"

**Fresh FSBO, 1 to 2 weeks:** "I sell a lot in [Neighborhood] and keep a list of active buyers. Can I send you a quick market snapshot for your street?"

**Established FSBO, 2 to 4 weeks:** "How has traffic been? I'd love to host an open house at your property this weekend at no cost to you. Would you be open to that?"

**"I don't want to pay commission":** "If working with me nets you more after commission than selling on your own, would that be worth a conversation? I can show you the numbers for your street."

**"I'm doing fine on my own":** "Are you getting offers from pre-approved buyers, or mostly lookers?"

**"A dozen agents have called me":** "I called because I looked at your listing and noticed [specific detail]. Can I share what I'd do differently with the marketing?"

</ScriptSheet>

## FAQ

**How many times should you follow up with a FSBO?**
A workable plan is at least five touches over 21 days, because many FSBO sellers are not ready to talk on the first call. Space your touches across calls, texts, and emails, each adding new value like a comp update or market shift. The agent who stays consistent wins when the seller's confidence dips. For a complete framework, see our [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) guide, which covers multi-touch nurture sequences.

**What is the best time to call FSBO sellers?**
There is no single time that works everywhere. Many agents try weekday evenings and Saturday mornings, when owners are more likely to be home, then track their own contact rates for two weeks and keep the window that works. Calling on day three or four of a new listing, instead of the first 48 hours, can also get you a less rushed conversation.

**Are FSBO leads worth pursuing?**
Yes. In its [2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/fsbos-reach-all-time-low-more-sellers-rely-on-agents), NAR reported that FSBO sales were 5% of home sales and that 91% of sellers used an agent. NAR also reported a median FSBO sale price of $360,000 against $425,000 for agent-assisted sales, though those are different homes and not a like-for-like comparison. Sellers who list on their own have a home to sell and a timeline to meet, so your job is to show how your pricing and marketing improve their net. FSBOs sit alongside [expired listings](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) as one of the more common outbound lead sources for listing agents.

**What is the best script to convince a FSBO to list with you?**
A script that tries to convince the seller usually fails on the first call. The one that works best asks about their plan, offers recent sales, and asks for a short meeting, as in the complete call script above. The decision to list tends to come after the seller sees numbers that differ from their expectations.

**How do I find FSBO leads?**
Check Zillow's "For Sale By Owner" filter, Realtor.com, Craigslist, and Facebook Marketplace daily. Drive your farm area and note yard signs without agent branding. Paid lead services also aggregate FSBO listings, which can save manual searching time. Whatever source you use, check the [Do Not Call rules](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) before dialing.
