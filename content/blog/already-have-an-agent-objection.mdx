"I already have an agent" sounds like one objection. It is four. A signed buyer, a cousin who just got licensed, a seller who used someone in 2019, and a prospect who wants off the phone all say the exact same words. Your response has to change depending on which one you are talking to.

Roughly 7 out of 10 prospects who say this have never signed anything. If you only need the three core responses, the [quick answer for "I already have an agent"](/objections/already-have-an-agent/) has them in one page. This post is the long version: why prospects say it, how to tell the four situations apart, what to say in each one, and when to let it go. For the framework behind all objection work, see our guide to [real estate objection handling](/blog/real-estate-objection-handling/).

## Why Prospects Say It

Prospects reach for "I already have an agent" for three reasons, and only one of them is about the agent.

**It ends the call fast.** No explanation needed, no follow-up question expected. It is the most socially acceptable exit line in real estate.

**They feel loyal to someone.** A friend, a relative, someone from church. They have not hired that person, but saying yes to you would feel like a betrayal.

**They are committed.** They signed a buyer agreement or a listing agreement. This group is smaller than most agents assume, but it exists, and it deserves respect.

Your job in the first exchange is not to overcome anything. It is to find out which reason you are hearing. One calm question does that. Everything after depends on the answer.

## Situation 1: They Signed With an Agent

This is the one situation where you do not compete. A signed agreement is a commitment, and pushing against it makes you the agent they warn their friends about. Your goals are to confirm it, learn when it ends, and leave on terms that make you the first call if it falls apart.

The signal: they answer the agreement question without hesitating. "Yes, we signed last month." Specifics like dates and names mean the commitment exists.

<ScriptExample label="Signed Agreement: Confirm and Set the Follow-Up">
Good, then you're in the right hands and I won't get in the way. Out of curiosity, how long does your agreement run? I'd love to check back once you're settled, just to see how it went.
</ScriptExample>

Do not ask about their experience in the same breath. You already have the date. Log it, set the reminder, and get off the phone in under a minute. Short and respectful is what they remember.

## Situation 2: A Friend or Family Member in the Business

This is the most common version and the hardest to move, because the loyalty is personal. "My sister-in-law is an agent." You will never win an argument against a sister-in-law. Do not try.

What you can do is separate the relationship from the job. Many people with a friend in the business have not talked to that friend about this move. Some are not sure the friend works their area or their price range. A few are dreading the awkwardness of mixing family and money.

<ScriptExample label="Friend or Family: Separate the Relationship From the Job">
That's great, it helps to have someone you trust. Is she active in [Area], or is she more on the other side of town? I only ask because a lot of people I talk to love their friend in the business but end up wanting someone who works their neighborhood every day.
</ScriptExample>

If the answer is "she's right here and she's great," stop. You are done. If the answer is "she's mostly in [Other City]" or "we haven't talked about it yet," you have an opening. Ask about their move, not about the friend.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Never say anything about the friend's skill, volume, or experience. You are asking about fit, not ability. The moment it sounds like a knock on someone they love, the call is over.
</CalloutBox>

## Situation 3: A Past Agent They Used Before

"We'll probably use the agent who sold us this house." This person has history, not a commitment. Often the last conversation with that agent was years ago. Sometimes they cannot remember the agent's last name.

The move here is time. Ask when they last heard from that agent, then let the answer do the work.

<ScriptExample label="Past Agent: Ask About the Gap">
Makes sense to go back to someone who did a good job for you. When was the last time you two talked? I ask because the market around you looks pretty different from when you bought, and I'd want whoever you use to have that fresh.
</ScriptExample>

If they talked last month, respect it and offer a market update as a backup. If the answer is "huh, it's been a while," they just told you the relationship is a memory. Move to their timeline and what is prompting the move.

## Situation 4: The Brush-Off

There is no agent. There might have been an open house conversation once. "I already have an agent" is just the exit. You can spot it when the answer to the agreement question is vague: "We're good," "Yeah, someone," or a quick change of subject.

Do not call the bluff. Give them an easy way to stay on the line instead.

<ScriptExample label="Brush-Off: Lower the Stakes">
No problem at all, I'm not trying to step on anyone's toes. Quick one so I know whether to bother you again: are you thinking about a move this year, or is that further out?
</ScriptExample>

A timeline answer means the conversation is still alive. Keep asking about their situation. A second deflection means stop. Offer a no-strings market update and end the call warm. The tone matters more than the script here, which is the whole point of [how to not sound salesy on real estate calls](/blog/how-to-not-sound-salesy/).

## Decision Table: Read It, Respond, Know When to Stop

| What they said | What it usually means | Your response | When to back off |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| "We signed with someone last month." | Committed. A written agreement exists. | Confirm, ask when the agreement runs out, log it. | Right away. Do not pitch. Follow up after the end date. |
| "My brother-in-law is an agent." | Personal loyalty, not always a working relationship. | Ask if they work your area and whether they have talked about this move yet. | When they confirm the friend is local and already on it. |
| "We'll use the agent from last time." | History, often years old. | Ask when they last talked and point out how the market has changed since. | When the contact is recent and they sound happy. |
| "Yeah, we have someone." (vague) | A brush-off. Usually no agreement. | Lower the stakes and ask about their timeline. | After a second deflection. Offer a market update and end warm. |
| "We have someone, but they've been slow." | Unhappy, and hinting at it. | Ask what has been missing. Listen. Say nothing about the other agent. | If they shut it down with "it's fine." Respect it. |

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Backing off is part of the script, not a failure of it. The agent who knows when to stop gets the callback when the signed deal falls through, the friend goes quiet, or the old agent never calls back.
</CalloutBox>

## Example Call: The Past-Agent Version

Here is a full call. The lead is a homeowner from a circle prospecting list. They own a three-bedroom bought six years ago.

**You:** Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling a few homeowners on Maple Street because a house two doors down just went under contract. Have you thought at all about what your place might be worth now?

**Lead:** We've thought about it. But we already have an agent.

**You:** That's great. Who are you working with?

**Lead:** The woman who sold us this house. Linda something. She was great.

**You:** It's good to go back to someone who did a good job for you. When was the last time you two talked?

**Lead:** Probably when we closed. So six years.

**You:** Got it. Six years is a long time in this market. Are you thinking about a move this year, or is that further out?

**Lead:** Maybe spring. Our youngest starts middle school and we want to be in a different district.

**You:** That makes sense. Spring comes up quickly once school is involved. Do you know yet whether you'd buy in the new district before you sell, or sell first?

**Lead:** No idea. That's kind of what's stressing us out.

**You:** That's the most common worry I hear, and it's solvable. Here's what I'd suggest. Reach out to Linda, she knows you. If you'd also like a second opinion on what Maple Street homes are selling for now and how the timing could work, I could walk you through it in 15 minutes. No obligation either way. Would Tuesday evening or Thursday afternoon be easier?

**Lead:** Thursday's fine. I'll probably still call Linda.

**You:** You should. I'll send you the Maple Street sale numbers before Thursday so you have them for either conversation.

The agent never argued with the loyalty. One question about the last contact showed the relationship was six years old. The timeline question surfaced the problem, which was buying and selling at the same time. The close offered value that works even if they go back to Linda, so a yes cost the lead nothing. Log the school timeline and the buy/sell concern in your CRM before you dial the next number.

## Following Up After a Back-Off

Every back-off in the table above needs a date attached. Signed agreements expire. Friends in the business get busy. Old agents leave the industry. The prospects who told you no today are a follow-up list, not a dead list, if you log what they said and when to call back. ## FAQ

**What do you say when the prospect's agent is a friend or family member?**
Do not compete with the relationship. Ask whether the friend works in their area and whether they have talked about this move yet. If the friend is local and already involved, back off and stay in touch. If not, ask about their move, not the friend.

**What if they plan to use the agent from their last purchase?**
Ask when they last talked to that agent. If it has been years, the relationship is history, not a commitment, and you can move to their timeline. If they talked recently and are happy, offer a market update as a backup and end the call.

**When should you stop pushing after "I already have an agent"?**
Stop right away if they confirm a signed agreement. Stop after a second deflection from a vague brush-off. Stop when a friend or past agent is local, recent, and doing the job. In every case, log the details and set a follow-up date.

**What should you do when a prospect says their current agent has been slow?**
Ask what has been missing and listen. Do not say anything about the other agent. If they keep talking, you have an opening to ask about their timeline. If they close it down with "it's fine," respect that and follow up later.
