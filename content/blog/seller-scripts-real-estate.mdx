A good seller script for real estate does four things in order: it gives a one-sentence reason for the call, asks why the owner is thinking about selling, learns the timeline and what they expect for a price, and then offers a short visit to see the home and give a realistic price range. Everything in a seller conversation is built to lock in that visit, ideally today or tomorrow, and not to win the listing on the first call.

This post gives you the core seller script with branches for what the seller says next, the questions to ask in order, scripts for the seller situations that come up most often, answers to the common seller objections, and a call sheet you can print. The scripts work whether the seller reached out first or you called them, and they pair with the lead-source posts linked below.

## The Seller Script for Real Estate Agents to Start With

If you use only one script, use this one. It works for a homeowner who asked about their home's value, a past lead you are calling back, or a neighbor you reached through a recent sale. Only the reason in the first sentence changes.

<ScriptExample label="Seller conversation: the core call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your home on [Street]. [Give one reason: you asked about its value, a home nearby just sold, or I am working with someone who wants to be in your neighborhood.]

What has you thinking about selling?

What would moving let you do that you can't do now?

When would you ideally like to be moved?

Have you thought about what price you would want, and how did you come up with that number?

Have you spoken with any other agents about selling?

Based on what you have told me, the next step is for me to take a look at the home and show you what buyers are paying for homes like yours. Would tomorrow or Saturday work better?
</ScriptExample>

What the seller says next decides which branch to use.

- **If they say** "I'm just curious what it's worth": say "Totally fair. Most people who ask are thinking about selling, thinking about refinancing, or checking whether they have built equity. Which one is it for you?"
- **If they say** "Maybe in a few months": say "That helps. What's driving that timeline?" Then ask "If you are a few months out, this is a good time to look at it, because decisions about condition and prep now can change your result later. Can we spend 20 minutes on it?"
- **If they say** "We haven't decided anything": say "That's okay. If you did decide, where would you go next?" If they have no answer, they are not a seller yet, so offer a market update and a date to check in. Do not spend 30 minutes on a call that has no motivation.
- **If they say** "Just email me a number": use the online estimate branch in the situations below.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Ask for the reason before you give any information. A seller who says "we want something bigger" has told you what they want. The question that follows ("what's changing for you?") tells you why, and the third question about what happens if nothing changes tells you how soon.
</CalloutBox>

## The Seller Questions to Ask, in Order

Sellers answer better when the questions follow the order they would think about the move: the home, the price, the reason, other agents and the money. This is the seller version of the [LPMAMA framework](/blog/lpmama-real-estate/) (location, price, motivation, agent, mortgage, appointment).

| Step | What you want to learn | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Property details and what buyers would like | "Can you tell me about the home? How long have you lived there? What would buyers love about it? What improvements have you made?" |
| Price | Their expectation, and where it came from | "Have you thought about what price you would want if you sold? How did you come up with that number? Would it help to see what similar homes nearby have sold for?" |
| Motivation | The real reason and the timeline | "What has you thinking about selling? What would moving let you do that you can't do now? What happens if the home doesn't sell? When would you ideally move?" |
| Agent | Whether they are working with someone | "Have you spoken with any other agents about selling? Did you sign anything with anyone? What are you hoping an agent would help you with most?" |
| Money | Equity and what they hope to walk away with | "Do you still have a mortgage on the property? Do you know the approximate balance? Have you thought about how much you would like to walk away with?" |
| Next step | The appointment | "The next step is for me to see the home and show you what buyers are paying for homes like yours. What does your schedule look like later today or tomorrow?" |

You do not have to ask every question on the first call. If the seller gives you the reason and the timeline and agrees to a visit, the rest can wait for the visit. Motivation and timeline are the two answers to protect.

## Seller Scripts for Common Situations

### The Owner Who Asked for a Home Value

People who requested a value from your site often forget they did. Start with a small, easy question and let them tell you what happened.

<ScriptExample label="Seller script: home value request">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. It looks like you requested a home value on your property in [Month]. I was going through our records and wanted to make sure we did not miss you. Did you end up doing anything with the property, or are you still there?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they sold:** say "Congratulations. Just so I update our records, what month did that close?" and thank them.
- **If they still own it:** ask "What had you thinking about the value back then?" and then stay quiet and let them talk.
- **If they say** "I'm thinking about refinancing": say "Got it. When you are refinancing, the number matters a lot. Have you gotten a specific value from anyone, or are you using online estimates? Most banks send an appraiser who has not seen your upgrades. I can come by, see what actually adds value, and give you a realistic range so you know whether refinancing makes sense."
- **If they say** "I don't remember requesting that": say "No worries, it was a while back. You still own [Address], right? If someone offered you a strong price for it, would you even consider moving?" If yes, go to the core call. If no, ask "Have you checked what homes around you have sold for lately? A lot of owners are surprised."

### The Seller Who Wants a Number Right Now

Some owners ask for a price on the first call or want you to email one. A number without seeing the home is a guess, and a guess can hurt you later when the real price is lower than the one you suggested.

<ScriptExample label="Seller script: online estimates versus a visit">
I can give you a range, and the online estimates are probably off because they cannot see your condition, upgrades or layout. What I normally do is come by for 20 minutes, look at the home, and then give you a realistic number range. That way you are making a decision off facts and not guesses. Would Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning be easier?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I just need a ballpark": say "Of course. Based on the recent sales near you, homes like yours have been selling in a wide range, and where yours lands depends on condition. Seeing it takes me 20 minutes and gets you a number you can use."
- **If they say** "I don't have time for a visit": say "I understand. Can we do 15 minutes on video this evening, and then I'll come by for a walk-through when it suits you?"

### Booking the Listing Appointment

Once you have the reason, the timeline and the price expectation, ask for the meeting in a way that gives two choices and assumes there is a next step.

<ScriptExample label="Seller script: booking the visit">
Based on what you have told me, I'd like to take a look at the home and show you exactly what buyers are paying for homes like yours. I can stop by, see the property, and give you a clear price range and a plan. What does your schedule look like later today or tomorrow? And will anyone else be part of the decision?
</ScriptExample>

After they agree, confirm the details out loud: the day, the time, who will be there and the best number if anything changes. Then send a calendar invite and ask them to text you if they need to move it. A [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/) with every decision maker in the room goes better than one where you have to follow up with a spouse who was not there. For the confirmation call, use the list in [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/).

## Seller Objection Scripts

Most objections on a first seller call come down to a few, and the answers below keep the conversation moving toward the visit. Say them in your own words, and pause after the first sentence so the seller can respond.

Seller objections come fast and in no particular order. Some agents use live prompts like [Sayso's Cue](/products/cue/) to see an objection response on screen while the seller is still talking, which helps when the objection is one you did not rehearse.

### "We're Talking to Other Agents"

Do not argue with this. It is a sensible thing for a seller to do, and it tells you what they care about.

<ScriptExample label="Seller objection: talking to other agents">
That's smart, and I would do the same thing. What are the most important things you are looking for in the agent you choose? And how will you know when you have found the right one?
</ScriptExample>

The answers become your agenda for the appointment. For more on this moment, see our page on [I already have an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/).

### "What's Your Commission?"

Answer directly when asked, and tie the answer to what the seller takes home.

<ScriptExample label="Seller objection: commission">
Happy to go over it. I'd like to show you what you would net at the price we recommend, so you can compare the whole picture and not just one number. Can we put them side by side when I come by?
</ScriptExample>

The full word-for-word responses are in [how much is your commission](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/) and the post on the [commission too high objection](/blog/commission-too-high-objection/).

### "I Think My Home Is Worth More"

Agree with the goal and let the sales data do the talking.

<ScriptExample label="Seller objection: price expectation">
I hear you, and I want to get you the best result we can. What are you basing that number on? Let's look at the most recent sales near you together and see where your home fits, and then we can talk about a plan that gets you as close to your number as the market allows.
</ScriptExample>

Our [price too high](/objections/price-too-high/) page has more on this, including how to set expectations before the first showing.

### "Let Me Think About It" and "We'll Wait Until Spring"

Both of these are timing objections. Ask what specifically they want to think about, and use the answer. Responses for each are in [I need to think about it](/objections/need-to-think-about-it/) and [we'll wait until spring](/objections/well-wait-for-spring/), and the two common brush-offs, [not ready yet](/objections/not-ready-yet/) and [call me later](/objections/call-me-later/), have their own pages.

## What to Write Down After Every Seller Call

A seller call that is not logged is a lead you will have to start over with. Capture these items in your CRM before your next call.

- Why they are selling, in their own words
- Where they would move and by when
- The price they want and where it came from
- Estimated mortgage balance, if shared
- Whether they are talking to other agents, and whether they signed anything
- Everyone involved in the decision
- The appointment day, time and who attends, or the date you will follow up

<ScriptSheet title="Seller call sheet">

**Opening:** "I'm calling about your home on [Street]. [One-sentence reason.] What has you thinking about selling?"

**Motivation:** "What would moving let you do that you can't do now? What happens if the home doesn't sell? When would you ideally move?"

**Price:** "How did you come up with that number? Would it help to see what similar homes have sold for?"

**Agent:** "Have you spoken with any other agents about selling? Did you sign anything?"

**Mortgage:** "Do you still have a mortgage? Roughly what is the balance? How much would you like to walk away with?"

**Book the visit:** "I'd like to see the home and show you what buyers are paying for homes like yours. Tomorrow or Saturday?"

**"Just curious":** "Are you thinking about selling, refinancing, or checking whether you have built equity?"

**"Email me a number":** "Online estimates miss condition and upgrades. I can come by for 20 minutes and give you a realistic range."

**"Talking to other agents":** "That's smart. What are the most important things you're looking for in the agent you choose?"

**"What's your commission?":** "Happy to go over it. Let me show you what you'd net first so you can compare the whole picture."

**"My home is worth more":** "What are you basing that number on? Let's look at the recent sales together."

</ScriptSheet>

## Mistakes That Lose the Appointment

**Pitching before asking.** The seller has not told you the reason yet, so any pitch is generic. Ask first.

**Giving a price over the phone.** A guess sets an anchor that you cannot easily move once you see the home.

**Talking commission first.** Raise the value of the plan before the fee comes up, unless they ask directly. If they do, answer it directly.

**Skipping the decision makers.** Ask who else is involved and get them to the meeting.

**Offering vague times.** "Let me know when you're free" gets no reply. "Tomorrow at 4 or Saturday morning" gets an answer.
