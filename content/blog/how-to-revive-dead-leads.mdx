A dead lead in real estate is a prospect who has stopped responding to your calls, texts and emails, usually for weeks or months, without booking an appointment or becoming a client. The label describes silence, not a decision. Most dead leads are dormant: they still plan to buy or sell and simply stopped replying. A small group is dead for good, such as a wrong number or someone who asked you to stop, and those come off your list.

This post is about reviving a whole database of them, not winning one call. You will sort the pile into groups by age, last stage and source, decide which group to call first, set a cadence for each group, track the right numbers, and know when to finally archive a lead. For the words to use once someone picks up, see [what to say to old leads](/blog/what-to-say-to-old-leads/), and for the follow-up messages to send a cold lead, see [follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/). Both sit inside our larger [real estate follow up scripts](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-scripts/) guide.

## Why Revival Needs a System, Not a Blast

Most agents treat a dead database one of two ways. They ignore it, or they blast every name with the same text on a slow Tuesday. Both waste it.

The blast fails because the leads are not the same. A seller you met at their kitchen table eight months ago and a portal inquiry that never answered once do not deserve the same message, the same channel or the same hour of your day. Treat them the same and you spend your best dialing time on your worst odds.

A revival system fixes that. It answers four questions before you send anything:

- Which leads are worth a call, and which get a text?
- Who goes first?
- How many touches does each group get, and over how long?
- When do you stop?

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
The words matter on the call. The system decides which calls you make. An agent with average scripts and a sorted database will out-book an agent with great scripts dialing names in random order.
</CalloutBox>

## Step 1: Remove the Leads That Are Dead for Good

Clean before you sort. Pull these out first so they never land in a call block:

| Lead type | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Dormant | Stopped replying, never said no, number and email still work | Sort into a revival group (the rest of this post) |
| Stalled | Replied before with "not ready yet" or "call me later" | Follow up on the timeline they gave you, outside the revival push |
| Dead for good | Wrong number, bounced email, asked to be removed, or already bought or sold with someone else | Archive, and respect any request to stop contact |

If a lead asked you to stop calling, follow the [Do Not Call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) and mark them in your CRM so nobody on the team dials them by mistake.

## Step 2: Sort Dead Leads by Age, Last Stage and Source

Every dormant lead gets three tags. Add them as CRM tags or custom fields so you can filter in seconds instead of scrolling.

**Age: how long since their last reply.** Not since they came in. Since they last said anything.

- 1 to 6 months silent
- 6 to 12 months silent
- 12 to 24 months silent
- Over 24 months silent

**Last stage: how far the relationship got before it stalled.** This is the strongest signal you have, because it tells you how much you know about them.

- **Met or set:** You had an appointment, a showing or a listing presentation.
- **Talked:** You had a live phone conversation and learned their motivation, area or timeline.
- **Texted:** They replied in writing but you never got them on the phone.
- **Never responded:** A form fill and silence.

**Source: where they came from.** Source does not decide priority on its own. It decides the angle of your first touch.

- **Home valuation or seller inquiry:** Lead with an updated value.
- **Buyer portal inquiry:** Lead with a listing or sale that matches what they searched.
- **Open house or sign call:** Lead with the street or neighborhood they walked into.
- **Referral or sphere:** Lead with the person who connected you, and call, never mass-text.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
If your notes from the first conversation are thin, the "Talked" tag is worth less than it looks. Before the push, skim the notes on every Talked and Met lead and pull one specific detail into a field you can see when you dial: the street, the kid's school, the lease end date. Sorting exists to surface that detail, not only to clean the list.
</CalloutBox>

## Step 3: Build Your Revival Groups and Call Order

Combine stage and age into four groups. Call them in this order.

| Group | Who is in it | Why this order | First channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Met, set or talked, silent under 12 months. All referral and sphere leads. | You know their situation and they will remember your name | Call |
| B | Met, set or talked, silent 12 months or more. Texted, silent under 12 months. | Some history, but they need a stronger reason to re-engage | Text with a specific reason, then call |
| C | Texted, silent 12 months or more. Never responded, silent under 6 months. | Thin history; a reply is the goal before a call | Text |
| D | Never responded, silent 6 months or more | Lowest odds; keep the effort low | Email or a single text |

Group A gets your first call blocks because every connect there can become a conversation the same day. Group D never gets a full hour of dialing. If someone from Group C or D replies, they jump to the front of the line, regardless of which group they started in.

Before each block, find one reason to reach out that is specific to the group. A home that sold on their street, a rate shift, a new listing that matches their old search, a lease that was ending around now. "It's been a while" is not a reason. If you cannot find one for a lead, push them to the next day's block and find one.

## Step 4: Set the Cadence for Each Group

Each group gets a different number of touches inside the same 30 days. The higher the group, the more you call.

- **Group A:** Up to 3 call attempts, each paired with a same-day text if they do not pick up, plus one voicemail. Spread across the first three weeks.
- **Group B:** Text first. Call everyone who replies the same day. Call non-responders once in week two, then a final text in week four.
- **Group C:** Two texts and one email across the month. Call only the leads who reply.
- **Group D:** One email and one text. No call unless they reply.

The moment any lead replies, they leave the revival cadence. A reply is a live conversation, and it belongs in your normal follow-up rhythm, built around a scheduled next step. For how often to touch a lead once they are back in the pipeline, see [how often to follow up with real estate leads](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/).

## The 30-Day Dead Lead Revival Plan

Here is the whole push on one page. Run it on your sorted database and adjust the days to your calendar.

| Days | Group | Action | Channel | What to track |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | All | Remove dead-for-good leads, tag age, stage and source, build Groups A to D, pull one detail per A and B lead | CRM | Count per group, leads archived, leads with no usable contact info |
| 3–7 | A | First call block. Text the same day if no answer | Call + text | Dials, connects, conversations, appointments set |
| 3–7 | B | First text with a group-specific reason. Call every reply the same day | Text, then call | Replies, conversations |
| 8–14 | A | Second call attempt and voicemail | Call | Connects, callbacks |
| 8–14 | B | One call to every lead who did not reply | Call | Connects, conversations |
| 8–14 | C | First text, angle set by source | Text | Replies |
| 15–21 | A | Third and final call attempt | Call + text | Connects, appointments set |
| 15–21 | C | Email with one useful local update | Email | Replies, opt-outs |
| 15–21 | D | Single email with one useful local update | Email | Replies, opt-outs, bounces |
| 22–28 | B, C, D | Final text to everyone who has not replied | Text | Replies, opt-outs |
| 22–28 | All replies | Work every reply toward a booked appointment | Call | Appointments set, appointments held |
| 29–30 | All | Review results. Move replies to the active pipeline, non-responders to quarterly nurture, and archive the rest | CRM | Reply rate and appointments by group and by source |

Two rules hold the plan together. First, protect the Group A call blocks on your calendar like listing appointments. Second, never let a reply sit. A reply from a lead who was silent for a year cools off fast, so call it back the same day.

When a reply turns into a conversation, move toward a specific time to meet. The deep version of that move is in [how to close for the appointment](/blog/how-to-close-for-appointment/), and the questions that re-qualify a returning lead are in [questions to ask real estate leads](/blog/questions-to-ask-real-estate-leads/). If the reply is a stall, the [call me later](/objections/call-me-later/) response gives you the next line.

## What to Track During a Revival Push

Track by group and by source. Totals hide the lesson.

- **Connect rate:** live conversations divided by dials, per group. Tells you whether your phone data is any good.
- **Reply rate:** replies divided by leads contacted, per group and per source. Tells you which groups deserve calls next time.
- **Appointments set and held:** the only number that pays you. Count both, because a set that never holds was not a revival.
- **Opt-outs and bounces:** if one group or source throws off a lot of them, the data or the message is the problem.
- **Time spent per group:** hours dialing against appointments set. This is how you learn whether Group B deserves calls or only texts in your market.

At the end of the 30 days, write down the one group and one source that produced the most appointments per hour. Next quarter, that group goes first.

## When to Finally Archive a Dead Lead

Archiving means you stop active outreach and keep the record. It is not deleting the contact.

Archive a lead when any of these is true:

- They asked you to stop contacting them.
- Every phone number and email is bad, and you cannot find a working one.
- They bought or sold with another agent, and you have confirmed it.
- They have gone through two full revival pushes with no reply on any channel.

Everyone else who did not reply this round goes to quarterly nurture, not the archive. A lead who ignored a 30-day push may still answer a useful update in six months. Before you move a Group A or B lead to quarterly, send one last message that makes the silence easy to end.

<ScriptExample label="Last Touch Before Moving to Quarterly Nurture">
Hey [Name], I have reached out a few times and do not want to crowd your phone. I will stop texting for now and just send a quick [Area] market update every few months. If anything changes on your end, reply here and I will pick it right back up.
</ScriptExample>

That message closes the loop without guilt and tells them exactly what to expect next. Some of your best replies of the whole push come from this one.

## FAQ

**What is a dead lead in real estate?**
A dead lead is a prospect who stopped responding to your outreach, usually after weeks or months of silence, without booking an appointment. Most are dormant rather than dead, meaning they may still plan to buy or sell. Only leads with bad contact info, a request to stop contact, or a completed purchase or sale elsewhere are dead for good.

**How do you organize a dead lead database before reaching out?**
Remove the leads that are dead for good, then tag every remaining lead by age (time since last reply), last stage (met, talked, texted or never responded) and source. Combine stage and age into priority groups so your call time goes to the leads you know best and who will remember you.

**Which dead leads should you call first?**
Start with leads you met or spoke with live who went silent within the last 12 months, plus every referral and sphere lead. You know their situation and they will recognize your name, so each connect can turn into a conversation. Leads who never responded at all get a text or email, not a call block.

**When should you archive a dead lead?**
Archive when they ask you to stop, when every number and email is bad, when they transacted with another agent, or after two full revival pushes with no reply on any channel. Leads who simply did not reply this round go to quarterly nurture instead.

**What should you track when reviving dead leads?**
Track connect rate, reply rate, appointments set and held, opt-outs and time spent, broken out by group and by source. The goal is to learn which group and source produce the most appointments per hour, so that group goes first in your next push.
