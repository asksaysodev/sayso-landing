LPMAMA is a six-part qualifying framework for real estate calls: Location, Price, Motivation, Agent, Mortgage, and Appointment. You ask one or two questions from each part, in roughly that order, so that by the end of the call you know where the lead wants to be, what they can afford, why they are moving, who else is involved, whether the money is real, and when you are meeting.

This framework sits at the center of our guide on [how to talk to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/), and the script below is the one to keep in front of you. It works for buyers and sellers, and each part ends with what to do when the lead gives you a short or unexpected answer.

<ScriptExample label="The LPMAMA Script (Buyer or Seller)">
Location: "Tell me about the area you're focused on. What draws you there?" (Seller: "Tell me about the home. How long have you lived there?")

Price: "What monthly payment feels comfortable for you?" (Seller: "Have you thought about what price you'd want if you sold?")

Motivation: "What has you thinking about making a move right now?"

Agent: "Are you working with an agent yet, or still gathering information?"

Mortgage: "Have you had a chance to talk with a lender yet?" (Seller: "Do you still have a mortgage on the property?")

Appointment: "The best next step is a quick sit-down to map this out. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?"
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We're just looking": say "Totally fair, most people start there. What would have to change for looking to turn into moving?"
- **If they say** "Can you just send me some listings?": say "Happy to. So I send the right ones, what's got you thinking about a move right now?"
- **If they say** "I don't know our price range yet": say "That's normal. Is the number you have in mind based on a lender conversation or more of a comfort level?"
- **If they say** "We already have an agent": say "Good to know. Did you sign anything with them, or have you mostly been talking?"
- **If they say** "Why do we need to meet?": say "So I can build a plan around your timeline instead of guessing. It usually takes 20 minutes."

## How to Use LPMAMA Without Sounding Like a Form

The fastest way to ruin LPMAMA is to read it top to bottom like a mortgage application. Leads can hear a checklist, and the moment they do, their answers get shorter. Treat the six letters as a map of what you need to know, and let the conversation decide the order.

In practice, most leads bring up Location or Price first because that is what they are thinking about. Answer them, then use what they said to move to the next letter. If a buyer says "We want to be closer to the school," that is a Motivation answer hiding inside a Location answer, so follow it. The goal is to cover all six by the end of the call, not in sequence.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
React to every answer before you ask the next question. A short "That makes sense" or "Tell me more about that" turns six questions into one conversation and keeps the lead talking.
</CalloutBox>

## The LPMAMA Questions, Letter by Letter

### L: Location

For buyers, you want to know where they want to live and why that place matters. For sellers, Location means the property itself: its condition, history, and what buyers will love about it.

- **Buyer:** "What areas are you most interested in, and what draws you there?"
- **Seller:** "Tell me about the home. What would buyers love about it?"
- **Natural follow-up:** "If the perfect home showed up just outside that area, would you consider it?" (Seller: "What improvements have you made since you bought it?")
- **Write down:** the areas, the reason behind each one (commute, schools, family nearby), and any hard limits. For sellers, note beds, baths, upgrades, and anything they worry could hurt the price.

### P: Price

Buyers think in monthly payments more than list prices, so start there. Sellers usually have a number in their head, and you want to hear it early so it does not surprise you at the [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/).

- **Buyer:** "What monthly payment feels comfortable for you?"
- **Seller:** "Have you thought about what price you'd want if you sold?"
- **Natural follow-up:** "Is that number based on a lender conversation or more of a comfort level?" (Seller: "How did you come up with that number?")
- **Write down:** the range, where it came from, and how flexible it is. A buyer who says "there's some wiggle room" and a buyer who says "not a dollar over" need different plans.

### M: Motivation

Motivation is the letter that matters most, and it is the one agents rush. The first answer is almost always the surface reason. Keep asking until you reach the reason that creates a timeline. Sayso's playbook calls these the three layers: surface, emotional, and consequential.

<ScriptExample label="Motivation in Three Layers">
Surface: "What has you thinking about making a move right now?"

Emotional: "What would that change for you?"

Consequential: "What happens if nothing changes in the next few months?"
</ScriptExample>

- **Write down:** all three layers in the lead's own words, plus the timeline the last one reveals. "Want something bigger" is a surface note. "Baby due in four months, lease ends in June" is a note you can plan around.

### A: Agent

This question protects your time and avoids stepping on another agent's client. Ask it plainly and without judgment. If they are already represented and have signed an agreement, respect it and keep the relationship friendly.

- **Buyer:** "Are you working with an agent yet, or still gathering information?"
- **Seller:** "Have you spoken with any other agents about selling?"
- **Natural follow-up:** "Did you sign any type of agreement with them?" then "What has that experience been like so far?"
- **Write down:** the agent's name if they share it, whether anything is signed, and what the lead liked or disliked. For more on this moment, see our script for when a lead says they [already have an agent](/objections/already-have-an-agent/).

### M: Mortgage

For buyers, Mortgage confirms the money is real. For sellers, it tells you how much equity they have and what they need to walk away with, which shapes every pricing conversation later.

- **Buyer:** "Have you had a chance to talk with a lender yet, or is that something I can help with?"
- **Seller:** "Do you still have a mortgage on the property? Do you know the approximate balance?"
- **Natural follow-up:** "Do you have a lender you trust, or would a recommendation help?" (Seller: "Have you thought about how much you'd like to walk away with?")
- **Write down:** cash or finance, pre-approval status and lender name, or the seller's rough balance and net goal.

### A: Appointment

Everything before this letter is what earns the meeting. Offer two specific times, as close to today as possible, because meetings set several days out get rescheduled more often. If the lead is not ready, lock in a specific next step instead of ending with "I'll follow up."

- **Buyer:** "Let's sit down and map out exactly what you're looking for. Does this afternoon or tomorrow work better?"
- **Seller:** "The next step is for me to see the home and show you what buyers are paying for homes like yours. Would tomorrow or Saturday work better?"
- **Write down:** the date, time, location, and every decision maker who will attend. If there is no meeting, write the exact next step and when it happens.

## FAQ

**What does LPMAMA stand for in real estate?**
LPMAMA stands for Location, Price, Motivation, Agent, Mortgage, and Appointment. It is a qualifying framework agents use on calls with buyer and seller leads to understand the full situation before they give advice or book a meeting.

**Does LPMAMA work for sellers as well as buyers?**
Yes. The letters stay the same, but the questions shift. For sellers, Location covers the property itself, Price covers their expected sale price, and Mortgage covers their balance and how much they want to walk away with.

**Do I have to ask the LPMAMA questions in order?**
No. The order is a guide, and most good calls jump around. Follow what the lead brings up, and make sure all six letters are covered before you hang up. Motivation deserves the most time wherever it lands.

**What is the most important letter in LPMAMA?**
Motivation. It tells you how serious the lead is and reveals the real timeline. Go past the first answer by asking why it matters and what happens if nothing changes. For the full question library, see our guide to [questions to ask real estate leads](/blog/questions-to-ask-real-estate-leads/).

**What should I write down after an LPMAMA call?**
Write the answer to each letter in the lead's own words, the three layers of motivation, the timeline, and the exact next step with a date. A lead temperature (hot, warm, or cold) helps you set the right follow-up cadence.

**How is LPMAMA different from a buyer consultation?**
LPMAMA is the qualifying conversation on the call. The buyer consultation is the meeting you book at the end of it, where you build the plan together. See our list of [buyer consultation questions](/blog/buyer-consultation-questions/) for what to ask once you sit down.
