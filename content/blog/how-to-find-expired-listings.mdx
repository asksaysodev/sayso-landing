The most direct way to find expired listings is to search your MLS for listings with an expired status in your market and a date range covering the last day or two. Check each result to confirm the home has not already relisted or sold, look up the owner in public records, check the number against the National Do Not Call Registry before you dial, and call as soon as the listing shows as expired. You can also build a watch list of active listings that are nearing the end of their agreement, so you are prepared on the day they expire.

This guide walks through those steps in order: what counts as expired, the MLS search settings to use, how to verify a listing and find the owner, how timing works, and a weekly routine with a tracking template you can copy. For what to say once an owner picks up, use our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/), and for the definition, see the glossary entry on [expired listings](/glossary/expired-listing/). Expired listing calls are one part of prospecting, covered in our [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/).

## What Counts as an Expired Listing

Redfin defines an expired listing as one where [the listing agreement between the seller and their agent ends without the home being sold](https://www.redfin.com/blog/expired-real-estate-listings/), which removes the property from the MLS and from active search results. That definition matters for your search for two reasons.

First, a portal's active search will not show these homes, so the MLS is where you look. Second, expired is only one of three "ended without a sale" statuses. A canceled listing is removed before the agreement ends, and either the seller or the agent can request that. Withdrawn listings are also taken off the market before a sale. Each status is a different story, and the owner of a canceled or withdrawn listing may be open to a conversation too.

HousingWire notes that listing agreements [typically last three to six months](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/how-to-find-expired-listings/) (90 to 180 days). The length of the agreement tells you roughly when to expect expirations and why a home that sat for several months often has a pricing or marketing story to uncover.

## Step 1: Search the MLS for Expired Status

HousingWire's guide lists searching your local MLS as its first method, and a free one, filtering by area and timeframe and recommending that you [set aside one to two hours a week](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/how-to-find-expired-listings/) for it. Every MLS has its own screens, so use the names below as a guide and look for the equivalent in yours.

| Filter | Setting | Why |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Status | Expired. Use separate searches for Canceled and Withdrawn | Each status needs a slightly different conversation |
| Date range | Yesterday through today for fresh listings. A wider range for older ones | Fresh and older expireds call for different approaches |
| Area | Your farm: zip codes, neighborhoods or school districts | Local knowledge makes your calls specific |
| Price range and property type | The segment you can serve well | Keeps your list matched to your business |
| Sort | Days on market, longest first | Long stays often show pricing or marketing problems |

If your MLS offers saved searches with email alerts, save this search so the new expireds arrive in your inbox each morning. If it does not, put a daily 15-minute search on your calendar. A search you remember to do beats a better one you skip.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Save separate searches for Expired, Canceled and Withdrawn. Mixing them makes it harder to tailor the opening of your call to what actually happened.
</CalloutBox>

## Step 2: Verify the Listing Before You Call

An expired status does not always mean the home is still unsold. Before a listing goes on your call list, check three things:

1. **It has not relisted.** Search the address for a new listing. Some owners relist with another agent right away.
2. **It has not sold or gone under contract.** A sold or pending status means the owner no longer needs a listing.
3. **It is not a different property with the same address.** Condos and multi-unit buildings can produce confusing matches.

Then spend a minute on the history, which is what separates your call from the others the owner will receive. Look at days on market, price changes, the photos and the description. ## Step 3: Find the Owner and Contact Information

MLS records describe the listing, so the owner's details usually come from elsewhere. Mashvisor's guide points to [county or city public records](https://www.mashvisor.com/blog/real-estate-expired-listings/), which are free but can be slow. Other options are a letter or a visit to the property, which do not require a phone number. Some agents buy owner contact data from third-party data services. Those services differ widely and are outside the scope of this guide, and the compliance step is the same whichever source you use.

That step is the National Do Not Call Registry. [NAR's guidance](https://www.nar.realtor/telemarketing-cold-calling) says you may not call a person whose listing with another agent has expired if their number is on the registry and the purpose of your call is to offer to list the property. Check every number before you dial, and read our guide to [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) for the details, including the exceptions and calling hours. Your broker can tell you how your office handles the registry.

## Step 4: Build a Pre-Expiration Watch List

You can prepare before a listing expires. Mashvisor describes searching your MLS dashboard by expiration date with a window of [four weeks or less](https://www.mashvisor.com/blog/real-estate-expired-listings/) to see which listings are close to the end of their agreement. HousingWire suggests watching for listings [creeping toward 140 days on market](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/how-to-find-expired-listings/) as one sign of trouble. Combine the two into a short watch list of homes that are priced above recent sales, have been on the market for months, or are a few days from the end of the agreement.

The watch list is for preparation, not for contacting the owner. NAR's Code of Ethics, in Article 16 and its standards of practice, says that Realtors [shall not solicit a listing which is currently listed exclusively with another broker](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/2026-code-of-ethics-standards-of-practice) (Standard of Practice 16-4). Use the time to research the home and its comparable sales, so you can call with a plan on the day it expires. Ask your broker how the Code of Ethics and your state's rules apply to your marketing.

## Step 5: Decide When to Call

The best day to start is the first day the listing shows as expired in your MLS, because the owner is deciding what to do next and other agents are likely calling too. Fresh expired calls compete with that volume, so open with something specific from your research instead of a general introduction.

The second window opens weeks or months later. HousingWire suggests [older expired listings, from about six to twelve months back](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/how-to-find-expired-listings/), because fewer agents are calling them. Some owners have changed their plans by then, so the conversation is about whether selling still matters rather than why the first attempt failed. Our guide on [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) has separate scripts for fresh, warm and older expireds, and the glossary entry on the [listing appointment](/glossary/listing-appointment/) shows where the conversation leads.

Choose your calling hours based on when your local owners answer, stay inside the calling rules, and keep your own records of contact outcomes so you can learn which times work in your market.

## A Weekly Routine for Finding Expired Listings

A short routine keeps the list fresh and the calls consistent.

| When | What | Time |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Each morning | Pull yesterday's Expired, Canceled and Withdrawn searches. Verify, research and add to your list | About 15 to 20 minutes |
| Weekly | Search the wider date range for older expireds. Update the pre-expiration watch list | About an hour |
| After each call | Record the outcome, the next follow-up date and any detail the owner shared | A few minutes |
| Monthly | Review how many calls became conversations and appointments | About 30 minutes |

Use a simple tracker so nothing falls through. Copy these columns into a spreadsheet or your CRM:

| Address | Status and date | Days on market | Price changes | Relisted? | Owner source | DNC checked | Call date and outcome | Next follow-up |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| [Street] | Expired [date] | [number] | [list] | No | Public record | Yes | [date, result] | [date] |

Treat the last column as the most important one. Many expired owners say not yet on the first call, and a dated follow-up is how those owners turn into appointments later. The [listing appointment checklist](/blog/listing-appointment-checklist/) covers what to do once one is booked.

## FAQ

**Does the MLS show expired listings?**
Yes. MLS systems typically let you search by status and date range, so you can filter for Expired, Canceled or Withdrawn listings in your area. The exact screen differs by MLS.

**Does Zillow show expired listings?**
An expired listing is removed from the MLS and from active search results, so an active portal search will not show it. Use your MLS status search instead.

**How do I find expired listing numbers?**
MLS records describe the property, so owner details usually come from public records or other sources you are permitted to use. Check any phone number against the National Do Not Call Registry before you call, since NAR's guidance says you may not call an owner on the registry to offer to list the home.

**What does it mean if a house listing says expired?**
It means the seller's agreement with their agent ended and the home did not sell. The home is no longer actively listed, and the owner may have decided to relist, take a break or sell another way.
