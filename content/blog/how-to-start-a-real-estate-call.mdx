Starting a real estate call comes down to the first 10 to 30 seconds. In that window the person on the other end decides whether you are a robocall, a telemarketer, or someone worth one more sentence. Your opener has one job: earn that sentence.

This guide covers only that window. For everything after it, from discovery questions to objections to booking the appointment, use the [real estate cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) and [how to talk to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/).

Below: openers by situation, a table of 12 you can use today, the opening lines that get hang-ups, and what to say when they ask "who is this?" or "how did you get my number?"

## What the First 30 Seconds Have to Do

A good opener does three things, in this order:

1. **Says who you are.** Your name, then your brokerage. One breath.
2. **Gives a reason.** One sentence tied to their situation. A listing that expired, a home that sold on their street, the search they ran, the referral who sent you.
3. **Asks one question.** Something they can answer in a word or two, so the call becomes a conversation.

If you are still talking at the 30-second mark without having asked a question, the opener has failed.

Tone matters as much as the words. High-volume [cold calling](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) data shows that calls where the agent sounds relaxed and conversational last 3-4x longer than calls where the agent sounds rushed or rehearsed, even when both agents use the same script. Slow your first sentence by about 30%. Breathe out before you dial so your voice sits lower. Know the structure well enough that you are not reading.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Have a default opener loaded for every situation before you start dialing. When someone picks up on dial 40 after a string of voicemails, you should not have to think. You should just start.
</CalloutBox>

## Openers by Situation

The structure stays the same. The reason changes based on why you are on the phone with this person.

### Cold Calls

They did not ask to hear from you. The reason has to be specific, or you sound like a purchased list.

<ScriptExample label="Cold: Reason for the Call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling because [a home on your street just sold / your listing came off the market last week]. Quick question about that, do you have a minute?
</ScriptExample>

"I'm calling about your home" is not a reason. "A home three doors down sold last week" is. Specificity signals preparation. Our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) and [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) show how the reason changes by list.

When you have no context at all, name the awkwardness instead of hiding it. Most agents open with "Hi, I'm [Name] with [Brokerage]." Prospects hear that format 5-10 times a week, and their brain files it as a sales call.

<ScriptExample label="Cold: Pattern Interrupt">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I know you're not expecting this call, and I'll be quick. I had one question about [specific topic].
</ScriptExample>

### Warm Calls

They know you, or they did something that put them on your list: a web search, a saved listing, a referral, a past transaction. Lead with that.

<ScriptExample label="Warm: Curiosity Question">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. Quick question: are you still thinking about selling in [Area], or have plans changed?
</ScriptExample>

"Or have plans changed" gives them an easy out, which makes an honest answer more likely. Use it only when you have context. On a cold list it falls flat.

For a referral, the referrer's name is the reason. Say it first.

<ScriptExample label="Warm: Referral">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. [Referrer] mentioned you might be thinking about [buying/selling] and asked me to reach out. Is this an okay time for a couple of minutes?
</ScriptExample>

### Inbound Calls

They called you, off a sign, an ad or a listing. They already have a question. Answer the phone like a person, then let them ask it.

<ScriptExample label="Inbound: Sign or Ad Call">
This is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Which property are you calling about?
</ScriptExample>

Get their name in the first exchange. "Happy to help. Who am I speaking with?" Then answer their question before you ask yours.

### Callbacks

They told you to call back, and you are calling at the time they gave. Remind them they asked.

<ScriptExample label="Callback: At the Time They Asked">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You asked me to try you back this afternoon about [topic]. Is now still good?
</ScriptExample>

A "call me later" is better than a hang-up. It means the first opener earned enough attention for a reply. Our guide to the ["call me later" objection](/objections/call-me-later/) covers how to set the callback so this second call happens.

### Voicemail Returns

They are calling you back after your message. They may not remember what it was about. Fill in the gap before they have to ask.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail Return: They Call You">
Thanks for calling back. This is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I left you a message about [the home that sold on your street / your listing]. I had one quick question about that.
</ScriptExample>

If you are the one calling again after leaving a voicemail, reference the message so the second call feels like a follow-up, not a new cold call: "Hi [Name], [Your Name] again. I left you a message Tuesday about [topic]."

## 12 Real Estate Call Openers by Situation

| Situation | Opener | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Cold: expired listing | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your listing on [Street] came off the market. Quick question about that, do you have a minute?" | The day after a listing expires or is withdrawn |
| Cold: nearby sale | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. A home on [Street] just sold, and I'm calling a few neighbors about it. Do you have a minute?" | Circle prospecting around a recent sale or new listing |
| Cold: FSBO | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner. I'm not calling to talk you into listing. I had one question about the property." | Owners selling without an agent |
| Cold: no context | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I know you're not expecting this call, and I'll be quick. I had one question about [topic]." | Cold lists with nothing specific to reference |
| Warm: web or portal lead | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were looking at homes in [Neighborhood] on [site]. Do you have a quick minute?" | Within minutes of a new online inquiry |
| Warm: prior contact | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. Quick question: are you still thinking about selling in [Area], or have plans changed?" | Anyone you have spoken with before about a move |
| Warm: referral | "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. [Referrer] mentioned you might be thinking about [buying/selling] and asked me to reach out." | Any introduction from a client, friend or agent |
| Warm: past client or sphere | "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I was thinking about you when [reason]. How's the house treating you?" | Sphere and past-client check-ins, where small talk fits |
| Inbound: sign or ad call | "This is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Which property are you calling about?" | Anyone calling your number from a sign, ad or listing |
| Callback: requested | "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You asked me to try you back this afternoon about [topic]. Is now still good?" | When they told you when to call again |
| Voicemail return: they call you | "Thanks for calling back. This is [Your Name]. I left you a message about [topic]. I had one quick question about that." | When someone returns your voicemail |
| Voicemail follow-up: you call again | "Hi [Name], [Your Name] again. I left you a message [day] about [topic]. Did I catch you at an okay time?" | Your second attempt after leaving a message |

Once they answer your question, the opener is done. For what to say next by lead source, see our [phone scripts for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/).

## Opening Lines That Get You Hung Up On

The gap between a bad opener and a good one is often 3-5 words. These are the ones to cut.

**"Is now a good time?"** The polite answer is "no." You just handed them the exit. Say "do you have a quick minute?" instead.

**"Hi, my name is [Name] and I'm calling from [Brokerage]."** This is the robocall and solar-panel format. Lead with their name and your reason, not your credentials.

**"I'd love to help you sell your home."** It is about you, and it assumes they want to sell. That triggers the ["not interested"](/objections/not-interested/) reflex. Ask about their situation first.

**"I was just calling to introduce myself."** No reason, no value to them. They are thinking, "why should I care?"

**"How are you doing today?"** On a cold dial it reads as fake rapport. It works on a past client or referral call. On a first touch, skip it.

**A long pause after "hello."** If you hesitate while you find your script, many people assume it is an auto-dialer and hang up. Start talking the moment they answer.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Record five calls. Play back only the first 10 seconds of each. You will hear what you cannot catch live: filler words, rushed pacing, the "um" before your name. Ten minutes of listening fixes more than an hour of rewriting your script.
</CalloutBox>

## What to Say When They Ask "Who Is This?"

"Who is this?" usually means they did not catch your name or are deciding whether you are a robocall. It is not a rejection. Slow down and give them the full answer in two sentences.

<ScriptExample label="Who Is This?">
Sorry, I went fast. This is [Your Name], I'm a local agent with [Brokerage]. I'm calling because [one-sentence reason].
</ScriptExample>

Do not get defensive and do not skip the reason. Then go straight back to your question.

## What to Say When They Ask "How Did You Get My Number?"

Answer it plainly. A vague answer sounds like you are hiding something. Name the source in one sentence.

<ScriptExample label="How Did You Get My Number?">
Fair question. [Your number was on your listing / You filled out a form on our site / Your number came from a homeowner data service we use for [Area]]. If you'd rather I not call, I'll take you off my list right now.
</ScriptExample>

Offering to remove them often lowers the temperature enough for them to stay on the line. If they ask to be removed, do it and log it. ## FAQ

**How long should a real estate call opener be?**
Short enough that you ask a question within the first 10 to 20 seconds. Name, brokerage, one-sentence reason, one question. If you are still talking at 30 seconds, cut it down.

**What do you say when someone asks "who is this?" on a cold call?**
Slow down, repeat your name and brokerage, and give your reason for calling in one sentence. "Sorry, I went fast. This is [Name] with [Brokerage], and I'm calling because [reason]." Then ask your question again.

**How do you answer "how did you get my number?"**
Name the source plainly: their listing, a form they filled out, a referral or a homeowner data service. Then offer to take them off your list. A plain answer keeps more people on the line than a vague one, and if they ask to be removed, remove them.

**Should you ask "how are you doing today?" when you start a call?**
Not on a cold call. It reads as fake rapport. On a past-client, sphere or referral call, where you have a relationship, it fits.

**How do I recover if I stumble on my opening line?**
Laugh it off and restart: "Sorry, let me start over. I'm [Name], and here's why I'm calling." Owning a fumble is more disarming than a smooth recovery attempt. The agents who lose the call are the ones who freeze, not the ones who stumble and keep going.
