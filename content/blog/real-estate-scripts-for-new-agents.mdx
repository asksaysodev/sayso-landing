A new agent needs five scripts to get started: a warm check-in call for people who already know you or asked about homes before, a call to a new online lead, a follow-up call to an open house visitor, a circle prospecting call to a neighbor, and a call to a for-sale-by-owner seller. Learn them in that order, because each one asks for a little more nerve than the one before it, and all five end the same way, with a short meeting booked for today or tomorrow.

Below you will find the word-for-word script for each call, what people usually say next and how to answer, a voicemail and text version, and a link to the deeper guide for each situation so you can go further once the basics feel natural. The scripts are written for the first weeks, when you are still learning to sound like yourself on the phone.

## How the Five Scripts Fit Together

Every call in this guide has the same four parts. You say who you are and why you are calling, you ask one question about their plans, you listen for the real reason behind the move and when it needs to happen, and you ask for a short meeting. The words change by situation; the shape does not. Once you can do the shape on a friendly call, you can do it on a harder one.

The goal of every call is a booked meeting, which is where the selling and the home search actually begin. Ask for it within a day or two, because meetings set far in the future are the ones that get rescheduled or cancelled.

## Before Your First Call

A little setup makes the first calls much easier. Keep it short.

- Print the script you plan to use, or keep it open on a second screen, with your name and brokerage filled in so you never read a blank.
- Open your calendar and pick two meeting times for today or tomorrow before you dial. You will offer them, so decide them first.
- Have a note template ready for motivation, timeline, whether they are working with an agent, and the next step.
- Check the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) that apply to the list you are using, and follow your brokerage's policy.

If dialing still makes your stomach drop, you are in good company, and our guide on [overcoming call reluctance](/blog/how-to-overcome-call-reluctance/) goes into what helps.

## Script 1: The Warm Check-In

Start here because the person on the other end already has a reason to talk to you. It works for a lead who asked about homes a while ago, for someone in your database, and for people you know personally.

<ScriptExample label="Warm Check-In: Someone Who Asked About Homes Before">
Hey [Name]? This is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I was going through my notes and saw you had asked about [number]-bedroom homes in [City]. Quick question, did you end up moving already, or are you still waiting?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "We already bought": say "Oh nice, congratulations. Just so I update my records, what city did you end up buying in, and about how long ago was that?" Thank them and move on. You have cleaned your list and left a good impression.
- **If they say** "We're still looking": say "Got it. Did you pause the search for a bit, or have you been casually looking this whole time?" Then ask the first motivation question, "What's got you wanting to move now?"
- **If they say** "We're not sure anymore": say "That makes sense, it's a big decision. What would need to be true for it to feel like the right time?" Then set a follow-up date and put it in your calendar.

When someone says they are still looking, keep digging for the reason. The first answer is usually the surface one ("something bigger"). Ask what is behind it ("our second baby is coming"), then what happens if nothing changes ("the baby is due in four months and we can't renew our lease"). That last answer tells you the timeline, and it tells you whether to book the meeting this week. Our [LPMAMA guide](/blog/lpmama-real-estate/) lays out the full set of questions.

If the person is a friend or a former coworker, say plainly that you are new: "I just got my license and I'm calling people I know. Is anyone you know thinking about buying or selling?" People in your [sphere of influence](/glossary/sphere-of-influence/) tend to be glad to help once they know what you are asking for.

**Common mistake:** jumping to "can I send you some listings?" before you know why they want to move.

## Script 2: Calling a New Online Lead

A lead who just asked about a home is the best place to practice, because they raised their hand. Call as soon as you reasonably can after they inquire, while your name still rings a bell.

<ScriptExample label="New Online Lead: First Call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name], a real estate agent with [Brokerage]. You recently asked about homes in [Area] on [source], and I wanted to call you personally while it's fresh. Is [Area] where you're looking to buy, or are you open to other areas too?

[Let them answer, then ask one question at a time.]

What price range are you aiming for? Are you renting right now, or do you own a home you'd need to sell first?

The best way for me to help is to spend 20 minutes with you mapping out what you want and what's realistic. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "I'm just looking": say "Totally fair, most people start there. What caught your eye?" Then ask when they would want to move if the right home came up. If it is a few months out, offer a short call to build a plan rather than a showing.
- **If they say** "Just send me listings": say "Happy to send homes. So I send the right ones, can I ask what's got you thinking about a move right now?" An automated search sends everything that fits a price and a bedroom count, and the 20-minute meeting is what makes the search useful. Our page on handling ["just send listings"](/objections/just-send-listings/) has more wording.
- **If they say** "I'm already working with an agent": say "That's great, I respect that. Are you under an agreement with them?" If yes, say "Perfect. If anything changes or you ever want a second opinion, I'm here." If no, ask what they hope an agent would help with most.
- **If they say** "I need to sell first": say "Got it, that's the first thing to plan around. Has anyone put together an estimate of what your home would sell for and what you'd net?" That question leads naturally to a meeting about both sides of the move.

Buyer leads get their own full set of scripts in our [buyer lead scripts](/blog/buyer-lead-scripts/) guide, and the [phone scripts for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/) post covers the qualification questions in more depth.

**Common mistake:** asking six questions in a row. One question, one answer, then a short comment that shows you listened, and then the next question.

## Script 3: The Open House Follow-Up

By the time you make this call you have already met the person, which makes it a warm call. Many new agents host open houses early on, so this is a script you will use often.

<ScriptExample label="Open House Visitor: Next-Day Call">
Hey [Name]? This is [Your First Name]. We met at the open house on [Street]! Are you still visiting open houses, or have you found the one?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "We're still looking": say "What type of home are you looking for? Tell me more about it." Then ask what did not work about the open house you met at, and whether they are trying to stay in that neighborhood or are open to other areas.
- **If they say** "We found one": say "Congratulations! Did you end up working with an agent on it?" Thank them for their time and ask whether you can check in after they move.
- **If they say** "We're working with someone": say "Good to hear. Is that someone you've signed with, or have you mostly been touring on your own?"

After a couple of questions, make the offer: "Most open house visitors either need better options or better clarity. Which one sounds more like you?" Whichever they choose, follow with "I usually sit down for 20 to 25 minutes and map out what's realistic right now. Would that help?" The full open house sequence, including the exit questions and the voicemail and text versions, is in our [open house scripts](/blog/open-house-scripts/) guide.

**Common mistake:** calling days later without mentioning anything specific from the conversation at the house. A detail from the visit, such as the backyard they liked, proves you remember them.

## Script 4: Circle Prospecting

This is the first call to a stranger, and it is the easiest of those because you have a real reason to call: something happened on their street. [Circle prospecting](/glossary/circle-prospecting/) means calling homeowners around a recent sale, a new listing or a buyer you are working with.

<ScriptExample label="Circle Prospecting: When You Have a Buyer">
Hi [Name]? This is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I'll be quick, I just have one question. I'm working with a buyer who wants to move into your neighborhood and missed out on another home. If the right opportunity came along for your place, is that something you'd want to know about?
</ScriptExample>

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Say only what is true. Use the buyer version when you really are working with a buyer who wants that area. If you have no buyer yet, use the just-sold version in our [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) guide, which starts from a real sale on their street.
</CalloutBox>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "No, we're not selling": say "Fair enough. Out of curiosity, how long have you owned it?" Then ask whether they have looked at how much equity they have built. That question moves the call from a buyer's search to the value of their home.
- **If they say** "Maybe, what would you offer?": say "That depends on the condition. Can you tell me about any upgrades or changes you've made? Honestly, I'd need to see the home to give you a fair answer. Would it be okay if I came by for 20 minutes?"
- **If they say** "Where would we even go?": say "That's the right question to ask, and it's something I help people plan. Where would you want to be?"

**Common mistake:** pushing after a clear no. Thank them and move to the next call. Calls to strangers go better when you keep the pace up and do not dwell on one conversation.

## Script 5: The FSBO Call

Owners selling on their own are the next step up, because they are not expecting your call and many are wary of agents. This script works because it says up front that you are not asking for the listing, which lowers their guard.

<ScriptExample label="For Sale By Owner: First Call">
Hi [Name]? This is [Your First Name]. I saw your home for sale by owner. I won't ask you to list with me. I'm just curious how it's going so far?

[Let them talk.]

What made you decide to sell it yourself? How did you land on your price? Have you had many showings or any offers?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next:

- **If they say** "It's going fine": say "Glad to hear it. If you did get an offer you liked, are you comfortable handling the contract and the negotiation on your own?"
- **If they say** "We don't want to pay a commission": say "That makes sense, and I'm not here to argue with it. Can I show you what homes like yours have sold for and what you'd net, so you can compare your options?" Our guide to [the commission question](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/) covers it in depth.
- **If they say** "Not interested": say "No problem. Out of curiosity, how long will you try selling it on your own before you'd consider other options?" See the [not interested](/objections/not-interested/) page for more.

When they are warming up, ask to visit: "I'd like to see the layout and tell you what comparable homes nearby have sold for. Would it be okay if I came by for 20 minutes?" Then book it with the close below. The [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/) guide matches the script to how long the home has been listed, and the [FSBO glossary entry](/glossary/fsbo/) explains the term if it is new to you.

**Common mistake:** arguing for why they need an agent. The first call is about earning a second one.

When these five feel natural, the next call to learn is the [expired listing script](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/), followed by the [withdrawn listing script](/blog/withdrawn-listing-scripts/) for sellers who took their homes off the market on purpose.

## The Close Every Script Ends With

Each of the five scripts leads to the same moment, where you ask for the meeting. Use two specific times, because a specific choice is easier to say yes to than an open question.

<ScriptExample label="Booking the Meeting (Works After Any of the Five)">
Let's take 20 minutes to strategize. Would later today or tomorrow be easier? Are you more of a morning, afternoon or evening person? And would that work for [spouse or other decision maker] too?

We can do a quick sit down at the office or over coffee, and we'll be done before the coffee gets cold. Let me confirm your email so I can send the invite. If anything changes, just text me, even if we need to push it a few minutes.
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Can you just email me?": say "Happy to send something. It's usually much clearer when we walk through it together. I have time tomorrow at 4 or Wednesday at 6. Which works better?"
- **If they say** "I need to think about it": say "Of course. What's the part you'd want to think through? Sometimes I can answer it right now." The page on the [need to think about it](/objections/need-to-think-about-it/) objection has more.
- **If they say** "Maybe later": lock in a next step instead of a vague promise. "Understood. Can I check back with you on [date] to see how things look?" Then put that date in your calendar before you hang up.

If a call goes nowhere, move on to the next one. Calling in batches keeps your rhythm up, and one hard conversation should not slow down the rest of your session.

## Voicemail and Text Versions

Most of your calls will go to voicemail, so have a short version ready. Keep it friendly and curious, with no pitch, and leave your number once.

<ScriptExample label="Short Voicemail for a Lead">
Hey [Name], this is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. You were checking out homes in [Area] and I didn't want you to miss anything that fits what you're looking for. I'll send you a quick text as well. Talk soon.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Text After a Missed Call">
Hey [Name], it's [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I just tried you about the homes you were viewing in [Area]. Are you looking to move soon or just exploring options?
</ScriptExample>

## A Three-Week Order for Learning Them

You do not need all five on day one. A gradual order keeps each step small enough to do well.

| Week | Scripts to use | What to focus on |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 1 | Warm check-in and new lead call | Asking one question at a time and writing down the answer to "why now?" |
| 2 | Add the open house follow-up | Mentioning one specific detail from your conversation at the house |
| 3 | Add circle prospecting and the FSBO call | Accepting a no politely and still asking for the meeting when you get a yes |

Read each script out loud before you use it, then try it on a colleague or a family member. Our guide on [how to practice real estate scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) has drills for this, and the goal is to understand the structure well enough that the words come out in your own voice.

Reading from a printed page works for the first few calls, but a page is hard to follow while a real person is talking. Some new agents keep their own scripts on screen during calls with a tool like [Sayso's Playbook](/products/playbook/), so the next line is there when they lose their place.

## Mistakes That Kill New Agent Calls

Most failed calls share the same few causes, and every one is fixable.

Reading the script in a flat voice is the most common, and it is easy to spot because the person on the other end goes quiet. Slow down your first sentence, say it the way you would say it to a friend, and change any word that does not sound like you.

Leading with your credentials is the next one. A new agent does not have a long list of them yet, and in any case people care more about their own situation than about your resume. Open with the reason you are calling and a question about them.

Sending listings too early comes up on nearly every buyer call. Without knowing the motivation and the timeline, you cannot send homes that fit, and sending them hands the conversation back to a search portal.

Not asking for the meeting is the quiet one. Many calls go well and then end with "talk soon," and nothing is booked. Decide your two times before you dial and offer them on every call that goes well.

Saying anything that is not true, such as claiming to have a buyer when you do not, can follow you around a small market. Use the version of each script that matches your real situation.

## FAQ

**How do you introduce yourself as a real estate agent on a call?**
Say your first name and brokerage, give the reason you are calling in one sentence, and ask a question. For example: "Hi [Name], this is [Your First Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw you asked about homes in [Area], and I wanted to call you personally." Skip the list of credentials.

**What is a good script for cold calling?**
A good one gives a real reason for the call, asks a single question about the person's plans, and ends with a request for a short meeting. The circle prospecting and FSBO scripts above follow that pattern, and our [cold calling guide](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) covers the full framework.

**How should you answer the phone as a Realtor?**
Answer with a warm greeting, your first name and your brokerage, then ask how you can help. If you are not at your desk, a short greeting like "Hi, this is [Name] with [Brokerage]" is enough. When a lead calls you back, thank them for calling and ask what they were looking at.

**Do new agents have to memorize their scripts?**
No. A script gives you a starting point and a structure, and the aim is to understand it well enough to say it in your own words. Practice out loud, and adjust the language to match your personality while keeping the order of the questions.
