A good realtor voicemail script takes under 20 seconds to say, which is roughly 40 to 50 words. It gives your name and brokerage, one specific reason for the call, the next step you want, and your number said slowly. It works best as the first move in a short sequence (voicemail, then a text a minute later) and not as a full pitch, because the goal of the message is a callback or a warmer next call.

This post gives you voicemail scripts for the situations that come up most, including new online leads, expired listings, for sale by owner sellers, home value requests and old buyer leads. It also covers what to say when they call back, a greeting for your own voicemail box, and what the FCC has said about ringless voicemail.

## What a Realtor Voicemail Is For

The voicemail does one job: it makes the next contact easier. A lead who has heard your name and a reason for the call is more likely to pick up the second time or answer a text, and a short message costs you nothing when the call goes to voicemail anyway. Treat each message as step one of a multi-touch sequence, which is how our [real estate follow-up scripts](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-scripts/) handle the first ten minutes with a new lead.

Four parts keep any voicemail short and clear.

| Part | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Your first name and brokerage, once | "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]." |
| Why | One specific reason tied to something the lead did or something about their home | "You were looking at homes in [Area]." |
| What next | A single next step, ideally one you control | "I'll send you a quick text as well." |
| Number | Slowly, at the end | "My number is [Number]." |

Everything outside those four parts is optional, and most of it makes the message worse. Leave out your credentials, your whole service list and any price talk.

## Voicemail Scripts by Situation

Each script below fits in under 20 seconds. Swap in the names, streets and neighborhoods from your CRM, because a message that names something specific sounds like a person and not a campaign.

### New Online Lead (Buyer or Seller)

Use this after the first call to a lead who just registered or inquired. It is intentionally short.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: new online lead">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were checking out homes in [Area] and I did not want you to miss anything that fits what you are looking for. I'll send you a quick text as well. Talk soon.
</ScriptExample>

Then send the text right away:

<ScriptExample label="Text to send after the voicemail">
Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I just tried you about the homes you were viewing in [Area]. Are you looking to move soon, or just exploring your options?
</ScriptExample>

If the lead filled out a home value form instead, swap "checking out homes in [Area]" for "asking about the value of your home on [Street]" and change the text question to "Are you thinking about selling, or just curious what it is worth?" That wording works whether the person is a buyer, a seller or both.

### Home Value Request That Went Quiet

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: old home value request">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I was going through our records and saw you asked about the value of your home on [Street] back in [Month]. I wanted to make sure we did not miss you. Give me a call back at [Number] when you have a minute.
</ScriptExample>

### Old Buyer Lead

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: buyer lead from a while back">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I was going through my notes and saw you had asked about homes in [City]. Quick question, did you end up moving already, or are you still looking? A text back is fine too. My number is [Number].
</ScriptExample>

This message asks a question the lead can answer in one word, which is easier than asking them to call back "to discuss options." For longer re-engagement sequences, see [what to say to old leads](/blog/what-to-say-to-old-leads/).

### Expired Listing

The owner has probably heard from several agents already, so this message skips the pitch and asks one real question. Calls to expired listing owners can fall under the Do Not Call rules, so scrub your list before you dial. Our [Do Not Call rules](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) post covers when that applies.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: expired listing">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling about your home on [Street]. I wanted to ask whether you are still planning to sell or waiting for something to change. I have one idea on what I would do differently, and I can share it in a few minutes. My number is [Number].
</ScriptExample>

The live call that follows is in our [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/), and the [expired listing](/glossary/expired-listing/) glossary page explains the lead type.

### For Sale By Owner (FSBO)

The same caution applies to FSBO sellers, so check the Do Not Call rules before calling. The message leads with a promise that removes the usual defensiveness.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: FSBO">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I saw your home on [Street] is for sale by owner, and I'm not calling to ask for the listing. I'm just curious how it is going so far. If you have a minute, my number is [Number].
</ScriptExample>

Match your next call to how long the home has been listed, using the stages in our [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/). The [FSBO glossary entry](/glossary/fsbo/) has the definition.

<ScriptSheet title="Voicemail call sheet">

**New online lead:** "You were checking out homes in [Area] and I did not want you to miss anything that fits. I'll send you a quick text as well."

**Old home value request:** "I saw you asked about the value of your home on [Street] back in [Month]. I wanted to make sure we did not miss you."

**Old buyer lead:** "Did you end up moving already, or are you still looking? A text back is fine too."

**Expired listing:** "Are you still planning to sell or waiting for something to change? I have one idea on what I would do differently."

**FSBO:** "I'm not calling to ask for the listing. I'm just curious how it is going so far."

**Text after any voicemail:** "I just tried you. Are you looking to move soon, or just exploring your options?"

**Always:** name and brokerage once, one reason, one next step, number said slowly, under 20 seconds.

</ScriptSheet>

## When They Call Back

A callback is a warm lead on the line, and it is easy to waste by asking "what can I do for you?" Repeat the reason from your message in one sentence and move straight to your first question.

| What they say | What you say |
|---|---|
| "Hi, someone from this number called me." | "Thanks for calling back, [Name]. This is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I left a message about [Street / homes in Area]. Is now an okay time for a quick question?" |
| "I got your message about homes in the area." | "Thanks for getting back to me. What's got you thinking about a move right now?" Then keep asking why until you reach the real reason and timeline. |
| "I'm still trying to sell." (expired or FSBO) | "Got it. When you first listed, where were you planning on going? Is that move still important?" |
| "Who is this and why are you calling?" | "I'm [Your Name], an agent with [Brokerage]. I left a message about [reason]. If it is not a good time, when should I try you again?" |

When the call goes well, the next step is to book a short meeting, ideally later today or tomorrow, and not to send a link. The same call-back moment is also where agents forget their best lines. ## A Voicemail Greeting for Your Own Line

Your outgoing greeting is the message that plays when a lead calls you, and it should make leaving a message easy. Say your name and brokerage, who you help, what to leave, and when you will call back. Only promise a return time you will keep.

<ScriptExample label="Greeting: standard">
Hi, you've reached [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm either on a call or with a client right now. Please leave your name, your number, and whether you're buying or selling, and I'll call you back by [time]. If it's easier, you can text this number and I'll reply there. Thanks for calling.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Greeting: after hours">
You've reached [Your Name] with [Brokerage] in [Area]. I'm away from my phone for the evening. Leave your name and number and the best time to reach you, and I'll get back to you first thing tomorrow morning.
</ScriptExample>

<ScriptExample label="Greeting: short version">
This is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Leave your name and number and the best time to call, and I'll get back to you today.
</ScriptExample>

Record the greeting in a quiet room, smile as you say it, and play it back once on speaker. If you would not leave a message after hearing it, record it again. A greeting that names both buying and selling keeps it useful for every kind of lead.

## Ringless Voicemail and Voicemail Drops: What the FCC Says

Ringless voicemail delivers a recorded message straight to a voicemail box without the recipient's phone ringing. It is often sold as a voicemail drop, and the rules matter before you use one.

<CalloutBox type="warning">
This is general information, not legal advice. Check with your broker and your state's rules before you send any recorded or automated message. Last reviewed: October 24, 2026.
</CalloutBox>

In a declaratory ruling adopted on November 14, 2022 and released November 21, 2022, the Federal Communications Commission found that ringless voicemail to wireless phones requires consumer consent, because it is a "call" made using an artificial or prerecorded voice and is covered by section 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) of the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. The FCC's announcement of the ruling says ringless voicemail is a form of robocall and is illegal if the caller did not have the consumer's prior express consent, and that violations can be enforced by the FCC or by the consumer in court. A footnote in the ruling adds that the consent must be written for certain telemarketing calls to wireless numbers.

The sources are the FCC's [Declaratory Ruling and Order (FCC 22-85)](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-22-85A1.pdf) and the FCC's [news release announcing it](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-389372A1.pdf).

For agents, that points to a short checklist.

- Ask your broker how your office collects and records consent before you use any tool that sends a recorded message to voicemail.
- Treat a recorded drop to a lead who has not given consent as a risk, even if the tool makes it easy.
- Remember that the Do Not Call Registry, calling hours and your own internal do-not-call list still apply to the calls and messages around it. Our [Do Not Call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains those, including what happens with FSBO and expired listing numbers.

The FCC ruling is about ringless voicemail. Your broker can tell you how your office treats other recorded or automated messages.

## Mistakes That Kill the Callback

**Rambling.** Past 20 seconds, people stop listening. If you cannot say the reason for the call in one sentence, you do not have a reason yet.

**Leading with the pitch.** "I'm the top agent in [Area]" gives the listener nothing to respond to. A question or a specific observation about their home does.

**Saying private details.** A voicemail can be heard by a spouse, a roommate or a family member. Do not mention a foreclosure, a divorce, a death, a mortgage balance or a price. Keep it to the property address and a reason that works for anyone to hear.

**Mumbling the number.** Say it slowly. If your message is short, say it twice.

**Sending the same message every time.** If a lead has heard "just following up" three times, the fourth tells them nothing. Change the reason each time, for example a recent sale on their street, a market note or a new listing that fits what they asked for.

**Leaving a voicemail and stopping.** The voicemail opens the loop and a text a minute later makes the next step easy. If the lead replies by text, you are in a conversation, which is the point.

## FAQ

**Are ringless voicemails illegal for real estate agents?**
They are not banned outright. The FCC ruled in 2022 that ringless voicemail to wireless phones is a call made with an artificial or prerecorded voice, which means the caller needs the consumer's prior express consent, and the consent must be written for certain telemarketing calls. Sending them without consent is what creates the risk. Ask your broker before you use one.

**How long should a realtor voicemail be?**
Aim for under 20 seconds, about 40 to 50 words. State who you are, the one reason you called and the next step, then give your number slowly. If you keep going, the listener is deciding whether to delete it.

**Should I leave a voicemail or send a text?**
Do both. Leave a short voicemail first, then send a text a minute later that asks one easy question. The voicemail gives the lead a name and a reason, and the text lets them answer without calling back.
