The best time to call expired listings is whenever the homeowners in your area are most likely to pick up, and no study we found shows which hour that is. Stay inside the legal calling window of 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the homeowner's local time, test a few time blocks for two weeks, and keep the ones that give you the most real conversations. How soon you call after the listing expires and what you say on the call usually matter more than the clock.

Expired listing calls work better when the script reflects what happened with the property and how long it has been off the market. Before dialing, review the listing history, price changes, comparable sales, and presentation. That preparation gives you a specific reason for calling instead of another generic opener.

This article is part of our complete [real estate cold call scripts](/blog/real-estate-cold-calling-guide/) guide. If the terminology is new to you, start with our definition of an [expired listing](/glossary/expired-listing/).

This guide covers when to call, a diagnostic approach that changes what you say based on what you actually know about the listing, scripts tailored to how long the property has been off market, and how the common script styles differ.

## What Is the Best Time to Call Expired Listings?

Agents disagree, and the guides that give an answer do not back it with data. One [HousingWire guide to expired listings](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/expired-listings-a-realtors-goldmine/) advises making your calls as early in the morning as possible in a protected block of at least 30 minutes a day. [Another ranking guide](https://www.amitree.com/resources/blog/expired-listing-scripts-that-work/) suggests weekday afternoons between 3 and 5 p.m. and a mid-morning call on Saturday. Neither cites research, so treat both as starting points to test, not rules.

The one firm rule is legal. Federal rules bar sales calls to homes before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the local time of the person you are calling ([47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)). Numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry add a separate limit, and our guide to the [do not call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) explains how it applies to expired listings. Ask your broker how your office scrubs lists before you dial.

Within that window, here is the reasoning behind each time block, so you know what you are testing.

| Time block | Why agents try it | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Early morning (8 to 10 a.m.) | Homeowners are often home before the day starts, and you reach them before other agents' calls pile up | Many people are getting out the door and short on time |
| Midday (11 a.m. to 2 p.m.) | Some people take calls on a lunch break | Most working homeowners are at work and may not answer |
| Mid-afternoon (3 to 5 p.m.) | One ranking guide suggests it as a calmer stretch of the day | School pickups and the end of the workday can pull people away |
| Early evening (5 to 8 p.m.) | Many working homeowners are home from work | A call during dinner can feel intrusive, and 9 p.m. is a hard stop |
| Saturday morning | Homeowners are home and not rushing to work | Some sellers dislike weekend sales calls, so check your broker's and your state's rules first |

This table is reasoning, not research. The way to find your own answer is to try a short test.

1. Pick the time blocks you want to compare and make the same number of calls in each one over two weeks.
2. Tag every call by block in your CRM, or use a simple tally sheet.
3. Count real conversations, not dials, and note any appointments.
4. Keep the two blocks with the most conversations per 20 dials and drop the rest.

Timing after the listing expires is the second question, and agents disagree there too. Some call within days of the expiration, while others wait several weeks or months so the seller has time to cool off. ## Diagnose Before You Dial: The 60-Second Pre-Call Framework

Spend at least a minute researching the listing before you pick up the phone. The goal is to identify a useful observation or question, not to diagnose the entire listing from a screen.

Here's what to look for:

**Days on market (DOM).** Was it listed for 14 days or 180? A listing that expired after 2 weeks tells a different story than one that sat for 6 months.

**List price vs. comparable sales.** If the listing price was materially above nearby comparable sales, pricing strategy is one useful area to explore. Treat it as a question to investigate, not a conclusion about why the property did not sell.

**Photos and listing presentation.** Take 10 seconds to pull up the original listing photos. Dark rooms, cluttered counters, no staging. You now have something specific to say: "I noticed the photos on the original listing didn't really showcase the kitchen. That's one of the first things I'd change."

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
The 60-second diagnosis gives you an informed starting point for the conversation about why their home didn't sell. When you can point to a likely issue and ask the seller what they saw, you sound like a consultant, not a cold caller.
</CalloutBox>

This diagnostic approach changes your tone, too. When you know why the home didn't sell, you sound confident instead of fishing. It's the same principle behind a strong [cold call opener](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/): preparation beats improvisation every time.

## Expired Listing Scripts Based on Timing

Not all expired listings are the same. A homeowner whose listing expired yesterday is in a completely different emotional state than someone whose listing expired three months ago. Your script needs to match that state. When nobody picks up, our [voicemail scripts](/blog/real-estate-voicemail-scripts/) cover what to leave for each situation.

### Fresh Expireds (0-14 Days Off Market)

These sellers are frustrated, overwhelmed, and getting hammered with calls. They've heard "I noticed your home came off the market" fifteen times today. You need a pattern interrupt, and the pre-call diagnosis gives you one.

<ScriptExample label="Fresh Expired: Diagnostic Opener">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I know you're probably getting a ton of calls right now, so I'll be quick. I looked at your listing before calling, and I think the issue wasn't the home. Would you be open to hearing what I'd do differently, even if you decide to go a different direction?
</ScriptExample>

This works because it immediately differentiates you. Every other agent is calling blind. You called with homework done. That shifts the dynamic from "another salesperson" to "someone who actually understands my situation."

The key phrase is **"even if you decide to go a different direction."** It removes pressure and makes the homeowner feel safe continuing the conversation. Once they say yes, you're in a diagnostic conversation and you can work toward [setting the appointment](/blog/appointment-setting-script/).

### Warm Expireds (30-60 Days Off Market)

The initial frustration has faded. These sellers are stuck in uncertainty, wondering whether to try again, lower the price, or pull the house off the market entirely. Your approach shifts from diagnostic urgency to consultative patience.

<ScriptExample label="Warm Expired: Consultative Approach">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. Your home on [Street] came off the market about a month ago, are you still thinking about selling, or have plans changed? I ask because there have been [X] sales in your neighborhood since then, and the market position for your home looks a bit different now than when you were listed.
</ScriptExample>

The **"market position has changed"** angle gives them a reason to re-engage without admitting the first attempt failed. You're not saying "let me try where the last agent failed." You're saying the situation has evolved, and that's worth a conversation.

### Stale Expireds (90+ Days Off Market)

Older expired listings call for a different approach. The original urgency may be gone, and the homeowner's plans may have changed. Start by learning whether selling is still relevant before offering advice.

<ScriptExample label="Stale Expired: Low-Pressure Re-Engagement">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I came across your home on [Street]. It was listed earlier this year but didn't end up selling. I'm not sure if selling is still on your mind, but I've been tracking that neighborhood and wanted to share something interesting. A home similar to yours on [Nearby Street] just sold for [Price]. Would that kind of info be useful to you?
</ScriptExample>

For older expired listings, focus on reopening the conversation rather than creating artificial urgency. Set a follow-up task and return with a relevant market update, comparable sale, or change in the seller's neighborhood.

## How the Common Expired Listing Script Styles Differ

Searches for expired listing scripts often name well-known coaches, including Mike Ferry and Tom Ferry. Those coaches publish their own scripts, so read the originals from the source rather than a copy, and use the comparison below to see what each style is trying to do. We describe approaches here and do not reproduce anyone's script.

| Style | How it opens | What it is built to do | Where it can fall short |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct ask | Confirms the listing is off the market and asks whether the owner still wants to sell | Finds out fast whether there is a live seller, then moves to a meeting | Can sound identical to the other agents calling the same list |
| Diagnostic (the approach in this guide) | Refers to something specific you noticed in the listing and asks about their experience | Separates you from agents who call blind and starts a real conversation about what to change | Takes a minute of research before every call |
| Credibility-led | Opens with your track record on expired listings | Gives skeptical sellers a reason to trust you | Homeowners tend to care about why their home did not sell more than your résumé |
| Value-led | Opens with comparable sales, a pricing observation or a market update | Gives the seller something useful even if they never hire you | Without a question, it can turn into a lecture |

Most agents end up blending these. One [roundup of agents who use these scripts](https://nowbam.com/scripts-lists-and-dialers-a-breakdown-of-bams-live-cold-calling-event/) from 2023 describes agents learning a coach's script thoroughly and then reordering the questions to follow the conversation, which is the habit worth copying. Tom Ferry's own [expired listings page](https://www.tomferry.com/real-estate-webinars/ondemand-registration-expireds/) frames expireds as missed opportunities and puts structure, preparation and positioning ahead of scripts alone, which matches the diagnostic approach above.

## What to Say When They Push Back

Expired listing calls often meet resistance because the seller has already been through an unsuccessful listing and may be receiving several similar calls. If a past experience is driving the concern, our guide to [handling the "bad experience with agents" objection](/objections/bad-experience-with-agents/) offers a more detailed response framework.

**"I'm getting calls from every agent in town."**

This is frustration, not rejection. Validate it.

*"I bet you are, and honestly, most of those calls probably sound exactly the same. That's why I did my homework before calling. Can I help you stop getting calls?"*

**"We're going to relist with the same agent."**

Don't compete. Plant a seed.

*"I hear you. Quick question, did they give you a specific plan for what they'd do differently this time? Because if the approach doesn't change, the result usually doesn't either.*

**"We've decided not to sell."**

*"That's a big decision. Just to make sure I understand, what was your goal when you listed the home the first time? Where were you going after you sold?"*

This converts a dead end into a long-term follow-up opportunity. If the seller says they have already pulled the listing, that is a different conversation from an expiration, and our [withdrawn listing scripts](/blog/withdrawn-listing-scripts/) cover what to say. ## FAQ

**What is the best time to call expired listings?**
There is no universal best time, and the guides that name one do not cite data. Stay between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the homeowner's local time, test a few blocks against your own contact data using the tally sheet above, and keep the times when your local sellers actually answer.

**How do I get phone numbers for expired listings?**
Expired listings come from your MLS and from data services, and our guide to [finding expired listings](/blog/how-to-find-expired-listings/) covers the options. Check every number against the National Do Not Call Registry before you dial, and ask your broker who is responsible for list scrubbing in your office.

**How many times should you follow up with an expired listing?**
Use a documented follow-up cadence rather than relying on a universal number. Space outreach across the channels the seller has agreed to use, and make each touch useful with a new comparable sale, pricing observation, or answer to a question from the first conversation.

**Do expired listing letters still work?**
Yes, as part of a multi-channel approach. A personalized letter that references specific details about the property differentiates you from agents who only call. Pair it with a call 2-3 days later: "I sent you a letter earlier this week about your home on [Street]. Did you get a chance to read it?"

**What should you never say to an expired listing?**
Never lead with your credentials, production numbers, or awards. The homeowner doesn't care about your achievements. They care about why their home didn't sell and whether you understand the problem. Also avoid "I can sell your home," since it's a promise you can't guarantee and it sounds like every other agent. Lead with diagnosis and questions, not claims.
