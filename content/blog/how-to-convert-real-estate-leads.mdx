To convert real estate leads, contact each new lead as quickly as you can, use the first call to learn why they are looking and when they need to move, and end the call with a specific next step such as a short meeting. After that, follow up with a reason each time until the lead books, buys or sells with you, or asks you to stop. Leads tend to slip away at one of three points: nobody reached them in time, the first call ended without a next step, or the follow-up stopped too soon.

This guide covers why speed matters, a five-part structure for the first call, scripts for portal and website leads (including Zillow leads) with the replies you will hear, what to do when a lead does not answer, how to follow up, and a way to find which step is costing you the most. For the wider conversation playbook, read [how to talk to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/), and for the questions to weave into the call, see [questions to ask real estate leads](/blog/questions-to-ask-real-estate-leads/).

## Speed to Lead: Why the First Few Minutes Matter

A new lead is thinking about homes right now, and that attention tends to fade. The practical takeaway for an agent is to build a routine that gets a real first contact out within minutes of the inquiry.

A simple routine looks like this:

1. Turn on instant notifications for new leads, and decide in advance who gets each one if you work on a team.
2. Call first. A voice gets a faster answer to "are you actually moving?" than a message does.
3. If there is no answer, leave a short voicemail and send a text right away, using the templates below.
4. If you cannot call because you are in a showing, send a text that sets a time: "Hi [Name], saw you were looking at homes in [Area]. I am with a client right now but would love to chat. Are you free in 30 minutes?"
5. Log the attempt so your follow-up knows where the lead stands.

Speed does not make a poor call good, which is why the next section matters just as much.

## The First Call: A Five-Part Structure

A good first call is a short conversation with a clear end, not a form to fill in. Use these five parts as a guide and let the lead's answers set the order.

| Part | Goal | Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 1. Reason for the call | Show the call is about them | "You were just looking at homes in [Area]. Are you still searching there?" |
| 2. The move | Learn why they are looking and why now | "What is making you think about moving right now?" |
| 3. Timeline and people | Find the date and the other decision makers | "When do you want to be in the new place? Who else is part of the decision?" |
| 4. Money | Learn where they stand without turning it into an interrogation | "Have you spoken with a lender yet, or will you be paying cash?" |
| 5. The next step | Book a specific meeting | "I have [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time]. Which works better?" |

Part 2 deserves the most time. A lead's first answer is rarely the real reason, so keep asking until the situation is clear.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Listen for three layers. The surface answer is "We want something bigger." Ask what is behind it and you may hear "Our second baby is coming." Ask what happens if nothing changes and you may hear "The baby is due in four months and we can't renew our lease." That last answer tells you the timeline, and the timeline tells you how to follow up.
</CalloutBox>

When a lead asks you to send homes early in the call, acknowledge the request and steer back to the situation: "I would be happy to send homes. Tell me about your goals. What has you thinking about a move right now?" Sending listings before you know the situation turns you into another search portal instead of an advisor. Our guide to [qualifying real estate leads](/blog/how-to-qualify-real-estate-leads/) covers how to sort what you learn.

<ScriptExample label="Portal or Website Lead: First Call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were just looking at homes in [Area], and I wanted to connect while it is fresh. Are you still looking in that area?

Is that your main area of interest, or are you open to others? What price range are you targeting right now?

I am curious, are you renting or do you own? (If they own: Will you need to sell before you buy?)

When are you hoping to make a move? What is pushing it now? Who else is part of the decision?

Before we talk about specific homes, have you spoken with a lender yet, or will you be paying cash?

The best way for me to help you find the right home is a 20-minute conversation to map out your plans. Then I can send you homes that fit. What works better, [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time]?
</ScriptExample>

### What they say next

- **"I'm just looking."** Say: "Totally fair, most people start there. What caught your eye, the price, the area or something specific? If the right place came along, when would you want to be in it?" The answer tells you whether this lead is a few weeks out or a few months out.
- **"Just send me some homes."** Say: "I would be happy to. So I send the right ones, tell me about your goals. What has you thinking about making a move right now?"
- **"I'm already working with an agent."** Say: "That is great, and I respect that relationship. Quick question: are you under an agreement with them?" If yes, say "Perfect. If anything changes or you want a second opinion, I am here." If no, say "Got it. A lot of people talk to agents casually early on. If you are not committed, I would be happy to show you how I work."
- **"I have to sell my home first."** Say: "In addition to helping you buy, I can help you sell. One of the first steps is knowing what your home would sell for and what you would net. Has anyone put together a market analysis for you?"
- **"I'm not ready to meet."** Say: "That is fine. Could we spend 10 minutes on a call so I understand what you want, and then I will only send what fits?" A short call keeps the next step small and keeps you in the conversation. The [just looking objection page](/objections/just-looking/) has more wording.

## Converting Zillow and Other Portal Leads

Portal leads arrive with a specific action behind them, such as a request to tour a listing, and that action is your reason for calling. The lead may have contacted more than one agent, so a prompt call that answers their request first earns trust before you ask a single question.

<ScriptExample label="Tour Request Lead">
Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I see you would like to tour [Address] at [Time] on [Day]. Does that time still work for you?

Great, I will confirm with the seller and call you back right away. What did you like most about this home? (Let them tell you what stood out.) Why is [that feature] important to you?

I am curious, why are you looking at homes now? When, in a perfect world, would you want to move by? Why then?

Here is how I am going to start working for you. First, I will confirm the showing time with the seller. If that time does not work for them, what other days and times could I suggest? I will call you back within about 30 minutes. Second, I will get answers to the questions we discussed. Third, I know you are looking for homes with [features], so I will pull a few others that might fit. Is there anything else I can do to make this easier for you?
</ScriptExample>

Notice that the script keeps its promises small and specific. Calling back within 30 minutes is a promise you can keep, and keeping it is the first proof that you are the agent who follows through.

## When They Do Not Answer

Many calls to new leads go to voicemail. Treat the voicemail and the text as one unit: the message gives your name and a reason, and the text lets the lead answer in one tap.

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail and Text After a Missed Call">
Voicemail: Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were checking out homes in [Area], and I did not want you to miss anything that fits what you are looking for. I will send you a quick text as well. Talk soon.

Text: Hey [Name], it is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I just tried you about the homes you were viewing in [Area]. Are you looking to move soon, or just exploring options?
</ScriptExample>

The text ends with a question that has an easy answer. Replies to a question like that also tell you the lead's temperature, which decides how often you follow up.

## Follow Up With a Reason

Many leads do not convert on the first call, so the follow-up decides how many you keep. Every follow-up needs a reason beyond "just checking in," and the best reasons come from what the lead already told you. If you are working a brand-new lead, the first days matter most, and our guides on [how often to follow up with real estate leads](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/) and [real estate follow-up scripts](/blog/real-estate-follow-up-scripts/) lay out a day-by-day sequence.

| What the lead told you | A good reason to call back |
| ----- | ----- |
| A date (lease ending, school year, job start) | A plan that works backward from that date |
| An area or a feature | A new listing or a recent sale that matches |
| A worry about price or rates | A short market update for their price range or neighborhood |
| A partner who was not on the call | An offer to include them in a short follow-up call |
| Nothing yet (they were guarded) | A new question you did not ask last time |

## Find Where Your Leads Are Getting Lost

The most useful conversion number is your own, measured by lead source. Count three gates over the last 90 days and compare them across sources:

| Gate | What to count | A weak result usually points to |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Contacted | Leads you reached in a real conversation, out of all new leads | Response time, call times, the number of attempts |
| Appointment set | Meetings booked, out of leads you reached | The first call: reason, questions, the ask |
| Signed | Clients who signed an agreement, out of meetings held | Preparation and the meeting itself |

People often ask what a good conversion rate for real estate leads is. The figures on page one range from about 1% to 10% and mostly do not say whether they count appointments, signed agreements or closings, so this guide does not borrow one. Your lead source, your market and your follow-up all move the number. ## FAQ

**How fast should I contact a new real estate lead?**
As quickly as you can, ideally within minutes. If you cannot call right away, send a short text that gives a specific time you will call. Speed matters because a lead's interest tends to be highest right after the inquiry.

**Should I call or text a new lead first?**
Call first, then text and email if the lead does not answer. A call can give you the lead's actual situation in a minute or two, while a text alone gives you much less to work with. The message that follows a missed call should end with an easy question.

**How many times should I follow up before I give up?**
Follow up until the lead books, buys, sells with someone else, or asks you to stop, and set your pace by the lead's timeline. A lead planning to move within about 60 days needs weekly contact, while a lead a year away needs a quarterly touch.
