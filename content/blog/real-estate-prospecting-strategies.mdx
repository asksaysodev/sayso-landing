Prospecting in real estate is the work of finding and contacting people who might hire you to buy or sell a home, before they have chosen an agent. The strategies that work fall into five groups: relationships (your sphere, past clients and referrals), neighborhoods (farming, circle prospecting, door knocking and open houses), lists of homeowners in a specific situation (expired listings, FSBOs and similar), online channels, and agent-to-agent tools such as reverse prospecting. Most agents do best with two or three of them done consistently rather than all of them done occasionally.

This guide explains what each strategy is, who it reaches and where it tends to fall short, then shows how to pick a mix, how to handle the calling part of prospecting, and where to go next for scripts, a weekly plan and the numbers.

## What Does Prospecting Mean in Real Estate?

The National Association of REALTORS describes [prospecting](https://www.nar.realtor/farming-prospecting) as seeking out potential clients through methods such as calling, networking, online marketing and social media. Farming, in the same page's words, means focusing on a specific geographic area "where an agent consistently markets themselves to become the go-to real estate expert in that community."

In practice, the difference is where the effort goes. Prospecting starts with a person or a list: you decide who you want to reach and you reach out. Farming starts with a place and builds familiarity over months. Both feed the same pipeline, and a prospect is simply someone who might become a client but has not hired you yet. If you want the longer definition and the one prospecting method that starts inside the MLS, read [what reverse prospecting means in real estate](/blog/reverse-prospecting-real-estate/).

## Real Estate Prospecting Strategies at a Glance

Every strategy below can produce buyer and seller conversations, although some lean one way. Use the table to see where each one fits before reading the details.

| Strategy | Who you reach | Works well when | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sphere and past clients | People who already know you | You have a database and keep in touch | Slow to build if you are new |
| Referral requests | Friends, neighbors and relatives of past clients | You do good work and ask for it | Depends on the people you already served |
| Farming | Homeowners in one area | You want to be known in a specific neighborhood | Takes months before results show |
| Circle prospecting | Neighbors of a sale or listing | You have a fresh trigger to mention | Needs current sales data and steady follow-up |
| Door knocking | Homeowners at the door | You like face-to-face and can cover a small area | Time-intensive and weather-dependent |
| Open houses | Visitors and neighbors | You have listings or can borrow a house | Few listings means few open houses |
| Expired, FSBO and other lists | Owners in a specific situation | You are comfortable calling | Many agents call the same people |
| Online and social | Anyone searching or following you | You like creating content | Slow, and attention is hard to hold |
| Reverse prospecting | Buyer's agents with matching clients | You have active listings in your MLS | Only works with listings you hold |

## Relationship Strategies: Sphere, Past Clients and Referrals

Your [sphere of influence](/glossary/sphere-of-influence/) is the group of people who know you and would consider hiring you or referring you. It includes family, friends, neighbors, former clients and people you meet through your kids' school, gym or church.

This group deserves a spot in nearly every mix because of how people actually pick an agent. According to the National Association of REALTORS' 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, [66% of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with before](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers) (reported by Virginia REALTORS, for transactions between July 2024 and June 2025). Relationships do not replace outbound prospecting, but they explain why the people who already know you are the first group to contact.

A simple routine works better than an elaborate one:

- Call each person in your database a few times a year with a real reason, such as a market update, a home anniversary or a question about how their year is going.
- Ask for referrals directly, using words that feel natural to you. The [referral scripts post](/blog/real-estate-referral-scripts/) has wording for past clients and your sphere.
- Record what you learn after each conversation (a possible move, a new job, a family change) so the next call picks up where the last one ended.

## Neighborhood Strategies: Farming, Circle Prospecting, Door Knocking and Open Houses

Neighborhood strategies give you a built-in reason to reach out, because something is happening on their street.

**Farming.** You pick an area, then keep showing up in it through mail, calls, events and online content. The goal is that when someone in the area thinks about moving, your name is the first one they think of. Pick an area small enough that you can contact it consistently, since sporadic farming gets forgotten.

**Circle prospecting.** When a home nearby lists or sells, you contact the surrounding homeowners with that news. This works because the call has a reason that is about them, not about you. The [circle prospecting glossary entry](/glossary/circle-prospecting/) explains the method, and the [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/) cover what to say. The [just listed and just sold scripts](/blog/just-listed-just-sold-scripts/) cover the same trigger events in more detail.

**Door knocking.** [Door knocking](/glossary/door-knocking/) puts you face to face with homeowners, which makes it easy to build trust and hard to scale. It suits agents who want the practice at conversation and have a small, defined area.

**Open houses.** Open houses bring buyers, neighbors and curious homeowners to you. The open house itself is only half the work. The other half is the follow-up, which the [open house scripts](/blog/open-house-scripts/) walk through step by step.

## List-Based Strategies: Homeowners in a Specific Situation

Some prospecting starts with a list of homeowners whose situation suggests they may need an agent now. These lists are only as useful as the conversation that follows, because the person on the other end is often frustrated, stressed or already hearing from other agents.

- **Expired listings:** owners whose listing ended without a sale. See the [expired listing scripts](/blog/expired-listing-scripts/) and the [expired listing glossary entry](/glossary/expired-listing/). - **For sale by owner (FSBO):** owners selling without an agent. See the [FSBO scripts](/blog/fsbo-scripts/).
- **Withdrawn listings:** owners who took the home off the market. See the [withdrawn listing scripts](/blog/withdrawn-listing-scripts/).
- **Absentee owners, pre-foreclosure, probate and divorce:** owners whose circumstances call for more care in the conversation. See the [absentee owner](/blog/absentee-owner-script/), [pre-foreclosure](/blog/pre-foreclosure-script/), [probate](/blog/probate-real-estate-script/) and [divorce listing](/blog/divorce-listing-script/) scripts.

## Online and Content Prospecting

Social media, email newsletters, video and a website all help people find you or remember you. They tend to work best as support for the other strategies rather than as a stand-alone plan, since attention is slow to build and the people you reach are not always local or ready to move.

Where online prospecting does produce direct conversations is with inbound leads: people who fill out a form or message you. Those leads are worth a fast, well-prepared call, which the [phone script for leads](/blog/real-estate-phone-script-for-leads/) and the [ISA scripts](/blog/real-estate-isa-scripts/) cover.

## Reverse Prospecting: Start With the Match

Reverse prospecting flips the usual order. Instead of finding a buyer and then looking for a home, your MLS compares your active listing to the saved searches other agents have set up for their clients, and gives you a list of buyer's agents who may have a client for it. Then you contact those agents. It is the one strategy on this list that only works with a listing you already hold, and the [reverse prospecting guide](/blog/reverse-prospecting-real-estate/) shows how it works and what to say.

## How to Make Prospecting Calls Work

Calling is the part of prospecting many agents find hardest, even though it lets you reach a lot of people quickly. Whether you call your sphere, a farm area or a purchased list, four habits make the biggest difference.

1. **Lead with a reason for the call.** A specific reason, such as a sale on their street or a listing that expired, gives the person a reason to stay on the line.
2. **Ask one question and listen.** Questions about timing and plans lead to better conversations than a pitch for your services. The guide on [how to start a real estate call](/blog/how-to-start-a-real-estate-call/) covers the first ten seconds.
3. **End with a next step.** Even a no-appointment conversation should end with permission to follow up, a send-over of market data or a date to speak again.
4. **Follow up on a schedule.** Many people are not ready on the first call. The [follow-up scripts for cold leads](/blog/follow-up-scripts-cold-leads/) and the guide to [how often to follow up](/blog/how-often-follow-up-real-estate/) show what to say and when.

## How to Choose Your Prospecting Mix

You do not need every strategy. You need the two or three that match your situation, your personality and the hours you can protect each week. Use these starting points.

| Your situation | A reasonable mix to start with |
|---|---|
| New agent with a small database | Calling a targeted list, circle prospecting and weekly outreach to everyone you know |
| Agent with a large past-client list | Regular contact with past clients and sphere, plus one neighborhood or list activity |
| Agent with active listings | Open houses, circle prospecting around your listings and reverse prospecting |
| Team leader or ISA | A single list-based or inbound-lead calling routine, with follow-up tracked in the CRM |
| Limited time each week | Protected calling time for one list, plus a standing monthly sphere check-in |

After you choose, put the mix on a calendar. ## Common Prospecting Mistakes

- **Spreading across too many channels.** Five strategies done once a month produce less than two done every week.
- **Stopping after the first call.** A lot of the value comes from the follow-up, so schedule it before you hang up.
- **Skipping the tracking.** Without counts for attempts, conversations and appointments, you cannot tell which strategy deserves more time.
- **Assuming one lead type.** A neighborhood call, an expired-listing call and a buyer inquiry all call for different questions, so match the opener to who is on the line.
- **Waiting until you feel ready.** Confidence on calls comes from making them. The guides on [overcoming call reluctance](/blog/how-to-overcome-call-reluctance/) and [practicing scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) help with that.

## FAQ

**How do you do prospecting in real estate?**
Choose two or three strategies that match your situation, block time on your calendar for them each week and track your attempts, conversations and appointments. Review the numbers weekly and spend more time on the strategy that produces conversations.

**What is the difference between farming and prospecting?**
Farming means marketing consistently to a specific geographic area so that you become the known agent there. Prospecting means actively seeking out potential clients by calling, networking, online outreach and similar methods. Many agents do both.

**How much time should I spend prospecting each day?**
Published advice varies. Indeed's career guide suggests [spending 90 minutes every workday](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-prospect-in-real-estate) reaching out to potential clients, and Buildout's tips article suggests [60 to 90 minutes a day](https://www.buildout.com/blog-posts/real-estate-prospecting-tips). Your own number depends on your goal, which the prospecting calculator can help you work out.

**Do prospecting calls still work for real estate agents?**
They work as one channel among several, and results depend on the list, the opening line and the follow-up. Relationships still lead to most seller hires, so calling pairs well with regular contact with the people who already know you.
