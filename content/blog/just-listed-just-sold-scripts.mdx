A just sold script in real estate tells nearby homeowners that a home on their street has closed and asks one question about their own plans. A just listed script tells them a home nearby is for sale, usually with an invitation to an open house or a question about whether they know anyone who wants to move in. Both are short calls that give the homeowner a reason to talk to you without asking for their listing, and both work for homeowners who may be thinking about buying, selling or both.

This post gives you one script for each, the voicemail version, a door knock version for just listed homes, the follow-up questions to ask once someone engages, and a short list of what to check before you dial. If you want the broader set of neighborhood triggers, including market milestones, start with our [circle prospecting scripts](/blog/circle-prospecting-scripts/). The scripts here take a different path through the call, so the two posts work well together.

## Just Listed or Just Sold: Which Script to Use

The trigger decides the reason for your call, and the reason decides what you ask.

| Trigger | What you say | Goal of the call | Next step if they engage |
|---|---|---|---|
| A home nearby just listed | A home on your street is for sale, and there is an open house | Get them to the open house or learn their plans | Invite them to the open house, ask who they know who might move in |
| A home nearby just sold | A home on your street closed | Start a conversation about their own plans | Offer a quick look at what their home might be worth |
| You are holding an open house | Same as just listed | Meet neighbors in person | Invite them in |

Neither script asks the homeowner to list. The goal is a conversation that tells you whether they have a reason to move, and whether you should check in again in a week or in six months.

## Before You Dial: Five Things to Check

A neighborhood call lives or dies on being specific and accurate. Check these before you start a calling block.

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The exact address and status | Say "just listed" only when it is on the market, and "just sold" only when it has closed. A pending listing is neither. |
| What you can say about the sale | Rules on sharing sold prices and details vary by MLS and brokerage. Ask your broker what you may quote on a call or in a voicemail. |
| The radius | Call homeowners the event could realistically affect, such as the same street or the nearby blocks. A sale three miles away is not news to them. |
| Your Do Not Call list | Scrub the numbers first. See our [Do Not Call rules for real estate agents](/blog/do-not-call-rules-real-estate-agents/) for what applies to your calls. |
| Open house details, if any | Day, start and end time and the address, written where you can see them. |

## The Just Listed Script

This script is built around the open house. It tells the neighbor why you are calling, gives them a reason to care about the listing even if they are not moving, and asks one question about their own plans.

<ScriptExample label="Just listed: the neighbor call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. We are helping your neighbor at [Address] sell their home and we are hosting an open house this [Day] from [Time] to [Time]. Did you know they were moving?

We are expecting a good amount of traffic, and the home will only work out for one family. If more than one buyer wants it, would you ever consider selling your own home at the right price?
</ScriptExample>

What they say next decides the direction of the call.

- **If they say** "No, we are staying": say "Fair enough. Out of curiosity, how long have you owned your home?" Then ask "Have you looked at what your equity might be?" and offer to send a quick value snapshot.
- **If they say** "It depends on the price": say "That makes sense. What would the number need to look like, and where would you go if you did sell?" Then offer to come by for 10 to 15 minutes to see the home.
- **If they say** "Why are you calling me?": say "You live close by, so I wanted you to hear about it from a neighbor's agent before the sign and the traffic show up. There is also a chance someone you know wants to live on this street. Would you like the open house details?"
- **If they say** "I might come by": say "Great. Do you know anyone who has talked about moving to the area? I will text you my number so you can pass it along."

<CalloutBox type="warning">
The "more than one buyer" line should reflect the real situation. Use it only when the listing is drawing interest or you expect it to. If it is not, use the question about who they know who might want to move to the street.
</CalloutBox>

### Just Listed Voicemail

<ScriptExample label="Voicemail: just listed">
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. We are hosting an open house at [Address], just down the street from you, this [Day] from [Time] to [Time]. I wanted to let you know in case you or someone you know is thinking about moving into the neighborhood. My number is [Number].
</ScriptExample>

### Just Listed Door Knock

If you are hosting the open house, a short visit to the neighbors adds a face to the call. This version also asks for a referral and permission to stay in touch.

<ScriptExample label="Door knock: neighbor of a new listing">
Hi, I'm [Your Name] with [Brokerage], and I'm helping the sellers at [Address]. Did you know their home was for sale? We promised the sellers we would let the neighborhood know ourselves. I'm hosting a private viewing for neighbors on [Day] from [Time] to [Time] if you would like to see it.

By the way, who do you know who might want to move into the area? Can I text you my card so you can pass it along when you think of them?

And I'm curious, what are your plans for your own home over the next two or three years? I send a neighborhood report once or twice a month with home values and local news. Would that be useful? What is your email?
</ScriptExample>

## The Just Sold Script for Real Estate Agents

This version leads with a reason to listen, holds back the numbers until the homeowner asks, and ends with a question about their plans. A homeowner who asks "how much did it sell for?" is already interested.

<ScriptExample label="Just sold: the neighbor call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'm calling because [Address] on your street just closed, and I wanted you to hear it from someone who works the area. I'm not calling to ask for a listing. When a home on your street sells, do you ever wonder what that does for the value of your own?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "How much did it sell for?": say "It closed at [Price] after [X] days, and the details matter, since condition and upgrades change the number. Would it help if I took a look at yours and told you where it would land?" Share only what your broker says you may.
- **If they say** "Yes, I do wonder": say "Most owners do. Have you looked at what your equity might be?" Then offer a quick value breakdown and ask for the best email or text number to send it.
- **If they say** "We're not moving": say "Totally understand, most folks are not. Out of curiosity, how long have you owned the home?" If they say "a long time", follow with "A lot of long-time owners are surprised by their equity. Would it be okay if I sent you a quick snapshot?"
- **If they say** "Why are you calling me?": say "Fair question. Homes near yours just sold, and I like to keep the neighbors informed. If it is not helpful, I will not call again. Would a short market update now and then be useful?"
- **If they say** "Take me off your list": say "Of course, I'll take you off right now." Then log it the same day and add the number to your internal do-not-call list.

Text it a minute later:

## Follow-Up Questions Once They Engage

A homeowner who answers the first question is willing to talk for a few more minutes. Use these questions to find out whether they are a seller, a buyer, or both, without asking for a commitment.

| Question | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| "How long have you owned the home?" | How much equity they may have, and how settled they feel |
| "Have you ever looked at what your equity might be?" | Whether they have thought about selling at all |
| "If you did sell, where would you go?" | A move already in mind, and whether they would also be buying |
| "What would the number need to look like for you to consider it?" | Their price expectation |
| "What would you change about your home if you could?" | Motivation to upsize, downsize or relocate |
| "Do you know anyone who is looking to move to the neighborhood?" | A referral, and a reason for them to stay in touch |

Stop asking when the conversation stops moving. If the homeowner answers two or three questions and gives you a reason, a timeline or a number, book the next step: a short visit to the home or a value breakdown you will deliver in the next few days. If they answer none of them, log the call, ask permission to send a periodic update, and move on to the next number. ## Mistakes That End the Call Early

**Asking for the listing.** The call exists to start a conversation, and an ask in the first sentence ends it.

**Calling too wide.** If the sale is not close enough to matter, the homeowner hears a sales call and not neighborhood news.

**Saying something you have not checked.** A wrong address, price or open house time costs you more trust than a short call earns.

**Quoting sale details your broker has not cleared.** Know what you can say before you dial.

**Stopping after one attempt.** Most neighbors are not ready to move today. The agent who sends a useful update every few weeks is the one they call when they are.
