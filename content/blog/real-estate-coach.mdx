A real estate coach is a paid professional who meets with you on a regular schedule, reviews your numbers, sets targets with you and holds you to them, and many coaches also teach scripts and systems that worked for other agents. You need one when your results have stalled for a reason you cannot see from the inside. If you have not yet built a steady prospecting habit, you can usually get further first with brokerage training and a mentor.

This guide explains what a coach does in a typical month, how a coach differs from a mentor, brokerage training and live call coaching, and how to decide whether you need one. It also covers what new agents should do first, how to find a mentor in real estate, and a scorecard you can use to choose a coach. For prices and a program-by-program comparison, see our guide to [real estate coaching programs](/blog/real-estate-coaching-programs/).

## What Does a Real Estate Coach Do?

A coach works on your business and your habits rather than doing the work for you. Over a month, most coaching relationships include some mix of the following:

- **A look at your numbers.** Calls made, conversations held, appointments set, clients signed and where the drop-off is largest.
- **A target for the next period.** One or two numbers you will move, written down.
- **A plan.** Scripts, a weekly schedule, a lead source to add or drop, or a system for follow-up.
- **Practice on hard conversations.** Some coaches role-play objections with you or review recorded calls. Ask whether yours does, because programs differ.
- **Accountability.** Someone expects you to report next time, which is the part many agents find hardest to create for themselves.

Sessions vary by program, but a typical one follows a pattern like this:

1. You report last period's numbers against the target.
2. You and the coach pick the one place the numbers slipped.
3. The coach offers a fix: a script, a change of schedule, a role play, or a question to ask your prospects.
4. You agree on what you will do before the next session and how you will measure it.

A coach does not make calls for you, and a coach does not guarantee that a lead will turn into a client. The value comes from the structure and feedback, so the benefit depends on what you do between sessions.

## Do You Need a Real Estate Coach?

The honest answer depends on where you are. This table matches common situations to the help that usually fits first.

| Your situation | What usually helps first |
|---|---|
| New agent with no daily routine yet | Brokerage training and a mentor. Set a daily calls target with the [prospecting calculator](/tools/prospecting-calculator/), build the habit, then add a coach once you have numbers to coach. |
| Steady activity, but income has been flat for a year or more | A business or prospecting coach who can look at your numbers and find the leak. |
| You know the plan but do not follow it | Accountability: group coaching, a coach with weekly check-ins, or an accountability partner at your brokerage. |
| Specific moments on calls go wrong (an objection, the appointment ask) | Role play with a coach, call review, or live call coaching. See the section on where live call coaching fits below. |
| Your referrals have dried up | Relationship coaching built around your [sphere of influence](/glossary/sphere-of-influence/). |
| You lead a team | A leadership or management track, plus a routine for [coaching agents on their calls](/blog/how-to-coach-real-estate-agents-on-calls/). |

If you can name the number you want to move, such as appointments per month, and you can afford the program out of your take-home pay, coaching is worth considering. Our guide to [what real estate coaching costs](/blog/real-estate-coaching-cost/) shows how to compare a program's price with what you keep from a closing.

## Real Estate Coach vs Mentor vs Brokerage Training vs Live Call Coaching

Agents often use these words as if they mean the same thing. They are four different kinds of help, and the right choice depends on what you need.

| Option | What it is | How it is paid for | Best for | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coach** | A paid professional with a structured program and scheduled sessions | You pay the program (published prices range from a $799 course to several thousand dollars a month) | Accountability, a plan, breaking through a plateau | Quality depends on the individual coach, and most sessions happen away from your live calls |
| **Mentor** | An experienced agent who advises you informally | Often informal; one mentoring guide notes that group mentorships may be free or charge membership fees | Judgment about your local market, career decisions, introductions | Availability, and advice reflects one person's path |
| **Brokerage training** | Classes or coaching offered by your brokerage | Varies; Keller Williams' Onward BOLD, for example, is $799 for six one-day in-person classes | Learning the basics and the company's systems | Tied to that company's way of working |
| **Team leader or sales manager** | The person who coaches you when you work on a team | Part of your team arrangement | Day-to-day feedback on calls and appointments | Limited by the leader's time |
| **Live call coaching** | Software that shows suggested responses on screen during a call | Subscription | Handling an objection in the moment | Does not set goals or hold you accountable |

Mentors and coaches overlap, and many articles use the words interchangeably. A practical way to separate them is this: a mentor shares what worked for them over a career, usually without a fixed program, while a coach follows a structure, tracks your numbers and expects you to report.

## Where Live Call Coaching Fits

A coach or mentor helps before and after your calls, and during the call you are on your own, which is where an unexpected objection tends to throw agents off. Some agents add live call coaching alongside whichever coach they choose, using a tool like [Sayso](/why-sayso/) that shows a suggested response or next question on screen while the prospect is talking. It supports a coach rather than replacing one, since it does not review your numbers or hold you to a target.

## Real Estate Coaching for New Agents

In your first year, the biggest gains usually come from doing the basics every day, and a coach helps most once you have numbers to look at. A sensible order looks like this:

1. **Use what your brokerage already offers.** Some brokerages include training or coaching for their agents, so ask what is included before you buy anything. Keller Williams, for example, lists seven coaching formats on its MAPS page, including Productivity Coaching for new agents seeking quick production, and its Onward BOLD program is open to prospective agents as well as licensed ones.
2. **Find a mentor.** The next section explains how.
3. **Set a daily activity target and keep it.** The [prospecting calculator](/tools/prospecting-calculator/) works backward from your income goal to the calls and conversations you need each day.
4. **Practice your scripts.** Our guides to [practicing real estate scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) and [scripts for new agents](/blog/real-estate-scripts-for-new-agents/) give you material to rehearse, and our [role play guide](/blog/real-estate-role-play/) shows how to practice with a partner.
5. **Add a coach when you have three months of numbers.** A coach can only improve what you can show them.

Our page for [new agents](/for/new-agents/) is a good next stop if you want to feel more confident on calls from the start.

## How to Find a Mentor in Real Estate

The advice that mentor guides agree on is to start close to home and build the relationship gradually. A workable approach:

1. **Start at your brokerage.** Ask whether it has a formal mentor program, and if not, which experienced agents in your office are willing to talk.
2. **Use associations and local events.** Local and national real estate associations, specialty groups and networking events put you in rooms with agents who have the experience you want.
3. **Match on fit.** Look for someone who works your kind of market and lead sources and whose style you like. A great agent in a different niche may give advice that does not apply to you.
4. **Ask for something small first.** A 20-minute conversation about one specific problem is easier to say yes to than "will you be my mentor?"
5. **Agree on expectations once it clicks.** Settle how often you will talk, for how long, and what you want to achieve, such as a six-month window with a review at the end.
6. **Give something back.** Share what you learned, bring a useful lead or an introduction, and thank them.

<ScriptExample label="Message to a Possible Mentor">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I am new at [Brokerage] and I noticed that you [specific thing, such as handle a lot of expired listings or work with first-time buyers in the same neighborhoods I do]. I am working on [one specific skill, such as asking for the appointment] and would love 20 minutes to ask how you handle it. I will bring my questions so I do not waste your time. Would sometime next week work?
</ScriptExample>

**How much should you pay a mentor?** Often nothing. One mentoring guide notes that group mentorships may be free or may require membership fees. If someone asks you for a large payment in exchange for "mentoring," treat it as a coaching purchase and put it through the same questions below, including the cancellation terms.

## How to Choose a Real Estate Coach

Most "how to choose" advice stops at "know your goals." Work through these steps in order.

1. **Name the problem in one sentence, with a number.** "I make about the same number of calls each week, but only a few turn into appointments" is a problem a coach can work on. "I want to do better" is not.
2. **Match the type of coaching to the problem.** Prospecting and script coaching, referral and relationship coaching, business and team-building coaching, and brokerage programs each serve different problems. Our [coaching programs guide](/blog/real-estate-coaching-programs/) describes the five types and the programs in each.
3. **Find out who will actually coach you and how you are matched.** In a large program, your coach is one of many. Tom Ferry's plans page, for example, describes a "Right Coach Guarantee" under which the company says it will match you with the coach who fits your goals and "make it right" if it is not a fit, though the page does not state a time limit. Tim and Julie Harris say that for Premier 1:1 clients complete a detailed profile and the team reviews it by hand before matching them with a certified coach. Ask any program how matching works.
4. **Use the introductory step, and ask your questions.** Many programs offer one: Tom Ferry offers a free 30-minute call with a coaching specialist, the Mike Ferry Organization offers a free coaching evaluation, and Tim and Julie Harris advertise a seven-day, $1 trial of Premier coaching that is not charged the $997 if you cancel before day 8. These calls are also sales calls, so arrive with your problem statement and the scorecard below.
5. **Read the terms before the price.** Some programs are month to month, some renew annually, and some ask for a starting commitment. Floyd Wickman's site, for example, lists weekly one-hour coaching with a six-month commitment. Ask for the cancellation and refund terms in writing.
6. **Compare the cost with your take-home pay.** Work out how many extra closings the program needs to pay for itself. Our [coaching cost guide](/blog/real-estate-coaching-cost/) has a table for that.

### A Question Scorecard to Copy

Ask every program the same questions and write the answers down, so the one with the best pitch does not win by default.

| Question | What a good answer sounds like | Your notes |
|---|---|---|
| Who will coach me, and how am I matched? | A named coach or a clear matching process, and a way to change coaches | |
| What will we work on in the first 90 days? | A specific plan tied to your problem statement | |
| How often and how long are sessions? | A schedule you can keep, in writing | |
| Do you review my real calls or role-play with me? | A direct yes or no, and how it works | |
| What is the minimum term, and how do I cancel? | Plain terms in writing, with no surprises | |
| What is included, and what costs extra? | A full list (seats, events, tools, shipping, tax) | |
| Can I speak with an agent at my level who has been in the program for a year? | Yes, without a long delay | |

## Warning Signs Before You Sign

- The seller promises a specific income or number of closings.
- You are pressured to sign on the first call, or the offer expires "today."
- Nobody can tell you who your coach will be.
- The cancellation terms are vague or only available after you pay.
- The program offers one script for every lead type, when your buyer and seller leads need different conversations.
- You cannot speak with a current member at your production level.

## A 90-Day Coaching Test

Treat the first three months as a test with a clear finish line, whichever coach or mentor you choose.

<CalloutBox type="key-takeaway">
Before the first session, write down your numbers for the last four weeks and one target to move. At day 30, 60 and 90, compare the same numbers and ask two questions: did the number I chose move, and can I name one thing the coaching changed in how I work? If not, change the approach or end the arrangement while the cost is still small.
</CalloutBox>

## FAQ

**How much does a real estate coach cost?**
Among the programs that publish prices on their own websites (checked October 2026), prices range from $799 for a six-week course and $997 a year for group coaching to $549 to $1,299 a month for one-on-one coaching, and $2,999 a month or more for team plans. Several well-known programs do not publish a price. See our [cost guide](/blog/real-estate-coaching-cost/) for the full table.

**Is a real estate coach worth it?**
It is worth it when the program fits your problem and you act on it between sessions. Compare the total cost with what you keep from a closing after your split, and only sign up if you expect the coaching to add clearly more than that.

**Can a coach guarantee results?**
The guarantees on the pages we read cover coach fit or a short trial, not income. Treat any promise of a specific income as a warning sign.

**How long should I stay with a coach?**
Terms range from month to month to annual or multi-month commitments, so check before you sign. A 90-day test with written targets tells you whether to continue.
