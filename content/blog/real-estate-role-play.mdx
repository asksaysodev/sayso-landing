Real estate role play works best when one agent plays the agent, a partner plays a prospect with a specific situation and a few set replies, and both of you score the round against the same checklist afterward. Without the set replies and the checklist, role play turns into a friendly chat that feels productive and changes nothing on your real calls.

This post gives you six scenarios you can use today (three with buyer leads and three with sellers), the exact lines the "prospect" should say, the agent script to practice with branches for what comes next, and a scorecard that tells you whether the round was good. Most of what ranks for this search is video, and video is great for tone but cannot hand you the words. So the lines are written out here, and you can record your own audio to check the tone.

## How a Real Estate Role Play Session Works

A useful session takes about 20 minutes and needs two people. If you do not have a partner, the questions in the FAQ below cover practicing alone or with an AI voice tool, and our guide to [how to practice real estate scripts](/blog/how-to-practice-real-estate-scripts/) covers the solo steps that come before partner work.

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read the card | 2 minutes | The partner reads the prospect card in the scenario. The agent only knows the lead source and what the call is for. |
| Play the call | 5 minutes | The agent uses the script as a starting point. The partner gives the prospect lines in order and stays in character. |
| Score it | 3 minutes | Both people fill in the scorecard below, then compare. |
| Swap and repeat | 8 minutes | Switch roles, or repeat the same scenario with the partner making it harder. |
| Pick one line | 2 minutes | Write down the one line you will use on your next real call. |

The prospect card is what makes this different from a casual conversation. Every card has the same four parts, so you can write your own for any lead type.

| Card part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Situation | Who the prospect is and what they did to become a lead |
| Hidden reason | The real motivation and timeline the agent has to uncover with questions |
| Replies, in order | Three things the prospect says, starting with the easiest and ending with the hardest |
| Passing result | What the agent must have by the end of the call |

The hidden reason matters most. The agent should have to ask for it, and the partner should only give it up after a second or third question. That mirrors real calls, where people start with the simple answer ("we want something bigger") and only reach the real reason ("the baby is due in four months and the lease is up") when you keep asking.

## The Scorecard: How to Rate Each Round

Score each line from 0 (missed it), 1 (partly), to 2 (clearly did it). The standards come from the way the scripts below are built: the call exists to lock in a next step, ideally today or tomorrow, and a lead's first answer is rarely the real reason.

| # | What to check | 0, 1 or 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The opening gave a clear reason for the call within two sentences | |
| 2 | The agent asked "why" or "tell me more" at least twice to get past the first answer | |
| 3 | The agent repeated back something the prospect said before moving on | |
| 4 | The agent answered pushback without arguing, pitching or sending a link instead | |
| 5 | The agent asked for a specific time with two options (later today or tomorrow) | |
| 6 | The call ended with a confirmed next step: time, who attends, and how to reach each other | |

A total of 10 to 12 means the scenario is ready for live calls. A score of 7 to 9 means repeat the same scenario once more before moving on. Anything under 7 usually means the opening or the first question is shaky, so go back to reading it aloud and recording it.

<CalloutBox type="tip">
Score the call, not the person. If your partner says "that was good" and cannot say which line moved the conversation, the round did not teach you anything. Make them point to the line.
</CalloutBox>

## Six Real Estate Role Play Scenarios With Scripts

Each scenario has an agent script, the prospect card, and the branches. The scripts follow standard call flows for each lead type; change the wording until it sounds like you. For the thinking behind these calls, see our guide on [how to talk to real estate leads](/blog/how-to-talk-to-real-estate-leads/).

### Scenario 1: The Online Lead Who Is "Just Looking" (Buyer)

**Card.** The prospect registered on a home search site two weeks ago. Hidden reason: their lease ends in about four months and they have not told anyone. Replies in order: "I'm just looking." / "Maybe in six months or so." / "Can you just send me what's new?"

**Passing result:** the agent learns the lease deadline and books a short call or meeting.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: online lead, first call">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. You were looking at homes in [Area] on our site, and I did not want you to miss anything that fits. Are you still looking in that area?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I'm just looking": say "Totally fair, most people start there. What caught your eye, the price, the area, or something specific?"
- **If they say** "Maybe in six months": say "That helps. What's making you think about moving then?" Then ask "why does that timing matter?" to reach the real reason.
- **If they say** "Just send me what's new": say "Happy to. Tell me what's got you thinking about a move right now, so what I send actually fits." Sending homes before you understand the move weakens your position, so the question comes first.
- **To finish:** "Let's take 10 minutes to get you set up so you only see homes that fit. Would later today or tomorrow be easier?"

For the objection behind this one, see our page on responding to [I'm just looking](/objections/just-looking/) and the one on [prospects who only want listings sent](/objections/just-send-listings/).

### Scenario 2: The Buyer at the End of a Showing (Buyer)

**Card.** A couple just toured a home. Hidden reason: they are frustrated that everything good goes quickly, and they have not spoken to a lender. Replies in order: "It's nice, but not the one." / "Honestly it's been frustrating, nothing is in our budget." / "Just send us the info and we'll think about it."

**Passing result:** the agent gets the fit rating, finds the frustration, and books a second meeting.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: after the showing">
So where does this land for you? If 10 is exactly what you are looking for and 1 is way off, where would you put it?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** a number below 9: say "Got it, so we're close but not there. What would it need to have to feel like a 10?" Then ask "What's been the most frustrating part of the search so far?"
- **If the frustration is limited inventory:** say "That makes sense. Some homes are coming up that have not hit the big sites yet. If I could show you what is coming soon and how I find it, would that help?"
- **If the frustration is affordability or rates:** say "A lot of buyers feel that. It usually helps to sit down and map out the options so you are not guessing. If we took 20 to 30 minutes and laid it out, would that make things easier?"
- **If they say** "just send us the info": say "I'm happy to send info. It's usually much clearer when we go through it together. I have time tomorrow at 4 or Wednesday at 6, which works better?"
- **If they say** 9 or 10: ask "What's making it feel that strong?" and "If we moved on this, how soon would you want to be in?" Then ask whether they have talked to a lender and whether anyone else needs to see the home before they decide.

Our [buyer consultation questions](/blog/buyer-consultation-questions/) cover the meeting this call is trying to book.

### Scenario 3: The Buyer With a Lender Already (Buyer)

**Card.** The prospect has been pre-approved with a lender they found online. Hidden reason: they are not sure the rate is good but feel disloyal asking. Replies in order: "Yes, we have a lender." / "We're happy with them." / "I don't want to switch."

**Passing result:** the prospect agrees to a second opinion without feeling pushed.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: lender question">
Have you had the chance to speak with a lender yet, or is that something I can help with?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "Yes, we have one": say "That's great, I'm glad you took that step. I'm not saying you need to switch. A second opinion just gives you something to take back to your lender so they can match or improve it. Would you be open to a quick conversation with one of the lenders I trust, just to confirm you are getting the best terms?"
- **If they say** "We're happy with them": say "Then you have nothing to lose. If their terms are better, you will know for certain. If another lender can beat them, you keep that money."
- **If they have not spoken to anyone:** say "A common mistake is waiting until you find the perfect home to sort out financing. Getting it handled now means you can move quickly when the right one appears. Can I connect you with a lender I trust?"

Check your state and brokerage rules on lender referrals before you use this one live.

### Scenario 4: The Homeowner Who Requested a Home Value (Seller)

**Card.** The prospect requested a home value from your website last spring and forgot about it. Hidden reason: they are thinking about selling in a few months after a job change. Replies in order: "I was just curious." / "Maybe in the next few months." / "I don't even remember requesting that."

**Passing result:** the agent learns the reason and timeline and books a 20-minute visit to the home.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: home value request">
Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. It looks like you requested a home value on your property in [Month]. I was going through our records and wanted to make sure we did not miss you. Did you end up doing anything with the property, or are you still there?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "I was just curious": say "Totally fair. Most people who check their value are thinking about selling, thinking about refinancing, or checking whether they have built equity. Which one were you?"
- **If they say** "Maybe in a few months": say "That's helpful. What's driving that timeline?" Then add "If you are a few months out, this is actually a good time to look at it, because choices about condition and prep now can make a real difference later. Let's spend 20 minutes walking through it."
- **If they say** "I don't remember requesting that": say "No worries, it was a while back. You still own [Address], right? If someone offered you a strong price for it, would you even consider moving?" If yes, move into the seller questions in the next section. If no, ask "Have you checked what homes around you are selling for lately? A lot of owners are surprised."
- **To finish:** "Online estimates miss condition and upgrades. I'd swing by for 20 minutes, see the home, and give you a realistic range. Would Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning be easier?"

### Scenario 5: The Neighbor Who Gets a Call About a Buyer (Seller)

**Card.** A homeowner on a street where a client wants to buy. Hidden reason: they have thought about downsizing but never mentioned it. Replies in order: "We're not selling." / "What would someone even pay?" / "Why do you want to come inside?"

**Passing result:** the agent gets a second question answered (how long they have owned it or where they would go) and books a visit.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: I have a buyer">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I'll be quick, I have one question. I have a client actively looking to move into your neighborhood. They missed out on another home and are serious about moving. If the right opportunity came along for your place, is that something you would at least want to know about?
</ScriptExample>

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Only use the "I have a client" line when you have a real client or buyer looking in that neighborhood. If you do not, switch to a just sold or just listed opener from our [just listed and just sold scripts](/blog/just-listed-just-sold-scripts/).
</CalloutBox>

- **If they say** "No, we're not selling": say "Fair enough. Out of curiosity, how long have you owned it?" Then ask "Have you ever looked at what your equity might be?" and move toward a valuation visit.
- **If they say** "What would someone pay?": say "That depends on condition. Can you tell me about any upgrades you have done? Honestly I would need to see the home to give you a fair answer. Would you be okay if I came by for 20 minutes?"
- **If they say** "Where would we even go?": say "What would that number need to look like, and where would you go if you did?" That question tells you whether this is a real seller.

### Scenario 6: The Seller Who Is Interviewing Other Agents (Seller)

**Card.** A homeowner booked a listing appointment for Thursday and you are making the confirmation call. Hidden reason: they have already spoken to one agent who suggested a higher price. Replies in order: "We're talking to a few agents." / "Can you just tell me what it's worth?" / "What will you charge?"

**Passing result:** the agent finds out how they will choose, learns their price expectation, and confirms the appointment with all decision makers.

<ScriptExample label="Agent script: confirming a listing appointment">
Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I wanted to confirm that [Day] at [Time] still works for our appointment. Before I come out, I have a few quick questions so I am fully prepared. Do you have a few minutes?
</ScriptExample>

- **If they say** "We're talking to a few agents": say "That's smart. What are the most important things you are looking for in the agent you choose?" Then ask "How will you know when you have found the right one?"
- **If they say** "Just tell me what it's worth": say "I'd rather see the home and the recent sales first so the number is real. Realistically, what are you hoping to list it for?" 
- **If they say** "What will you charge?": answer plainly and tie it to their net proceeds. The commission question has word-for-word responses in [how much is your commission](/objections/how-much-is-your-commission/).
- **To finish:** "Will everyone who is part of the decision be there when I arrive?"

The full list of confirmation call questions is in our [listing appointment questions](/blog/listing-appointment-questions/) post.

## How to Make a Role Play Round Harder

Once a scenario scores 10 or more, change one thing at a time so you are practicing adaptation and not memory.

- **Change the mood.** Have the prospect sound rushed, tired, or suspicious from the first sentence.
- **Add an interruption.** The partner cuts in with a question halfway through the opening.
- **Use silence.** After the agent's question, the prospect waits five seconds before answering. Most agents rush to fill silence, and this teaches you to wait.
- **Reverse the order of the replies.** Start with the hardest line.
- **Bring in a decision maker.** Midway through, the partner says "my husband wants to join" or "I need to ask my wife," and the agent has to adjust. The [spouse needs to decide](/objections/my-spouse-needs-to-decide/) page covers that moment.

## Mistakes That Make Role Play Useless

**The partner is too nice.** If every prospect says yes by the second reply, you are rehearsing a call that will never happen. Stay in character and make the agent earn the second answer.

**You only practice the opening.** Openings are easy because you control them. The branches after a pushback are where calls fall apart, so spend most of the round there.

**Nobody asks for the time.** The most common miss on the scorecard is item 5. Agents ask "would you like to meet?" instead of offering two specific times, which invites a "maybe."

**You score on feeling.** Use the scorecard every time, even when the round felt great.

**You never test it live.** A practice score only matters if it carries over to a real call. After a session, use the one line you picked on your next three calls and see what happens. ## FAQ

**How often should agents do real estate role play?**
Most agents do better with short, regular sessions than with one long one. Two or three sessions of about 20 minutes a week, each focused on a single scenario, is a realistic schedule. Pick the scenario that matches the lead type you are about to call.

**Can I do real estate role play by myself?**
Yes, in two ways. Read the prospect replies aloud and answer them, recording your side so you can listen to your tone, or use an AI voice tool and give it the prospect card as its instructions. Both help with the words. A human partner is better at interrupting and staying in character, so add one when you can.

**What real estate role play scenarios should new agents start with?**
Start with the lead types you will actually call this month. For most new agents that means an online buyer lead (scenario 1) and a home value request (scenario 4), because both are common and both have clear passing results.
