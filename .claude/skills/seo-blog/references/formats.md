# Post formats

Pick the format from the backlog `category` and what page one shows (`leading_format`, the ranking pages' structure). Match the format Google already ranks, then beat it on usefulness.

## Comparison guide (coaching programs only)

1. Direct answer: the short list and how to choose, in two or three sentences.
2. How we compared: criteria, the date prices were checked, and a one-line disclosure that Sayso sells a related product.
3. Comparison table with the same columns for every row: focus, format, published price, commitment or terms where published.
4. One H3 per option: what it is, who it suits, price (sourced, dated), one honest limitation.
5. How to choose, by situation.
6. Where live call coaching fits: the in-context mention, framed as a different kind of help, never ranked against the options.
7. Closing section; FAQ only if useful.

Describe every option from its own published material. No invented ratings.

## Cost guide (coaching cost)

The Cut Brush template.

1. Direct answer: the price range in one sentence, scoped to what was checked, then what drives it.
2. Price table by type or program, each row sourced and dated.
3. What changes the price (H3 per factor).
4. Terms and extra costs as published: minimum commitments, add-ons, fees.
5. A worked example of the annual cost against an agent's net commission.
6. Alternatives, where the in-context mention fits; state Sayso's own price plainly.
7. Closing section; FAQ only if useful.

## Explainer (reverse prospecting, real estate coach, types of prospecting tools)

1. Definition or direct answer first.
2. How agents use it, with an example.
3. What to say on the call it leads to, or how to choose.
4. Common mistakes or misconceptions.
5. In-context mention where a step happens on a call; closing section.

For "types of tools" posts, describe categories (data, dialers, CRMs, live coaching) and what each does, with no company named.

## How-to guide (use Mojo, listing appointment checklist, find expired listings, convert leads, coach agents, prospecting plan)

1. Direct answer: the steps in one or two sentences.
2. Numbered steps as H2s or H3s, each with what to do, what to say if it involves a call, and a common mistake.
3. A checklist, template or table the reader can copy.
4. In-context mention where a step happens on a call.
5. Closing section; FAQ only if useful.

## Script post (seller, buyer, just listed and just sold, withdrawn, new agents, negotiation, referral, voicemail, role play, conversation examples)

1. Direct answer: the core script in two or three sentences, or the opening line.
2. When to use these scripts and what the call is for (the next step, not the listing).
3. One `<ScriptExample>` per situation, each followed by "what they say next" branches: the common replies and what to say to each.
4. Voicemail versions where it fits.
5. Mistakes that kill the call.
6. In-context mention: practicing or keeping the script on screen during the call.
7. Closing section; FAQ only if useful.

Scripts come from `docs/content/ultimate-script-book.md` where it covers the situation. Keep them buyer and seller neutral unless the post is about one side.

## Refresh

Keep what already works. Change only what the brief's refresh hypothesis calls for: the opening answer, a missing section, a scannable list or table, outdated facts, the title or description when the query mix shows a mismatch, and links. Hubs must link to every post in their cluster.
